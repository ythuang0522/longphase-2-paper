\toolname integrates genetic variation and allele-specific methylation in a shared phasing graph, then withholds phase assignments predicted to be unreliable. Joint use of these evidence classes extends earlier approaches that use methylation or SVs to connect haplotype blocks in regions with few heterozygous SNVs\cite{akbari2021nanomethphase,fu2024methphaser,akbari2023parentoforigin,lin2022longphase,holt2024hiphase}. Whereas LongHap uses methylation to bridge blocks after sequence phasing\cite{pfennig2026longhap}, \toolname incorporates methylation and sequence evidence during haplotype construction. The evidence classes made distinct contributions: methylation increased contiguity at similar SNV switch error rates, whereas indels accounted for most of the increase in both contiguity and error. The corresponding trade-off in \whatshap suggests that this limitation is not specific to \toolname, although shared difficulties in representing or modelling indels may contribute. GNN correction reduced the additional error associated with indels, and improvements in indel calling could further improve this balance\cite{zheng2026accelerated,kolesnikov2024local}.

Selective withholding complements evidence integration by reducing reliance on uncertain phase assignments. \toolname reports phasing entropy and weighted vote totals as measures of the underlying evidence. These differ from the model-based confidence estimates provided by \hapcut\cite{edge2017hapcut2} and statistical phasers\cite{hofmeister2023shapeit5}: when votes are present, low entropy indicates their agreement, not necessarily strong evidence or a low probability of error. The GNN incorporates neighbouring graph and sequence features, allowing it to identify candidates that a high-entropy threshold alone would miss. However, its added value over entropy thresholds, support-count thresholds, random withholding or \hapcut's confidence pruning remains to be established at matched variant retention. Its phase-error score is neither calibrated nor included in the output. Because withholding variants and splitting blocks change which variant pairs can be assessed, reductions in switch error rate must be interpreted jointly with phased fraction and contiguity. Margin's lower error rate but shorter blocks at 10$\times$ illustrates this trade-off. Most SNVs unphased by the GNN lay outside regions with one-to-one assembly alignments, where the present analysis cannot establish whether their phase was correct.

Benchmark choice substantially altered the measured separation between methods. At 30--60$\times$, the highest and lowest switch error rates differed by 1.2--1.3-fold against GIAB v4.2.1 but by 4.3--4.8-fold against v5.0q, although more than 96\% of each benchmark's autosomal regions were shared. The original v5.0q evaluation focused on variant calling rather than phasing\cite{olson2026giabv5}; our results show that benchmark choice also changes the measured separation between phasing methods in this data set. The greater separation against v5.0q cannot yet be attributed to its additional tandem-repeat and homopolymer coverage, its complete phasing of heterozygous variants, or differences in representation and phase within shared regions. Nor does greater separation alone establish that all additional disagreements are phasing errors. Evaluation against both benchmarks, with separate analyses of shared and benchmark-specific regions and stratification by genomic context\cite{dwarshuis2024stratifications}, is needed to distinguish these contributions.

The current evaluation has several limits. All data derive from HG002, and the network was trained on nanopore runs from the same down-sampling series, using labels from the evaluation benchmark. Genome-wide metrics include the 17 chromosomes that contributed training windows and therefore do not constitute an independent test of generalization. Phasing performance should be reported separately on held-out chromosomes and evaluated in other individuals against an independent benchmark, with model weights and thresholds fixed in advance. The HiFi results without retraining are consistent with transfer between sequencing platforms within HG002, but do not establish generalization to other individuals. Subsampling variability was not estimated for nanopore coverages above 20$\times$ or for HiFi, and HiFi call-set handling remains to be confirmed. For indels, SVs and 5mC, we measured phased fractions rather than class-specific phasing accuracy; SV and 5mC sites had no supervised training labels. Methylation co-phasing was assessed only on autosomes of a male lymphoblastoid cell line\cite{fu2024methphaser}. The method also assumes a diploid genome with balanced allele fractions.

Compatibility with pipelines that use \longphase\cite{zheng2022clair3,zheng2026clairs,keskus2025severus,ont2025wfhuman} provides a route to test the downstream value of selective phase withholding. In haplotype-aware somatic calling\cite{zheng2026clairs,keskus2025severus}, reducing errors in germline phase could improve the assignment of tumour reads to haplotypes, but withholding phase may also reduce the information available to the caller. Varying the germline phaser while keeping the somatic caller fixed would quantify this trade-off. A calibrated per-variant confidence measure would further allow downstream methods to account explicitly for uncertainty in inherited phase.
