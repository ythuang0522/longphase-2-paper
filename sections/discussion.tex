\toolname rests on two ideas: that every class of heterozygous evidence a read carries belongs in one haplotype model, provided that each observation is weighted by how far it can be trusted, and that a phaser should withhold phase where its own output is unreliable. The first extends observations made for methylation\cite{akbari2021nanomethphase,fu2024methphaser,akbari2023parentoforigin} and for SVs\cite{lin2022longphase,holt2024hiphase} that SNVs alone leave blocks stranded where heterozygosity is low; unlike LongHap, which adds methylation after sequence phasing to bridge the blocks it leaves\cite{pfennig2026longhap}, \toolname weighs methylation and sequence evidence together as the phase is built. The evidence classes did not contribute alike. Methylation lengthened blocks at a similar switch error rate, whereas indels contributed most of the additional contiguity and most of the additional error, as they did for \whatshap; this is consistent with difficulties introduced by indel evidence, although limitations that both tools share in representing or modelling indels remain possible. Joint phasing is therefore a choice between contiguity and accuracy, which GNN correction narrows and more accurate indel calling\cite{zheng2026accelerated,kolesnikov2024local} would narrow further.

The second idea replaces a binary phased-or-unphased state with a graded one, but only partly. \toolname writes the phasing entropy and weighted vote totals of every phased variant, in the spirit of the posteriors of \hapcut\cite{edge2017hapcut2} and statistical phasers\cite{hofmeister2023shapeit5}, yet many removed benchmark heterozygotes had an entropy of exactly 0, unanimous but thin support that a high-entropy threshold alone would not flag. The network reads the evidence across a window rather than the likelihood of the phasing model, as \hapcut's pruning does, but whether it withholds better than simpler rules (entropy or support-count thresholds, random removal or \hapcut's confidence) at matched numbers of withheld variants has not been tested, and its phase-error score is neither calibrated nor reported. Because GNN correction only withholds phase, part of its gain follows by construction; Margin's lower switch error rate at 10$\times$, which coincided with three-fold shorter blocks, shows why error must be read alongside contiguity. What GNN correction withheld lay mostly outside the regions that the assembly-derived truth set can assess, where phase cannot currently be verified, and its benefit carried over to HiFi data from the same individual.

Several limitations bound these conclusions. All data come from one individual, HG002; the network was trained on its nanopore data, with labels from the evaluation benchmark and windows from the same down-sampling replicates, and the genome-wide rates include the 17 chromosomes that contributed training windows (Methods). The gains therefore require confirmation on the held-out chromosomes, on an independent individual and benchmark with frozen weights and thresholds, and above 20$\times$. The HiFi values come from one down-sampling per coverage, and the HiFi runs appear to share one call set. Benchmark choice changed the magnitude of disagreement and the separation between methods\cite{wagner2022giab,hansen2026q100}; the contributions of regional coverage, variant representation and phase discordance remain unresolved, and comparisons should report both truth sets and stratify errors by genomic context\cite{dwarshuis2024stratifications}. For indels, SVs and 5mC we report how many were phased, not whether they were phased correctly, and corrections to SV and 5mC phase are unsupervised. 5mC co-phasing was assessed only on the autosomes of a male lymphoblastoid cell line\cite{fu2024methphaser}, and the comparison with MethPhaser awaits verification. \toolname assumes a diploid genome with balanced allele fractions.

As a drop-in replacement in pipelines built on \longphase\cite{zheng2022clair3,zheng2026clairs,keskus2025severus,ont2025wfhuman}, \toolname adds per-variant phasing evidence without interface changes. The most direct use is in haplotype-aware somatic calling\cite{zheng2026clairs,keskus2025severus}, which accepts inherited phase without qualification: withholding phase at the loci where switch errors concentrate should keep spurious germline phase out of haplotagged tumour reads. Testing that, by varying only the germline phaser upstream of a fixed somatic caller, and providing a calibrated per-variant confidence that downstream tools can read, as they read genotype quality, are the next steps.
