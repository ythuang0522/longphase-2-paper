\toolname combines genetic variation and allele-specific methylation in a shared phasing graph with selective withholding of phase assignments predicted to be unreliable. This approach extends methods that use methylation or SVs to connect blocks in regions with few heterozygous SNVs\cite{akbari2021nanomethphase,fu2024methphaser,akbari2023parentoforigin,lin2022longphase,holt2024hiphase}. Compared with MethPhaser, which joins existing SNV phase blocks, \toolname produced shorter blocks with lower switch error rates and a smaller fraction of incorrectly phased SNVs (Fig.~\ref{fig:methylation}d,e and Supplementary Fig.~1e). This comparison also includes the effect of GNN correction, which was not applied to the MethPhaser input. \toolname also had lower SV phasing error rates than \whatshap. On HiFi data, \toolname and HiPhase had similar SV phasing error rates overall; \toolname had lower rates for SVs of at least 1~kb and HiPhase for shorter SVs. The number of added phased sites did not predict the number of blocks joined. 5mC added about twice as many phased sites as indels but joined fewer blocks, with little change in SNV switch error rates. The numbers of blocks joined by indels and 5mC were nearly additive. SVs barely changed block length, consistent with a design in which SVs never connect two blocks on their own. Indels joined more blocks but were also associated with more switch errors. A similar trade-off in \whatshap suggests that indel representation or modelling remains a shared challenge. GNN correction reduced the additional error associated with indels (Supplementary Note~1 and Supplementary Fig.~10). More accurate indel calls could reduce this error cost\cite{zheng2026accelerated,kolesnikov2024local}.

Selective withholding requires a distinction between evidence agreement and confidence in phase. \toolname reports phasing entropy and weighted vote totals. Unlike model-based confidence estimates from \hapcut\cite{edge2017hapcut2} and statistical phasers\cite{hofmeister2023shapeit5}, low entropy indicates agreement among available votes, not necessarily strong evidence or a low error probability. The GNN uses neighbouring graph and sequence features and may detect candidates missed by an entropy threshold alone. However, its added value over simpler withholding rules and \hapcut's confidence pruning remains untested when the same number of variants retain phase assignments. The GNN prediction score is neither calibrated nor included in the output. These distinctions limit the use of the reported scores as quantitative measures of error risk.

On nanopore data, \toolname phased 29--48\% fewer genotype errors than \whatshap, at a cost of 0.7--1.0 percentage points in benchmark-SNV completeness. On HiFi data, completeness was 0.2--0.5 percentage points lower than that of \whatshap and HiPhase. Withholding phase assignments and splitting blocks change which variant pairs can be assessed, so switch error rates must be interpreted together with completeness and contiguity. The calls that \toolname left unphased rarely matched the benchmark: 9.8--13.0\% matched a benchmark heterozygous SNV, compared with 89.9--93.3\% of the calls that it phased. Among unphased calls, 3.3--7.6\% were heterozygous calls at sites classified as homozygous in v5.0q. Most unphased calls were absent from the benchmark, which identifies a false positive inside the benchmark regions but leaves a call unclassified outside them. Among the assessable benchmark heterozygous SNVs that \toolname left unphased, \whatshap phased 10--23\% incorrectly, three to six times its overall rate. SVs showed the same pattern on both platforms: given the same SNVs and SVs, \whatshap phased 0.7--6.3\% more SVs correctly than \toolname, but 1.5--2.3 times as many incorrectly (Supplementary Table~6). In an exploratory analysis of a subset of unphased SNVs, most lay outside one-to-one assembly alignments, where their phasing accuracy remained unresolved.

Benchmark choice affected the apparent separation between methods, with greater differences in switch error rates against v5.0q than against GIAB v4.2.1. Many apparent errors under v4.2.1 occurred at the same variant pairs across tools, and switch-error counts in shared benchmark regions were substantially lower when the same outputs were scored against v5.0q. The counts were lower for all three phasers, including \whatshap and \hapcut, which have no parameters trained on either truth set. Errors in v4.2.1 phase transfer are one possible explanation, but shared reads, alignments and variant calls could also contribute to correlated outputs. Independent checks with reads or parental data are needed to distinguish these explanations.

Within v5.0q benchmark regions, \toolname had 2.1- to 9.5-fold lower switch error rates than \whatshap and \hapcut, including in newly covered regions. However, much of the genome-wide difference arose outside the benchmark regions of both truth sets, where the phase is least certain. This component remains exploratory. The v5.0q release evaluated variant calls rather than phase\cite{olson2026giabv5}. Our results support comparing phasers against assembly-phased truth sets, with phase errors reported separately inside and outside benchmark regions and stratified by genomic context\cite{dwarshuis2024stratifications}. Reporting which calls receive phase assignments also captures genotype errors propagated onto haplotypes, which phase-error rates alone do not measure.

Generalization remains the main evaluation limit. The network was trained on HG002 nanopore runs from the same down-sampling series as the main evaluation, using labels from the evaluation benchmark, and genome-wide HG002 metrics include 17 training chromosomes. On held-out chromosomes, the advantage over \whatshap and \hapcut persisted, but correction removed fewer errors at a higher cost in withheld SNVs (Supplementary Note~1). These chromosomes were excluded from GNN training but contributed to the choice between two label definitions and to earlier tuning of the graph constants. The HiFi results without retraining support transfer between platforms within HG002. With fixed weights and threshold, the lower switch error rates and the effect of correction persisted in two further individuals, one of them with a different variant caller (Supplementary Fig.~3). These tests used one nanopore chemistry, coverages up to 30$\times$ and one or three down-sampling seeds, and were scored against assembly-derived phase that is not independent of the evaluation reads in HG008-N. Tests in more individuals and chemistries, against independent benchmarks, are still needed. Subsampling variability was not estimated above 20$\times$ for nanopore data or at any HiFi coverage.

Further work should test both the scope of co-phasing and its downstream effects. 5mC evaluation measured phased fractions rather than phasing accuracy, and neither SVs nor 5mC sites had supervised training labels. SV phase was scored with one SV caller against variants called from the assembly rather than a curated SV benchmark. Methylation co-phasing was assessed only on autosomes of a male lymphoblastoid cell line\cite{fu2024methphaser}; the method assumes diploidy and balanced allele fractions. Compatibility with pipelines that use \longphase\cite{zheng2022clair3,zheng2026clairs,keskus2025severus,ont2025wfhuman} enables tests of selective withholding in haplotype-aware somatic calling. More accurate germline phase could improve tumour-read assignment, but withholding phase may reduce information available to the caller. Varying the germline phaser while fixing the somatic caller would quantify this trade-off. A calibrated per-variant confidence measure could further allow downstream methods to account explicitly for uncertainty in inherited phase.
