\toolname combines genetic variation and allele-specific methylation in a shared phasing graph with selective withholding of phase assignments predicted to be unreliable. This approach extends methods that use methylation or SVs to connect blocks in regions with few heterozygous SNVs\cite{akbari2021nanomethphase,fu2024methphaser,akbari2023parentoforigin,lin2022longphase,holt2024hiphase}. Whereas LongHap adds methylation after sequence phasing\cite{pfennig2026longhap}, \toolname integrates both during haplotype construction. The evidence classes contributed differently: methylation improved contiguity at similar SNV switch error rates, whereas indels produced most of the gains in contiguity and increases in error. The same trade-off in \whatshap suggests that difficulties in indel representation or modelling may affect both methods. In joint SNV and indel phasing, \toolname with GNN correction had 8--31-fold lower switch error rates than \whatshap at variant pairs containing an indel. GNN correction reduced the additional error associated with indels, and improved indel calling could further improve the balance\cite{zheng2026accelerated,kolesnikov2024local}.

Selective withholding requires a distinction between evidence agreement and confidence in phase. \toolname reports phasing entropy and weighted vote totals. Unlike model-based confidence estimates from \hapcut\cite{edge2017hapcut2} and statistical phasers\cite{hofmeister2023shapeit5}, low entropy indicates agreement among available votes, not necessarily strong evidence or a low error probability. The GNN uses neighbouring graph and sequence features and may detect candidates missed by an entropy threshold alone. However, its added value over simpler withholding rules and \hapcut's confidence pruning remains untested at matched variant retention. Its onset-prediction score is neither calibrated nor included in the output. These distinctions limit the use of the reported scores as quantitative measures of error risk.

Withholding variants and splitting blocks also change which variant pairs can be assessed. Switch error rates must therefore be considered together with completeness and contiguity. Before GNN correction, \toolname already had 1.9--2.5-fold lower switch error rates than \whatshap at similar block N50. Most additional SNV calls phased by \whatshap did not match a benchmark heterozygous SNV. Among assessable benchmark heterozygous SNVs phased by \whatshap alone, 10--23\% were phased incorrectly, three to six times its overall rate. \toolname had a similar error rate among the corresponding SNVs phased by it alone, but this set was 13--25 times smaller. Margin's lower error rate but shorter blocks at low coverage illustrates a different balance. At 60$\times$, most SNVs unphased by the GNN lay outside one-to-one assembly alignments, where our analysis cannot establish whether their phase was correct.

Benchmark choice changed the apparent separation between methods. At 30--60$\times$, the highest and lowest switch error rates differed by 1.2--1.3-fold against GIAB v4.2.1 but by 4.3--4.8-fold against v5.0q. Many apparent errors under v4.2.1 occurred at the same variant pairs across tools, and most disappeared when the same outputs were scored against v5.0q in shared benchmark regions. All three phasers agreed with v5.0q at most of these sites, including \whatshap and \hapcut, which have no parameters trained on either truth set. Errors in v4.2.1 phase transfer are one possible explanation, but shared reads, alignments and variant calls could also contribute to correlated outputs. Independent checks with reads or parental data are needed to distinguish these explanations.

Within v5.0q benchmark regions, \toolname had 2.1- to 10-fold lower switch error rates than \whatshap and \hapcut, with the largest separation in newly covered regions. However, much of the genome-wide difference arose outside the benchmark regions of both truth sets, where the phase is least certain. This component remains exploratory. The v5.0q release evaluated variant calls rather than phase\cite{olson2026giabv5}. Our results support comparing phasers against assembly-phased truth sets, with phase errors reported separately inside and outside benchmark regions and stratified by genomic context\cite{dwarshuis2024stratifications}. Reporting which calls receive phase assignments also captures genotype errors propagated onto haplotypes, which phase-error rates alone do not measure.

Generalization remains the main evaluation limit. All data came from HG002. The network was trained on nanopore runs from the same down-sampling series, using labels from the evaluation benchmark. Genome-wide metrics include 17 training chromosomes. On held-out chromosomes, the advantage over \whatshap and \hapcut persisted, but correction removed fewer errors at a higher cost in withheld SNVs. These chromosomes were excluded from GNN training but contributed to earlier tuning of the graph constants. The HiFi results without retraining support transfer between platforms within HG002, but do not establish transfer to other individuals. Evaluation in other individuals against an independent benchmark, with fixed weights and thresholds, is needed. Subsampling variability was not estimated above 20$\times$ for nanopore data or at any HiFi coverage.

Further work should test both the scope of co-phasing and its downstream effects. SV and 5mC evaluation measured phased fractions rather than phasing accuracy, and neither class had supervised training labels. Methylation co-phasing was assessed only on autosomes of a male lymphoblastoid cell line\cite{fu2024methphaser}; the method assumes diploidy and balanced allele fractions. Compatibility with pipelines that use \longphase\cite{zheng2022clair3,zheng2026clairs,keskus2025severus,ont2025wfhuman} enables tests of selective withholding in haplotype-aware somatic calling. More accurate germline phase could improve tumour-read assignment, but withholding phase may reduce information available to the caller. Varying the germline phaser while fixing the somatic caller would quantify this trade-off. A calibrated per-variant confidence measure could further allow downstream methods to account explicitly for uncertainty in inherited phase.
