\toolname joins two ideas that read-based phasing has kept apart: that every class of heterozygous evidence a read carries belongs in one haplotype model, and that a phaser should report, variant by variant, how far its own output can be trusted. The first extends an observation made independently for methylation\cite{akbari2021nanomethphase,fu2024methphaser,chen2025hapbridge} and for SVs\cite{lin2022longphase,holt2024hiphase}: SNVs alone strand haplotype blocks wherever heterozygosity is low. Calling allele-specific 5mC from modification tags into the same weighted graph as SNVs, indels and SVs raises block N50 above SNV-only phasing by more than 70\% at 60$\times$ (5.0 versus 2.9~Mb; 40\% after correction), with no post-processing step and on nanopore rather than HiFi reads, to which joint small- and structural-variant phasing had been confined\cite{holt2024hiphase}. The second replaces the binary phased-or-unphased state that every current tool reports, ours included, with a graded one, giving downstream analyses the handle on phase reliability that genotype quality provides for calls.

The most consequential finding here concerns evaluation rather than algorithms: scored against two truth sets, the same phased VCFs on the same reference yield not only different error magnitudes but a different ordering of methods. GIAB v4.2.1\cite{wagner2022giab} excludes most segmental duplications, satellite arrays and large tandem repeats from assessment; the T2T-based HG002 Q100 benchmark\cite{jarvis2022hg002,rhie2023y,giab2025q100}, projected onto GRCh38 from a near-complete diploid assembly of the same individual, assesses exactly that sequence. Every tool accumulates more switch errors there, in rate as in absolute terms, and the spread between tools widens from under 30\% to three- to fivefold: mis-mapped reads, homopolymer errors and collapsed paralogues create systematically wrong linkages there rather than random noise, the errors that the evidence calibration in \toolname targets. The ranking can also invert: \longphase~1.0 made more switch errors than \whatshap under v4.2.1 and fewer under Q100. Almost every published comparison of phasers\cite{patterson2015whatshap,edge2017hapcut2,shafin2021margin,holt2024hiphase,fu2024methphaser,lin2022longphase} used GRCh38-based GIAB releases and so describes the easier part of the genome. Two observations argue that the extra errors are genuine and not artefacts of a draft truth set: Hamming distance is nearly unchanged between benchmarks for the four current tools, marking isolated switches rather than the long-range disagreements a mis-projected benchmark would create, and the advantage of \toolname over \whatshap and \hapcut persists, with a smaller margin, under v4.2.1. Q100 is nonetheless still being refined, and residual discordance in the hardest regions may be benchmark rather than phasing error; comparisons should therefore report both truth sets and stratify switch errors by genomic context\cite{nurk2022t2t,liao2023pangenome}.

The neural network complements the graph algorithm rather than replacing it. Where Ralphi\cite{battistella2025ralphi} and GCphase\cite{luo2024gcphase} learn haplotype assembly itself and must scale to whole chromosomes, \toolname confines learning to windows around high-entropy variants, as graph networks have been used to resolve ambiguous regions of assembly graphs rather than to assemble genomes\cite{vrcek2025assembly}; inference costs one to two minutes per genome and fewer than 700,000 parameters compile into the binary. Because the correction only withholds phase, the objection arises that fewer switch errors merely reflect a smaller denominator. Three observations argue otherwise. The rate is computed per assessed pair, and of the roughly 60,000 SNVs unphased at 60$\times$ only 2,411 are heterozygous in the benchmark and enter that denominator, far too few to explain the fall from 740 to 490 genome-wide switch errors. Block N50 is almost unaffected (2.87 versus 2.9~Mb), which pruning informative links could not achieve. And the phased fraction stays within about a percentage point of \whatshap (91.6\% versus 92.5\% at 30--60$\times$)\todo{confirm 91.6\% is the phased fraction after correction; this argument rests on it}. At 10$\times$ the trade is less favourable, 14\% of what is unphased being genuine heterozygotes and N50 falling from 0.93 to 0.84~Mb. The asymmetry is the point: 86--96\% of what is removed are calls the benchmark does not support as heterozygous, mostly caller artefacts at clustered or low-quality sites whose spurious phase would otherwise reach haplotagged reads and the somatic callers that consume them\cite{zheng2026clairs,chen2025clairsto,keskus2025severus,park2024deepsomatic}.

Several limitations remain. The network was trained and evaluated on one individual, HG002, sequenced on a single nanopore chemistry\todo{Methods must state which HG002 chromosomes and coverages were held out of training, and which truth set supplied the training labels; without a chromosome-level hold-out the evaluation is circular}. Two choices bound the overfitting: the features are generic (vote counts, entropy, sequence context, graph topology; no sequence embeddings, no sample-specific statistics), and the output is restricted to withholding phase, so a miscalibrated model costs contiguity rather than inventing haplotypes. Transfer to PacBio HiFi, duplex chemistry and non-human genomes is untested; the graph algorithm supports both platforms, but for the network reported here cross-platform performance is an expectation, not a result. Indel co-phasing is a trade-off we have chosen rather than solved: it lengthens blocks but raises the uncorrected SNV switch error rate (0.036\% to 0.048\% at 60$\times$), and is worth taking only because correction more than repays the loss, leaving four-class co-phasing both more accurate and 40\% longer-blocked than uncorrected SNV-only phasing. Better indel calling\cite{zheng2026accelerated,kolesnikov2024local} would dissolve it, and SNV-only phasing remains available meanwhile. The phased fraction of SVs and indels is bounded by the upstream callers, and 5mC co-phasing requires modification-aware base calling and \texttt{MM}/\texttt{ML} tags retained through alignment. Finally, \toolname assumes a diploid genome with balanced allele fractions, which tumours violate. We address that setting separately, in companion manuscripts that carry the \longphase~1.0 graph, rather than the germline method described here, into tumour genomes: LongPhase-S\cite{ho2025longphases} for paired tumour--normal somatic haplotyping and tumour DNA fraction estimation, and LongPhase-TO\cite{chen2026longphaseto} for somatic haplotype reconstruction from a tumour alone (both under review; Methods). The division of labour is deliberate --- germline multimodal co-phasing and phase confidence here, somatic lineage and loss of heterozygosity there --- and it sets the next step, since tumour analyses built on germline blocks, from somatic callers to copy-number reconstruction\cite{keskus2025wakhan}, inherit whatever phase error the germline phaser leaves behind.

Trio assembly\cite{cheng2021hifiasm,rautiainen2023verkko}, Hi-C\cite{selvaraj2013hic} and Strand-seq\cite{porubsky2021fully,henglin2024graphasing} reach chromosome scale but need extra samples or libraries, and imputation\cite{sun2025advances,ebler2022pangenie,gong2025haplotypepanel} depends on panels still sparse for structural variation and many populations; \toolname needs one sample and one library, and phase reliability makes its output a natural input to hybrid read-plus-panel strategies\cite{liao2023pangenome}. As a drop-in replacement in pipelines already built on \longphase\cite{zheng2022clair3,zheng2026clairs,keskus2025severus,ont2025wfhuman}, it delivers the new annotations without interface changes, and what they enable is concrete. A compound-heterozygous diagnosis rests on a single \textit{trans} call between two variants in one gene; the entropy of those variants and of the links between them turns that call into a quantity a diagnostic report can qualify, flagging the cases that warrant a confirmatory assay. Allele-specific methylation analyses treat every haplotagged read alike, whereas weighting reads by the confidence of the variants that tagged them would keep imprinting and \textit{cis}-regulatory signals from being diluted by thinly supported blocks. And haplotype-aware somatic callers\cite{zheng2026clairs,keskus2025severus} accept inherited phase without qualification; propagating entropy would let them discount precisely the loci at which switch errors concentrate. Like genotype quality before it, phase confidence becomes useful only once it is consumed.
