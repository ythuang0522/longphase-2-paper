\subsection*{Overview of \toolname}
\toolname is a diploid germline phaser that takes long-read alignments, a reference genome and heterozygous variant calls, and returns phased VCFs and haplotagged alignments (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).  SNVs, small indels, structural variants (SVs) and allele-specific 5-methylcytosine (5mC) observations are represented together in one weighted graph per chromosome.  Initial haplotypes are resolved from weighted multi-neighbour votes, reads are used for a second phase-consistency pass, and a graph neural network (GNN) then scores local phase errors.  Variants predicted to be unreliable are unphased, never flipped; if this disconnects a haplotype block, the surviving components receive updated phase-set identifiers.  The detailed module rules are given in Supplementary Methods~1--6.  The method extends the graph formulation of \longphase~1\cite{lin2022longphase}.

\subsection*{Input variant calls}
Heterozygous SNVs and small indels were taken from PEPPER-Margin-DeepVariant\cite{shafin2021margin}; homozygous and filtered records were carried through unchanged but did not enter the graph.  Indels in short tandem repeats were retained as evidence but down-weighted, because a misplaced repeat indel can link the wrong alleles.  SVs were taken from Sniffles2\cite{smolka2024sniffles2} or cuteSV\cite{jiang2020cutesv} with supporting read names; homozygous SVs, duplicated positions and SVs coincident with SNVs were excluded.  The exact eligibility and repeat annotation rules are in Supplementary Method~2.

\subsection*{Calling allele-specific methylation}
Modification probabilities were read from the \texttt{MM}/\texttt{ML} tags produced by Dorado\cite{ont2025dorado} and carried through alignment.  A CpG was called methylated when its 5mC probability was at least 0.8, unmethylated when it was at most 0.2, and ambiguous otherwise.  Forward- and reverse-strand observations were mapped to one CpG coordinate.  A site was retained as a candidate allele-specific site when methylated and unmethylated reads were sufficiently balanced and the ambiguous fraction was small:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\min(M_R,U_R)}{\max(M_R,U_R)} \ge 0.6,
\qquad
\frac{A_R}{M_R+U_R+A_R} \le 0.2,
\label{eq:hetmeth}
\end{equation}
where $M_R$, $U_R$ and $A_R$ are the three read counts.  Candidates were then required to co-segregate with a neighbouring heterozygous marker.  For a pair of sites, writing $RR$, $RA$, $AR$ and $AA$ for the four observed state combinations, linkage required
\begin{equation}
\frac{\max(RR+AA,\;RA+AR)}{RR+AA+RA+AR} \ge 0.9.
\label{eq:linkmeth}
\end{equation}
The same criterion was used for SNV-anchored and methylation-only linking.  Only CpG dinucleotides were considered.  Weak candidates were admitted in a later expansion pass, and consecutive CpGs were merged into one locus so that both strands shared one read list.  The resulting methylated and unmethylated read names and depths were supplied to the phasing graph.  The depth bound and expansion rules are in Supplementary Method~1.

\subsection*{Read-level allele observations and filtering}
Alignments with mapping quality below 1, unmapped, secondary or duplicate records were excluded.  SNV alleles came from the aligned base and its Phred quality; indel alleles from the CIGAR; SV alleles from a matching CIGAR event or the caller's read list; and 5mC alleles from the methylation read lists.  Supplementary alignments were retained as evidence, so when two alignments of the same read overlapped by more than 20\% of their combined span, the shorter alignment was discarded.  Under the nanopore setting, a downstream SNV was removed when two SNVs at most 2~bp apart both lay in homopolymers of length at least three.  A copy-number-aware filter identified intervals bounded by excess front- or back-clipping and removed variants whose alternate-supporting reads had an unusually high mismatch load relative to reference-supporting reads.  Specifically, a read's mismatch load $R_m$ counted the heterozygous sites in the interval at which it carried the alternate allele, and each variant was scored by
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MR}(v) = \frac{\bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{ALT}}}{\bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{REF}} + \bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{ALT}}},
\label{eq:cnvmr}
\end{equation}
where the barred terms are the mean loads of reads supporting the ALT and REF alleles of $v$. Sites with $\mathrm{MR}(v)\ge 0.7$ were removed from all reads.  The three-state interval scanner and its constants are specified in Supplementary Method~2.

\subsection*{Weighted phasing graph}
Each heterozygous variant contributed reference and alternate allele nodes.  For each read, every allele was connected to the next 35 observed variants, with no edge-distance cap. Variants followed by a gap of more than 300~kb to the next heterozygous variant were skipped during haplotype resolution, casting no votes and receiving no haplotype or entropy.  A read contributed weight 1 when both observations had quality at least 12 and weight 0.1 otherwise.  Indel and 5mC observations and SV alternate observations were assigned fixed quality 60; reference-allele SV observations were assigned 30.  Thus all such observations have full weight at the default threshold, while reference-SV evidence is down-weighted only at a higher threshold.

For a variant pair $(u,v)$, the four allele-pair supports were accumulated as $w_{rr}$, $w_{ra}$, $w_{ar}$ and $w_{aa}$.  The two possible pairings have support $P=w_{rr}+w_{aa}$ and $Q=w_{ra}+w_{ar}$, and their ambiguity is $s=\min(P,Q)/\max(P,Q)$.

\subsection*{Haplotype resolution by multi-neighbour voting}
Variants were processed in coordinate order.  Each variant cast a vote to each downstream neighbour for the pairing with larger support, except when $s>0.7$; SNV--5mC pairs used a 0.3 threshold and required at least one read-equivalent of support.  Votes had weight 1 by default, 20 for near-unanimous support ($s\le0.1$ with total support at least 1, or one pairing below 1 while the other was at least 1), and 0.1 when the voting variant was a tandem-repeat indel.  The vote was translated using the already assigned haplotype of the upstream variant, producing totals $h_1(v)$ and $h_2(v)$.  When more than three votes had support of at most 1 read-equivalent, and at least one qualifying vote remained, only votes with $s<0.2$, weight at least 1 and a non-indel voting variant were retained.  The larger total assigned the haplotype.  A tie beyond the last accepted edge opened a new block; a tie within an existing block left the variant unphased and cast no votes.  The first variant position defined the phase-set identifier.  The constants were fixed by a pre-study sweep on HG002 at 10--60$\times$ and were not re-tuned for the reported experiments; the parameter tuning is described in Supplementary Method~3.

\subsection*{Read-based correction and phasing entropy}
After the graph assignment, each read was assigned to a haplotype by a weighted majority of its phased SNV and SV alleles (weight 1), indel alleles (weight 0.1) and 5mC alleles (not used for this pass).  Reads were retained only with a majority above 0.65 and total evidence above 1.  For each variant, the consistency of the two read populations was
\[
\rho=\frac{\max(n_1,n_2)}{n_1+n_2},
\]
where $n_1$ counts haplotype-1 reads carrying REF plus haplotype-2 reads carrying ALT, and $n_2$ counts the converse. The majority pairing assigned the phase only when $\rho>0.75$; otherwise the variant was left unphased.

For each phased variant, phasing entropy was calculated from the weighted haplotype totals after the single-read guard:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PE}(v)=-\sum_{i\in\{1,2\}}p_i\log_2p_i,
\qquad p_i=\frac{h_i}{h_1+h_2}.
\end{equation}
It ranges from 0 for unanimous support to 1 bit for equal support.  When $h_1+h_2=0$, PE was set to 0 and the case was identified from the accompanying $h_1,h_2$ fields; this occurs only at the first variant of a block.  PE, $h_1$ and $h_2$ were written to the VCF, and accepted voting edges with their accumulated weights formed the GNN input graph.

\subsection*{Graph neural network for phase-error detection}
The GNN operated on merged SNV/indel, SV and 5mC records.  A window of at most 41 variants (82 allele nodes) was opened around every phased variant with $\mathrm{PE}\ge0.8$, extending 20 variants on either side.  It assigned a non-calibrated phase-error score to each variant.  The 31 node features summarized phasing evidence, sequence context, graph structure and the four variant classes; six edge features summarized vote weight, genomic distance, allele matching and phase-set relations.  Edges were restricted to the window, symmetrized and supplemented with mean-feature self-loops.  Exact definitions and normalizations are in Supplementary Tables~1--2.

The network used four GPS graph-transformer layers\cite{rampasek2022gps}: a local four-head GATv2 branch\cite{velickovic2018gat,brody2022gatv2} over graph edges and a global four-head self-attention branch\cite{vaswani2017attention}, followed by residual normalization and a 128--256--128 feed-forward block.  A two-layer classifier produced the two-class softmax output.  The two allele-node scores were averaged, as were scores from overlapping windows.  Only variants no further from the trigger than half the largest trigger distance in the window were scored; the same rule defined the nodes used for training loss.  Nodes outside that zone supplied context only.  Exhaustive architecture and inference details are in Supplementary Method~4 and Table~3.

Training windows came from the same HG002 nanopore co-phasing runs used for evaluation at 10--20$\times$ (six coverages and ten down-sampling replicates, 60 runs).  Of 722,116 windows, chr1--14 and chr18--20 supplied training (602,788), chr15--16 validation (67,880), and chr17, chr21 and chr22 testing (51,448); the chromosome sets were disjoint.  No test-chromosome window entered gradient updates, batch-normalization statistics or checkpoint selection.  Windows contained all four variant classes, but only benchmarked SNV and indel nodes had labels, so SV and 5mC correction was transferred from neighbouring small-variant evidence.  The training and evaluation truth was the v5.0q small-variant benchmark.  Training windows were limited to 10--20$\times$, whereas results also included 30--60$\times$ and PacBio HiFi data.

Labels described local phase orientation.  Within each window and phase set, the first benchmarked variant anchored the orientation; later disagreements were labelled as a single-variant flip or unphase event, or as a break at the first site of a longer run.  Benchmark-absent variants and SV/5mC records were masked from the loss but retained as graph context.  The released model merged flip, unphase and break into one error class.  Error labels accounted for 2.96\% of labelled central nodes in the training split.  Correct nodes were subsampled to at most four per error node, and the resulting cross-entropy used focal weighting\cite{lin2017focal} with $\gamma=1.5$ and label smoothing 0.05.  Training used haplotype-swap and coverage-scaling augmentations; the optimizer, stopping rule and environment are listed in Supplementary Table~4.

Checkpoints were selected on chr15--16 by maximizing
\begin{equation}
S=R\left(2-\frac{1}{P}\right)
\end{equation}
over 81 score thresholds from 0.01 to 0.99, subject to $R\ge0.005$, where $P$ and $R$ are error-class precision and recall.  The selected checkpoint was epoch 44; validation and held-out classification metrics are given in Supplementary Table~4.  The deployment threshold $B=0.30$ was chosen on the 60 HG002 nanopore runs at 10--20$\times$ as the threshold that removed the most switch errors while withholding at most ten phased SNVs per removed error. It removed 33.1\% of switch errors at 8.3 withheld SNVs per removed error over the whole genome, and 21.2\% at 10.5, slightly above the limit, on the held-out chromosomes (Supplementary Method~4 and Supplementary Table~15).

\subsection*{GNN correction and phase-set update}
A variant with mean score at least 0.30 was unphased while preserving its genotype. Bridge protection was disabled. If removing these variants disconnected a block, the surviving connected components became separate phase sets (Supplementary Method~4).

\subsection*{Haplotagging}
Reads were tagged from the phased SNV, indel, SV and 5mC alleles they carried.  Each informative observation contributed one vote, and a read was assigned to the majority haplotype when its fraction was at least 0.60; reads whose informative variants belonged to multiple phase sets or lacked a majority were left untagged.  Tagged reads received HP, PS and phasing-quality fields.  SV alleles were re-derived from the CIGAR and 5mC evidence was matched by read name.  Full evidence and quality definitions are in Supplementary Method~5.

\subsection*{Benchmark data}
The benchmark used HG002 (NA24385) Oxford Nanopore R10.4.1 reads from the ONT \texttt{giab\_lsk114\_2022.12} release: two PromethION flow cells (PAG65784 and PAG68757), SQK-LSK114, 400 bases per second and 4-kHz sampling, basecalled with Dorado \texttt{dna\_r10.4.1\_e8.2\_400bps\_sup@v4.0.0} and \texttt{5mCG\_5hmCG@v2}; their read N50 values were 29.2 and 29.4~kb.  Reads were aligned to the GRCh38 no-alt analysis set by Oxford Nanopore with minimap2 v2.24 (\texttt{-y -ax map-ont}), retaining reads with mean base quality $\ge 10$.  The 60$\times$ data set is a random 91.14\% subset of the 70.4$\times$ alignment (samtools v1.15, \texttt{view -s 0.9114}), and the 10--50$\times$ data sets were down-sampled from it with samtools v1.23.1\cite{danecek2021samtools} \texttt{view -s} \textit{seed.fraction}.  Ten replicates were used at 10--20$\times$ and one at 30--60$\times$; variants were called separately at every coverage and replicate.  SNVs and indels came from PEPPER-Margin-DeepVariant r0.8, which reports unphased genotypes, SVs from Sniffles2 v2.8.0, and allele-specific 5mC sites from \toolname.
The seeds were 1--9 and 867 at 10$\times$ (fraction 0.1666), 12--21 at 12$\times$ (0.200), 22--31 at 14$\times$ (0.233), 32--41 at 16$\times$ (0.266), 42--51 at 18$\times$ (0.300), 1--10 at 20$\times$ (0.3333), and 42 at 30, 40 and 50$\times$ (0.5, 0.6667 and 0.8333).  PEPPER-Margin-DeepVariant used the \texttt{--ont\_r10\_q20} model for nanopore data and \texttt{--hifi} for HiFi data.
HG002 PacBio HiFi (Revio) reads were taken from a 48$\times$ GIAB alignment (Supplementary Table~5), which was used as 50$\times$; 10--40$\times$ data sets were down-sampled from it with samtools v1.23.1 \texttt{view --subsample} (seed 1; fractions 0.2083, 0.4167, 0.6250 and 0.8333), and variants were called separately at every coverage.

Accuracy was scored with \texttt{longphase compare} on autosomes 1--22 against two HG002 truth sets: GIAB v4.2.1\cite{wagner2022giab}, in the version with phase transferred from a hifiasm assembly (the v4.2.1 benchmark VCF itself is unphased), restricted to biallelic heterozygous records; and GIAB v5.0q\cite{olson2026giabv5}, derived from the diploid T2T-HG002 assembly\cite{hansen2026q100}, in which every heterozygous variant is phased.  The truth VCFs were not restricted to their benchmark regions, and a call was scored only where the truth VCF holds the same REF and ALT alleles in a heterozygous genotype.  Both truth VCFs list heterozygous variants outside their benchmark regions (9.3\% of the autosomal heterozygous records of v5.0q and 3.4\% of v4.2.1), so the error counts include sites that the benchmark regions do not cover, among them satellite and segmental-duplication sequence that the T2T-HG002 assembly resolves\cite{hansen2026q100}.  To locate switch errors and to score indels, every consecutive variant pair in an intersected block was recorded with a re-implementation of \texttt{longphase compare}, which reproduced its counts exactly in all 1,024 scored runs.  A pair was assigned to a region when both variants lay in it; regions were the intersection and the differences of the two sets of benchmark regions, and the GIAB v3.6 genome stratifications\cite{dwarshuis2024stratifications}.  Indel switch errors were counted over consecutive pairs that included at least one indel, and the indel Hamming distance was computed in the block orientation chosen on all variants.

\subsection*{Comparison with other methods}
\whatshap~v2.8\cite{patterson2015whatshap} and \hapcut~v1.3.4\cite{edge2017hapcut2} were run on the same alignments and variant calls; cross-tool SNV comparisons used SNV-only phasing, with indel phasing disabled in \whatshap.  \longphase~1.0\cite{lin2022longphase} and \toolname were run with the nanopore setting, and GNN correction used PE trigger 0.8, score threshold 0.30, no bridge protection and block splitting.  Co-phasing experiments separately evaluated the seven variant-class combinations listed in Supplementary Table~10, with and without correction.  Indel co-phasing was also compared with \whatshap's default indel phasing.  Margin v2.3.1\cite{shafin2021margin}, Ralphi\cite{battistella2025ralphi} and MethPhaser\cite{fu2024methphaser} were evaluated as described in Supplementary Table~5.  MethPhaser was given the SNV-only output of \toolname without GNN correction instead of the \whatshap or \hapcut phasing its authors recommend as input, so that MethPhaser and the joint SNV and 5mC phasing of \toolname started from the same SNV phasing. Without GNN correction, differences between them reflect how methylation is used, after phasing to bridge existing blocks (MethPhaser) or during phasing as additional graph evidence (\toolname); with GNN correction, they also include its effect.  GCphase\cite{luo2024gcphase} was not included: it treats every input record as a heterozygous SNV and ignores the FILTER field, so with these call sets it phased about 1.2 million rejected reference calls per 10$\times$ genome as heterozygous variants, and a fair comparison would require a different input.  The HiFi call sets were phased SNV-only with \toolname (\texttt{--pb}), with and without correction, and with \whatshap; the GNN weights were trained on nanopore data.
\whatshap was run as \texttt{whatshap phase --ignore-read-groups --reference} with \texttt{--only-snvs} for SNV comparisons, and \hapcut as \texttt{extractHAIRS --ont 1} followed by \texttt{HAPCUT2} with default parameters, including its default likelihood-based pruning, on the PASS heterozygous SNVs.  \longphase~1.0 was re-run on every down-sampling replicate.  All tools ran on the same workstation.

\subsection*{Evaluation metrics}
Metrics (Supplementary Fig.~8) were computed by intersecting heterozygous query and truth records by position and REF/ALT alleles, then partitioning the intersection into blocks phased in both files; singleton blocks were ignored.  Within each block, switch errors were the Hamming distance between truth and query switch-encoded haplotype strings, and the switch-error rate divided their number by the assessed consecutive SNV pairs.  Isolated switches and two-consecutive-switch flips were reported separately.  Hamming distance was the block-wise minimum over the two orientations, divided by the number of compared variants; unlike switch rate, it included indels when present in both files.  Phased fraction was the number of truth heterozygous SNVs or indels that were phased by the query divided by all truth heterozygous variants of that class.  Block N50 was the span-weighted median of query phase-set spans of at least two variants, pooled over chromosomes.  Switch-error positions were exported for the unphased-SNV analysis.  The exact block and denominator definitions are in Supplementary Method~6.

\subsection*{Analysis of unphased SNVs}
An SNV was counted as unphased when it was phased in the pre-correction output and unphased after GNN correction.  Such sites were classified against the v5.0q benchmark VCF, matched by chromosome and position and without a benchmark BED, as absent, homozygous or heterozygous (Supplementary Fig.~16a); the heterozygous count was also reported with matching REF and ALT alleles, the rule used by \texttt{compare}.  The analysis used the 10--60$\times$ outputs of replicate~1 with the final GNN model.  At 60$\times$, they were also classified against the T2T-HG002 assembly projected to GRCh38 as outside the 1:1 assembly BED, inside without an assembly variant at the same start coordinate, or inside with one.  A random sample of 50,000 SNVs phased before correction provided the background; enrichment was summarized by chromosome and 1-Mb bins.  To compare tools, shared heterozygous SNVs were partitioned into sites unphased by \toolname only, WhatsHap only or both, and the fraction of each tool-specific set within the other tool's switch-error intervals was compared with the fraction of that other tool's assessed SNVs within those intervals.

\subsection*{Runtime and memory}
Wall-clock time, CPU time and peak resident memory were measured with \texttt{/usr/bin/time}; reported \toolname times included alignment reading, graph construction, phasing, GNN correction and VCF writing, and excluded alignment, variant calling and down-sampling.  All runs used one workstation (AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, 16 cores and 32 threads, 249~GB of memory, Ubuntu 24.04, input and output on a hard-disk drive).  \toolname was compiled with g++ 13.3 at \texttt{-O3} and timed with one and with 24 threads; \whatshap and \hapcut ran single-threaded.  Runtime was measured once, on replicate~1 at 10$\times$ and on the 60$\times$ data set (Supplementary Table~16).

\subsection*{Statistics and reproducibility}
Ten nanopore down-sampling replicates at 10--20$\times$ were summarized by their mean and standard deviation, while 30--60$\times$ and HiFi used one down-sampling per coverage.  Subsamples for each platform were drawn from the same source sequencing data set of HG002. The reported standard deviations therefore describe variation due to read subsampling, conditional on that data set, rather than variation among independent sequencing experiments. Subsampling variability was not estimated at 30--60$\times$ or for HiFi. Tools compared at each coverage and replicate used the same sampled reads.  No statistical tests, sample-size calculation, randomization or blinding were used.  GNN inference used fixed model weights and no sampling.
