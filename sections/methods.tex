\subsection*{Overview of \toolname}
\toolname is a read-based phaser for diploid germline genomes (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).  It takes long-read alignments, a reference genome and variant calls, and returns phased variants and haplotagged alignments.  Phasing has four steps.  First, allele-specific 5-methylcytosine (5mC) sites are called from the alignments, and the alleles that each read carries at SNVs, small indels, structural variants (SVs) and 5mC sites are extracted.  Second, the observations of all four classes are combined in one weighted graph per chromosome.  Third, haplotypes are resolved from the graph by weighted voting and are then checked against the reads.  Fourth, a graph neural network (GNN) identifies probable phase errors, and these variants are left unphased.  The method extends the graph formulation of \longphase~1\cite{lin2022longphase} (detailed rules in Supplementary Notes~1--6).

The main parameters of \toolname are user-adjustable and are denoted by symbols, with their default values; the complete list is Supplementary Table~12.  All runs reported here used the defaults, which were the same at every coverage and on both platforms apart from the platform setting (nanopore or HiFi).  The defaults of voting and read filtering were set during the development of the method, before this study (Supplementary Note~3).

\subsection*{Input alignments and variant calls}
\toolname takes long-read alignments, a reference genome and variant calls of up to four classes.  Three classes come from external callers.  SNVs and small indels are read from the VCF of a small-variant caller such as DeepVariant\cite{poplin2018deepvariant} or Clair3\cite{zheng2022clair3}.  SVs are read from the VCF of an SV caller such as Sniffles2\cite{smolka2024sniffles2} or cuteSV\cite{jiang2020cutesv}.  Allele-specific 5mC sites are called internally by \toolname from the modification tags of the alignments (next subsection).  Only heterozygous calls that pass the caller's filters enter the graph; other small-variant records are written to the output unchanged.  Homozygous SVs, SVs at duplicated positions and SVs whose position coincides with an SNV are excluded (Supplementary Note~1).

\subsection*{Internal calling of allele-specific methylation}
\toolname identifies allele-specific 5mC sites in four steps (Supplementary Fig.~9 and Supplementary Note~2).  First, the CpG observation of each read is classified as methylated or unmethylated.  Second, CpG sites with a balanced mixture of methylated and unmethylated reads are selected as candidates.  Third, to confirm that the two methylation states lie on different haplotypes, a candidate is accepted only if it is linked to a nearby heterozygous site, that is, if the reads divide into the same two groups at the two sites.  Fourth, accepted CpGs are merged into loci and supplied to the phasing graph.

\emph{Read classification.}  Modification probabilities are read from the \texttt{MM}/\texttt{ML} tags that the basecaller writes (here Dorado\cite{ont2025dorado}) and that are carried through alignment.  Each read-level CpG observation is classified as methylated when its 5mC probability is high, unmethylated when it is low, and ambiguous otherwise.  Forward- and reverse-strand observations are mapped to one CpG coordinate.  Only CpG dinucleotides are used.

\emph{Candidate sites.}  A CpG site is a candidate when its methylated and unmethylated reads are balanced and its ambiguous fraction is small:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\min(M_R,U_R)}{\max(M_R,U_R)} \ge \alpha,
\qquad
\frac{A_R}{M_R+U_R+A_R} \le \beta,
\label{eq:hetmeth}
\end{equation}
where $M_R$, $U_R$ and $A_R$ are the numbers of methylated, unmethylated and ambiguous reads, respectively, and $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are user-adjustable thresholds (defaults 0.6 and 0.2).

\emph{Linkage test.}  Each read that covers two sites has one of two states at each site: the reference or the alternate allele at an SNV, and methylated or unmethylated at a CpG.  $RR$, $RA$, $AR$ and $AA$ are the numbers of reads with each combination of states.  The two sites are linked when most reads have one of the two consistent pairings:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\max(RR+AA,\;RA+AR)}{RR+AA+RA+AR} \ge \gamma,
\label{eq:linkmeth}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma$ is the user-adjustable linkage threshold (default 0.9).  Candidates are accepted in three stages.  First, each candidate is tested against a nearby heterozygous SNV or, where no informative SNV is in range, against another candidate CpG, and it is accepted if the two sites are linked.  Second, the candidates that are not linked are tested again against the accepted CpGs, so that a site without a linked SNV can be connected through an accepted CpG.  Finally, an accepted CpG is kept only if it is also linked to both the preceding and the following accepted site, which removes sites that are not consistent with their neighbours.  In each stage, a pair is tested only when enough reads span both sites.

\emph{Merging and output.}  Consecutive accepted CpGs are merged into one locus with a shared read list for both strands.  The methylated and unmethylated read names of each locus are supplied to the phasing graph.

\subsection*{Read-level allele observations and filtering}
Each read contributes one allele observation at each heterozygous variant that it covers, and these observations are the evidence for the phasing graph (Supplementary Fig.~10 and Supplementary Note~1).  \toolname first selects the alignments, then extracts the allele of each variant class, and last removes the observations of sites at which reads are probably misaligned.

\emph{Alignment selection.}  Unmapped, secondary and duplicate alignments, and alignments below a minimum mapping quality, are excluded.  Supplementary alignments are kept as evidence, so when two alignments of the same read overlap substantially, the shorter alignment is discarded.

\emph{Allele extraction.}  The source of an allele depends on the variant class.  An SNV allele is the aligned base, with its Phred quality.  An indel allele comes from the CIGAR: a read supports the alternate allele when an insertion or deletion begins immediately after the variant position, and the reference allele otherwise.  An SV allele comes from an insertion or deletion near the SV start whose length is similar to the SV length, from the junction of a split alignment that ends at the SV or, when available, from the caller's list of supporting reads.  A 5mC allele comes from the methylated and unmethylated read lists of the internal call.

\emph{Copy-number-aware filter.}  Reads from paralogous sequence that align to one locus can produce false heterozygous sites.  \toolname therefore identifies intervals bounded by an excess of front- or back-clipped alignments and, within each interval, tests whether the reads that support the alternate allele of a variant also carry an excess of other alternate alleles.  The mismatch load $R_m$ of a read is the number of non-SV heterozygous sites in the interval at which it carries the alternate allele.  Each variant $v$ is scored by
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MR}(v) = \frac{\bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{ALT}}}{\bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{REF}} + \bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{ALT}}},
\label{eq:cnvmr}
\end{equation}
where the barred terms are the mean loads of the reads that support the ALT and REF alleles of $v$.  Comparable loads in the two read groups give an MR near 0.5, and a larger load in the reads with the alternate allele increases MR.  MR is evaluated only when the two mean loads are above zero.  A site whose MR exceeds a fixed threshold is removed from all reads, except SVs, which are retained.

\subsection*{Weighted phasing graph}
\toolname combines the allele observations of a chromosome in one graph, in which the weight of an edge is the read support for a pair of alleles (Supplementary Fig.~11a,b).  Each heterozygous variant contributes a reference and an alternate allele node.  For each read, every allele is connected to the alleles that the read carries at the next $k$ heterozygous variants in chromosome order (default $k=35$), with no limit on genomic distance.  A read adds full weight to an edge when both observations pass a base-quality threshold, and a reduced weight otherwise.  Observations of indels, SVs and 5mC sites have no base quality and have full weight under the default settings.

For a variant pair $(u,v)$, the supports of the four allele pairs are accumulated as $w_{rr}$, $w_{ra}$, $w_{ar}$ and $w_{aa}$.  The two possible phasings of the pair have support $P=w_{rr}+w_{aa}$ and $Q=w_{ra}+w_{ar}$.  The ambiguity of the pair is $s=\min(P,Q)/\max(P,Q)$, which is 0 when all reads support one phasing and 1 when the two phasings have equal support.  A variant separated from the next heterozygous variant by more than a maximum distance is skipped during haplotype resolution: it casts no votes and receives no haplotype or entropy.

\subsection*{Haplotype resolution by multi-neighbour voting}
\toolname assigns haplotypes in one pass along each chromosome (Supplementary Fig.~11c--f).  Each variant receives votes from the upstream variants that are connected to it, and the weighted majority of these votes decides its haplotype, so that the phase of a variant does not depend on one neighbour only.

\emph{Votes.}  Each variant casts a vote to each downstream neighbour for the phasing with the larger support.  A pair casts no vote when its ambiguity $s$ exceeds a threshold $\tau$ (default 0.7); SNV--5mC pairs use a stricter threshold.  Votes are weighted by their reliability: a near-unanimous pair votes with a larger weight, and an indel in a tandem repeat votes with a smaller weight, because a misplaced repeat indel can link the wrong alleles (Supplementary Note~3).

\emph{Haplotype assignment.}  Each vote is translated through the haplotype already assigned to the upstream variant, and the votes give the totals $h_1(v)$ and $h_2(v)$.  A single-read guard limits the effect of weak votes: when several votes have the support of one read only and stronger votes remain, only the stronger votes are counted.  The variant is assigned to the haplotype with the larger total.

\emph{Phase blocks.}  A tie beyond the last accepted edge opens a new block.  A tie within a block leaves the variant unphased, and the variant casts no votes.  A variant whose votes all come from SVs, beyond the last variant reached by a vote of a small variant, also opens a new block, so that an SV is never the only link between two blocks; SVs still vote and are phased within a block.  The position of the first variant of a block is its phase-set identifier.

\subsection*{Read-based correction and phasing entropy}
After the graph pass, \toolname checks the phase of each variant against the reads and gives each phased variant a measure of uncertainty.

\emph{Read-based correction.}  Each read is assigned to a haplotype by the weighted majority of its phased SNV, SV and indel alleles, with a lower weight for indels; 5mC observations are not used in this pass (Supplementary Fig.~12a,b).  A read is used only when its majority fraction exceeds $\theta_{\mathrm{read}}$ (default 0.65) and its evidence is sufficient.  For each variant, $n_1$ is the number of haplotype-1 reads with the reference allele plus haplotype-2 reads with the alternate allele, and $n_2$ is the converse.  The consistency of the reads is
\[
\rho=\frac{\max(n_1,n_2)}{n_1+n_2}.
\]
The majority pairing assigns the phase of the variant only when $\rho>\theta_{\mathrm{var}}$ (default 0.75); otherwise the variant is left unphased.

\emph{Phasing entropy.}  The phasing entropy of a phased variant measures how evenly its votes are divided between the two haplotypes (Supplementary Fig.~12c).  It is calculated from the weighted haplotype totals after the single-read guard:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PE}(v)=-\sum_{i\in\{1,2\}}p_i\log_2p_i,
\qquad p_i=\frac{h_i}{h_1+h_2}.
\end{equation}
PE is 0 when all votes support one haplotype and 1 bit when the support is equal.  When $h_1+h_2=0$, which occurs only at the first variant of a block, PE is set to 0 and the case is identified from the $h_1$ and $h_2$ fields.  PE, $h_1$ and $h_2$ are written to the VCF, and the accepted voting edges with their accumulated weights form the input graph of the GNN.

\subsection*{GNN-based detection of phase errors}
The GNN re-examines the regions in which the phasing graph is uncertain and identifies the variants at which a phase error probably starts; \toolname leaves these variants unphased (Supplementary Note~4).

\emph{Windows and features.}  A window is opened around every phased variant with $\mathrm{PE}\ge\theta_{\mathrm{PE}}$ (default 0.8) and holds the neighbouring variants on either side, over the merged SNV, indel, SV and 5mC records (Supplementary Figs.~13 and~14).  Each allele node carries 31 features in four groups: phasing evidence, such as phasing entropy and weighted haplotype totals; genomic context, such as homopolymer length, GC content and variant density; graph structure, such as out-degree and phase-set size; and four one-hot variant-class indicators.  Six edge features summarize vote weight, genomic distance, allele matching and phase-set relations (Supplementary Tables~13 and~14).

\emph{Architecture.}  The network has four GPS graph-transformer layers\cite{rampasek2022gps}, each combining a local four-head GATv2 branch\cite{velickovic2018gat,brody2022gatv2} and a global four-head self-attention branch\cite{vaswani2017attention}.  The local branch aggregates information along the edges of the phasing graph, and the global branch gives each node access to all nodes of the window, including those without a direct graph connection.  A two-layer classifier gives each allele node a score for the error class (Supplementary Table~15).  Inference is implemented in C++ without external runtime dependencies.

\emph{Training.}  The network was trained to identify phase-disagreement onsets: isolated disagreements with the benchmark and the first position of each longer run of disagreements.  Labels came from the v5.0q small-variant benchmark.  Within each window and phase set, the first benchmarked variant anchored the orientation, and later variants were labelled by their agreement with it.  Only benchmarked SNV and indel nodes had labels.  SV and 5mC nodes and variants absent from the benchmark were kept as graph context but did not contribute to the loss, so the unphasing of SVs and 5mC sites relies on the model trained on small variants.  Training windows came from the 60 HG002 nanopore co-phasing runs at 10--20$\times$: chr1--14 and chr18--20 were used for training and chr15--16 for validation, and chr17, chr21 and chr22 were excluded from model fitting and checkpoint selection.  The loss was a focal-weighted cross-entropy\cite{lin2017focal} on class-balanced minibatches, with haplotype-swap and coverage-scaling augmentations (Supplementary Table~16).  The checkpoint, the random seed and the deployment threshold were selected on the validation chromosomes.  The choice between two label definitions also used results on chr17, chr21 and chr22, so these chromosomes are not independent of that choice (Supplementary Note~4 and Supplementary Table~17).  The effect of GNN-based unphasing on these chromosomes is reported in Supplementary Note~7, Supplementary Fig.~15e and Supplementary Table~18.  After training, the model and its threshold were fixed and applied unchanged to all coverages and to PacBio HiFi data.

\emph{Selective unphasing.}  The two allele-node scores of a variant are averaged, and then averaged over the windows that score it.  A variant with a mean score of at least $\theta_{\mathrm{GNN}}$ (default 0.35) is unphased, with its genotype preserved; it is never flipped.  If this disconnects a block, the surviving connected components become separate phase sets, and a variant left alone in its phase set is also unphased.  Large deletions with unanimous votes keep their phase.  The scores are not calibrated probabilities.

\subsection*{Haplotagging}
\toolname assigns each read to a haplotype from the phased SNV, indel, SV and 5mC alleles that it carries (Supplementary Note~5).  SNV alleles are read from the aligned base; indel alleles are identified by allele length; SV alleles are re-derived from the CIGAR and not taken from the caller's read list; and a 5mC site gives evidence only to the reads that are methylated at it, matched by read name.  Each informative observation contributes one vote, whatever its class, in contrast to the class weights of read-based correction.  A read is assigned to the majority haplotype when the fraction of its votes for that haplotype reaches a threshold.  Reads with no informative variant, without this majority or with informative variants in more than one phase set are left untagged.  Tagged reads receive haplotype (HP), phase-set (PS) and Phred-scaled phasing-quality (PQ) tags.

\subsection*{Benchmark data and truth sets}
Evaluations used the human sample HG002 (NA24385), sequenced on two platforms and scored against two phased truth sets, and two further individuals, HG008-N and COLO829BL (Supplementary Notes~6 and~8).

\emph{Sequencing data.}  Nanopore R10.4.1 reads came from the Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) \texttt{giab\_lsk114\_2022.12} release (read N50 of 29~kb) and were aligned to the GRCh38 no-alt analysis set with minimap2.  PacBio HiFi (Revio) reads came from a 48$\times$ GIAB alignment, which was used as 50$\times$.  To evaluate each phaser across coverage, the nanopore alignment was down-sampled with samtools\cite{danecek2021samtools} to 10--60$\times$, with ten replicates at 10--20$\times$ and one at 30--60$\times$, and the HiFi alignment to 10--40$\times$.

\emph{Variant calls.}  Variants were called separately at every coverage and replicate.  SNVs and indels were called with PEPPER-Margin-DeepVariant r0.8 and SVs with Sniffles2 v2.8.0 on both platforms, and allele-specific 5mC sites with \toolname on the nanopore data (Supplementary Tables~19 and~20).

\emph{Truth sets.}  Phasing accuracy was scored on autosomes 1--22 against two HG002 truth sets.  GIAB v4.2.1\cite{wagner2022giab} carries phase transferred from a hifiasm assembly.  GIAB v5.0q\cite{olson2026giabv5} is derived from the diploid T2T-HG002 assembly\cite{hansen2026q100}, and every heterozygous variant in it is phased.  The truth VCFs were not restricted to their benchmark regions, and a call was scored only where the truth VCF holds the same REF and ALT alleles in a heterozygous genotype.  For region-specific results, a consecutive variant pair was assigned to a region when both variants lay in it; regions were defined by the two sets of benchmark regions and by the GIAB v3.6 genome stratifications\cite{dwarshuis2024stratifications}.

\subsection*{Comparison with other methods}
\toolname was compared with \whatshap~v2.8\cite{patterson2015whatshap}, \hapcut~v1.3.4\cite{edge2017hapcut2} and, on HiFi data, HiPhase v1.7.0\cite{holt2024hiphase}.  All tools received the same alignments and variant calls and ran on the same workstation (command options in Supplementary Note~6 and Supplementary Table~20).

\emph{Sequence variants.}  Cross-tool SNV comparisons used SNV-only phasing.  HiPhase has no SNV-only mode, so its SNV-only runs used call sets restricted to SNVs.  Indel co-phasing was compared with the indel phasing of \whatshap and HiPhase and, in replicate~1, of \hapcut (Supplementary Table~4).  \toolname was evaluated on the seven variant-class combinations of Supplementary Table~6, with and without GNN-based unphasing.  The GNN weights, trained on nanopore data, were used unchanged on HiFi data.

\emph{Methylation.}  MethPhaser\cite{fu2024methphaser} uses methylation after phasing, to bridge the blocks of an existing SNV phasing.  To compare the use of methylation on the same input phasing, MethPhaser was given the SNV-only output of \toolname without GNN-based unphasing, instead of the \whatshap or \hapcut phasing that its authors recommend (configuration in Supplementary Table~20).  Both analyses used the same read alignments and SNV calls.  \toolname phased SNVs and 5mC jointly in a new graph and did not reuse the SNV-only phasing.  Without GNN-based unphasing, differences between the two tools therefore reflect how methylation is used: after phasing, to bridge existing blocks (MethPhaser), or during phasing, as additional graph evidence (\toolname).  With GNN-based unphasing, the differences also include its effect.

\subsection*{Evaluation metrics}
Phasing was evaluated by its accuracy (switch error rate and Hamming distance), its completeness (recall) and its contiguity (block N50), computed with \texttt{longphase compare} (Supplementary Fig.~16 and Supplementary Note~6).

\emph{Small variants.}  Heterozygous query and truth records were intersected by position and REF/ALT alleles, and the intersection was partitioned into blocks phased in both files.  The switch error rate was the number of switch errors divided by the number of assessed consecutive SNV pairs.  Hamming distance was the block-wise minimum over the two orientations, divided by the number of compared variants.  Because Hamming distance includes indels when they are present in both files and tends to increase with block length, configurations that differ in variant classes or block length were compared by SNV switch error rate.  Recall (phasing completeness) was the fraction of truth heterozygous variants of a class that matched a phased heterozygous query call, regardless of whether its phase was correct.  Block N50 was the span-weighted median of the spans of query phase sets with at least two variants, pooled over chromosomes.  A switch error was shared between tools when it lay at the same pair of SNV positions under the same truth set.  For each co-phasing configuration, \emph{blocks joined} was the net decrease in block count relative to SNV-only phasing in the same replicate and at the same GNN setting.

\emph{Structural variants.}  SV phase was evaluated against haplotype-resolved variants called by dipcall from the T2T-HG002 Q100 v1.1 assembly\cite{li2018dipcall,hansen2026q100}, for heterozygous deletions and insertions of at least 50~bp in replicate~1.  \whatshap and HiPhase read SVs only as sequence-resolved records, so the SV calls were converted to that form for these tools; \toolname received the unmodified calls.  A call was matched to a truth SV of the same type with size similarity of at least 70\% and a start position within 1~kb or, for deletions, reciprocal overlap of at least 50\%.  Each block was oriented by the majority phase agreement of its SNVs with the truth, and an SV matched to a heterozygous truth SV was correct when its oriented phase agreed with the assembly.  The SV phasing error rate was the number of incorrectly phased SVs divided by the number of correctly and incorrectly phased SVs.  Phased SVs matched to a homozygous truth SV were counted separately as genotype errors.

\subsection*{Analysis of unphased SNVs}
Two analyses characterized the SNV calls that \toolname leaves unphased: a comparison with the calls that \whatshap phases, and a classification of the calls that the GNN unphases (Supplementary Note~6).

\emph{Comparison with \whatshap.}  \toolname and \whatshap were compared on the same biallelic heterozygous SNV calls at 10, 30 and 60$\times$ (replicate~1).  Calls were grouped by whether they were phased by both tools, by one tool only or by neither, and each call was classified against the v5.0q VCF as heterozygous with the same alleles, heterozygous with other alleles, homozygous or absent.  Phased calls at positions with a homozygous v5.0q record were counted as genotype errors (Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}c).  Precision was the fraction of phased heterozygous SNV calls on chr1--22 that matched a benchmark heterozygous SNV, and F1 was the harmonic mean of precision and recall.  Precision here is a benchmark-match fraction: an unmatched call outside the benchmark regions is unclassified, not a confirmed error, and neither precision nor recall requires the matched call to have the correct phase.  For benchmark-matched calls phased by one tool only, phase errors were scored as in \texttt{compare}, and the fraction of these calls inside that tool's switch-error intervals was compared with the fraction among all calls phased by that tool.  Read depth was the \texttt{FORMAT/DP} value of the variant caller.

\emph{SNVs unphased by the GNN.}  An SNV was counted as unphased by the GNN when it was phased before this step and unphased after it.  This is the subset described as left unphased at high phasing uncertainty.  These SNVs were classified against the v5.0q VCF (Supplementary Fig.~6c) and, at 60$\times$, against the one-to-one alignment of the T2T-HG002 assembly, with a random sample of 50,000 SNVs phased before correction as the background.

\subsection*{Runtime and memory}
Wall-clock time, CPU time and peak resident memory of SNV-only phasing were measured on one workstation (AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, 16 cores, 249~GB of memory, Ubuntu 24.04, input and output on an NVMe solid-state drive), one run at a time with the page cache cleared before each run.  \toolname was run with 24 threads and on one thread; \whatshap and \hapcut have no thread option and ran on one thread (Supplementary Table~11).

\subsection*{Statistics and reproducibility}
Ten nanopore down-sampling replicates at 10--20$\times$ were summarized by their mean and standard deviation, while 30--60$\times$ and HiFi used one down-sampling per coverage.  The HG008-N analysis used three down-sampling replicates at 10$\times$ and at 20$\times$, summarized by their mean, and one at 30$\times$; the COLO829BL analysis used one down-sampling per coverage (Supplementary Fig.~17).  The SV evaluation, MethPhaser comparison, runs of \hapcut with indels, and runtime measurements used replicate~1 only. Each runtime configuration was timed once. Call composition, phase errors among calls phased by one tool only, switch-error interval enrichment, read depth and assembly-alignment status were analysed using replicate~1. Precision and F1 were calculated for all SNV-only runs.

Subsamples for each platform were drawn from the same source sequencing data set of HG002. The reported standard deviations therefore describe variation due to read subsampling, conditional on that data set, rather than variation among independent sequencing experiments. Subsampling variability was not estimated at 30--60$\times$ or for HiFi. Tools compared at each coverage and replicate used the same sampled reads, so fold differences between tools and the effect of GNN-based unphasing at 10--20$\times$ were also computed within each replicate (Supplementary Table~1).  No statistical tests, sample-size calculation, randomization or blinding were used.  GNN inference used fixed model weights and no sampling.

The GIAB FTP site provides the HG002 v4.2.1 phase-transfer VCF (\url{https://ftp-trace.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/giab/ftp/release/AshkenazimTrio/HG002_NA24385_son/NISTv4.2.1/GRCh38/SupplementaryFiles/}) and the v5.0q benchmark (\url{https://ftp-trace.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/giab/ftp/release/AshkenazimTrio/HG002_NA24385_son/v5.0q/}). It also provides the dipcall variant calls and one-to-one alignment regions of the T2T-HG002 Q100 v1.1 assembly on GRCh38 (\url{https://ftp-trace.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/giab/ftp/release/AshkenazimTrio/HG002_NA24385_son/v5.0q/dipcall_output/}) and the HG002 PacBio HiFi Revio alignment (\url{https://ftp-trace.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/giab/ftp/data/AshkenazimTrio/HG002_NA24385_son/PacBio_HiFi-Revio_20231031/}).

The HG002 nanopore R10.4.1 reads and alignments are available from the ONT open-data archive (\texttt{s3://ont-open-data/giab\_lsk114\_2022.12/}) under a CC BY-NC 4.0 licence. The files used are listed in Supplementary Table~20.

The HG008-N nanopore reads are available from the GIAB FTP site (\url{https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/ReferenceSamples/giab/data_somatic/HG008/Liss_lab/Northeastern_ONT-std_20240422/}), and the HG008 normal assembly (v6.3) from the GIAB tumour--normal analysis archive (\url{https://nist-giab.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/giab_tumor-normal/analysis/HG008/NIST_asm_dev/HG008N_v6.3/}).  The COLO829BL nanopore alignment is available from the Oxford Nanopore open-data archive (\texttt{s3://ont-open-data/colo829\_2024.03/basecalls/colo829bl/sup/PAU59807.d052sup4305mCG\_5hmCGvHg38.bam}), and the COLO829BL diploid assembly v3.0.0 (haplotype files SMASFTH1B2J6 and SMASFK4V33V8, open access) from the SMaHT data portal (\url{https://data.smaht.org}).
