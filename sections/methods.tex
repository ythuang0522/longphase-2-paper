\subsection*{Overview of \toolname}
\toolname is a diploid germline phaser that takes long-read alignments, a reference genome and heterozygous variant calls, and returns phased VCFs and haplotagged alignments (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).  SNVs, small indels, structural variants (SVs) and allele-specific 5-methylcytosine (5mC) observations are represented together in one weighted graph per chromosome.  Initial haplotypes are resolved from weighted multi-neighbour votes, reads are used for a second phase-consistency pass, and a graph neural network (GNN) then scores candidate phase-disagreement onsets.  Selected variants are unphased, never flipped; if this disconnects a haplotype block, the surviving components receive updated phase-set identifiers.  The detailed module rules are given in Supplementary Methods~1--6.  The method extends the graph formulation of \longphase~1\cite{lin2022longphase}.

\subsection*{Input variant calls}
Heterozygous SNVs and small indels were taken from PEPPER-Margin-DeepVariant\cite{shafin2021margin}; homozygous and filtered records were carried through unchanged but did not enter the graph.  Indels in short tandem repeats were retained as evidence but down-weighted, because a misplaced repeat indel can link the wrong alleles.  SVs were taken from Sniffles2\cite{smolka2024sniffles2} or cuteSV\cite{jiang2020cutesv} with supporting read names; homozygous SVs, duplicated positions and SVs coincident with SNVs were excluded.  The exact eligibility and repeat annotation rules are in Supplementary Method~2.

\subsection*{Calling allele-specific methylation}
Modification probabilities were read from the \texttt{MM}/\texttt{ML} tags produced by Dorado\cite{ont2025dorado} and carried through alignment.  A CpG was called methylated when its 5mC probability was at least 0.8, unmethylated when it was at most 0.2, and ambiguous otherwise.  Forward- and reverse-strand observations were mapped to one CpG coordinate.  A site was retained as a candidate allele-specific site when methylated and unmethylated reads were sufficiently balanced and the ambiguous fraction was small:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\min(M_R,U_R)}{\max(M_R,U_R)} \ge 0.6,
\qquad
\frac{A_R}{M_R+U_R+A_R} \le 0.2,
\label{eq:hetmeth}
\end{equation}
where $M_R$, $U_R$ and $A_R$ are the three read counts.  Candidates were then required to co-segregate with a neighbouring heterozygous marker.  For a pair of sites, writing $RR$, $RA$, $AR$ and $AA$ for the four observed state combinations, linkage required
\begin{equation}
\frac{\max(RR+AA,\;RA+AR)}{RR+AA+RA+AR} \ge 0.9.
\label{eq:linkmeth}
\end{equation}
The same criterion was used for SNV-anchored and methylation-only linking.  Only CpG dinucleotides were considered.  Weak candidates were admitted in a later expansion pass, and consecutive CpGs were merged into one locus with a shared read list for both strands.  The resulting methylated and unmethylated read names and depths were supplied to the phasing graph.  The depth bound and expansion rules are in Supplementary Method~1.

\subsection*{Read-level allele observations and filtering}
Alignments with mapping quality below 1, unmapped, secondary or duplicate records were excluded.  SNV alleles came from the aligned base and its Phred quality; indel alleles from the CIGAR; SV alleles from a matching CIGAR event, the junction of a split alignment or the caller's read list; and 5mC alleles from the methylation read lists.  Supplementary alignments were retained as evidence, so when two alignments of the same read overlapped by more than 20\% of their combined span, the shorter alignment was discarded.  Under the nanopore setting, a downstream SNV was removed when two SNVs at most 2~bp apart both lay in homopolymers of length at least three.  A copy-number-aware filter identified intervals bounded by excess front- or back-clipping and removed variants whose alternate-supporting reads had an unusually high mismatch load relative to reference-supporting reads.  Specifically, a read's mismatch load $R_m$ counted the heterozygous sites in the interval at which it carried the alternate allele, and each variant was scored by
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MR}(v) = \frac{\bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{ALT}}}{\bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{REF}} + \bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{ALT}}},
\label{eq:cnvmr}
\end{equation}
where the barred terms are the mean loads of reads supporting the ALT and REF alleles of $v$. Sites with $\mathrm{MR}(v)\ge 0.7$ were removed from all reads.  The three-state interval scanner and its constants are specified in Supplementary Method~2.

\subsection*{Weighted phasing graph}
Each heterozygous variant contributed reference and alternate allele nodes.  For each read, every allele was connected to the next 35 observed variants, with no edge-distance cap. Variants followed by a gap of more than 300~kb to the next heterozygous variant were skipped during haplotype resolution, casting no votes and receiving no haplotype or entropy.  A read contributed weight 1 when both observations had quality at least 12 and weight 0.1 otherwise.  Indel and 5mC observations and SV alternate observations were assigned fixed quality 60; reference-allele SV observations were assigned 30.  Thus all such observations have full weight at the default threshold, while reference-SV evidence is down-weighted only at a higher threshold.

For a variant pair $(u,v)$, the four allele-pair supports were accumulated as $w_{rr}$, $w_{ra}$, $w_{ar}$ and $w_{aa}$.  The two possible pairings have support $P=w_{rr}+w_{aa}$ and $Q=w_{ra}+w_{ar}$, and their ambiguity is $s=\min(P,Q)/\max(P,Q)$.

\subsection*{Haplotype resolution by multi-neighbour voting}
Variants were processed in coordinate order.  Each variant cast a vote to each downstream neighbour for the pairing with larger support, except when $s>0.7$; SNV--5mC pairs used a 0.3 threshold and required at least one read-equivalent of support.  Votes had weight 1 by default, 20 for near-unanimous support ($s\le0.1$ with total support at least 1, or one pairing below 1 while the other was at least 1), and 0.1 when the voting variant was a tandem-repeat indel.  The vote was translated using the already assigned haplotype of the upstream variant, producing totals $h_1(v)$ and $h_2(v)$.  When more than three votes had support of at most 1 read-equivalent, and at least one qualifying vote remained, only votes with $s<0.2$, weight at least 1 and a non-indel voting variant were retained.  The larger total assigned the haplotype.  A tie beyond the last accepted edge opened a new block; a tie within an existing block left the variant unphased and cast no votes.  A variant whose votes all came from SVs, beyond the last variant reached by a vote of a small variant, also opened a new block, so that an SV was never the only link between two blocks; SVs still voted and were phased within a block.  The first variant position defined the phase-set identifier.  The constants were fixed by a pre-study sweep on HG002 at 10--60$\times$ and were not re-tuned for the reported experiments; the parameter tuning is described in Supplementary Method~3.

\subsection*{Read-based correction and phasing entropy}
After the graph assignment, each read was assigned to a haplotype by a weighted majority of its phased SNV and SV alleles (weight 1), indel alleles (weight 0.1) and 5mC alleles (not used for this pass).  Reads were retained only with a majority above 0.65 and total evidence above 1.  For each variant, the consistency of the two read populations was
\[
\rho=\frac{\max(n_1,n_2)}{n_1+n_2},
\]
where $n_1$ counts haplotype-1 reads carrying REF plus haplotype-2 reads carrying ALT, and $n_2$ counts the converse. The majority pairing assigned the phase only when $\rho>0.75$; otherwise the variant was left unphased.

For each phased variant, phasing entropy was calculated from the weighted haplotype totals after the single-read guard:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PE}(v)=-\sum_{i\in\{1,2\}}p_i\log_2p_i,
\qquad p_i=\frac{h_i}{h_1+h_2}.
\end{equation}
It ranges from 0 for unanimous support to 1 bit for equal support.  When $h_1+h_2=0$, PE was set to 0 and the case was identified from the accompanying $h_1,h_2$ fields; this occurs only at the first variant of a block.  PE, $h_1$ and $h_2$ were written to the VCF, and accepted voting edges with their accumulated weights formed the GNN input graph.

\subsection*{Graph neural network for phase-error detection}
Phasing entropy was used to select candidate windows for refinement. Within each window, the GNN combined phasing evidence, genomic context and graph structure to identify phase-disagreement onsets for selective unphasing, as defined by the training labels. Positive targets include isolated disagreements and the first positions of consecutive disagreement runs. The resulting onset scores are not calibrated probabilities.

The GNN operated on merged SNV/indel, SV and 5mC records. A window of at most 41 variants (82 allele nodes) was opened around every phased variant with $\mathrm{PE}\ge0.8$, extending 20 variants on either side.

Each allele node carried 31 features in four groups: phasing evidence ($\mathrm{PE}$, the genotype and allele indices, the trigger and phase-set indicators, the signed offset from the trigger normalized by the largest absolute offset in the window, the two weighted haplotype totals $h_1$ and $h_2$, and six transforms of them); genomic context, derived from the reference sequence and input variant positions (homopolymer length, GC content, tandem-repeat unit count, local sequence entropy, variant density and distance to the nearest indel); graph structure (out-degree, phase-set size, log offset from the trigger, dispersion of the outgoing edge weights and a bridge-vertex indicator); and four one-hot variant-class indicators. Six edge features summarized vote weight, genomic distance, allele matching and phase-set relations. Edges were restricted to the window, symmetrized and supplemented with mean-feature self-loops. Exact definitions and normalizations are in Supplementary Tables~1--2.

The network used four GPS graph-transformer layers\cite{rampasek2022gps}, each combining a local four-head GATv2 branch\cite{velickovic2018gat,brody2022gatv2} and a global four-head self-attention branch\cite{vaswani2017attention}, followed by residual normalization and a 128--256--128 feed-forward block. The local GATv2 branch aggregates information along edges of the phasing graph, using edge features to weight the contributions of neighbouring allele nodes. The global self-attention branch allows each node to access information from all nodes within the same window, including those without a direct graph connection. A two-layer classifier produced the two-class softmax output. Architecture and inference details are in Supplementary Method~4, Table~3 and Supplementary Fig.~6.

Only variants no further from the trigger than half the largest trigger distance in the window were scored; the same rule defined the nodes used for training loss. Nodes outside that zone supplied context only. Because the zone is defined on genomic distance and not on variant index, the number of variants it holds depends on the local spacing. The two allele-node scores were averaged, followed by averaging across the windows in which the variant lay inside the scoring zone.

A variant with mean score at least 0.35 was unphased while preserving its genotype. Bridge protection was disabled. If removing these variants disconnected a block, the surviving connected components became separate phase sets (Supplementary Method~4).

Training windows came from the same HG002 nanopore co-phasing runs used for evaluation at 10--20$\times$ (six coverages and ten down-sampling replicates, 60 runs).  Of 722,116 windows, chr1--14 and chr18--20 supplied training (602,788), chr15--16 validation (67,880), and chr17, chr21 and chr22 testing (51,448); the chromosome sets were disjoint.  No test-chromosome window entered gradient updates, batch-normalization statistics or checkpoint selection.  Windows contained all four variant classes, but only benchmarked SNV and indel nodes had labels, and SV and 5mC correction relies on the model trained on those labelled small variants.  The training and evaluation truth was the v5.0q small-variant benchmark.  Training windows were limited to 10--20$\times$, whereas results also included 30--60$\times$ and PacBio HiFi data.

Labels described local phase orientation.  Within each window and phase set, the first benchmarked variant anchored the orientation; later disagreements were labelled as a single-variant flip or unphase event, or as a break at the first site of a longer run.  Benchmark-absent variants and SV/5mC records were masked from the loss but retained as graph context.  The released model was trained and applied in binary mode. The binary classifier merged the flip, unphase and break labels into one error class; predicted targets were subsequently unphased.  Error labels accounted for 2.96\% of labelled central nodes in the training split.  Correct nodes were sampled independently in each minibatch at a ratio of at most four per error node, and the resulting cross-entropy used focal weighting\cite{lin2017focal} with $\gamma=1.5$ and label smoothing 0.05.  Training used haplotype-swap and coverage-scaling augmentations; after a swap, every edge took the vote weight of the allele node it then left. The optimizer, stopping rule and environment are listed in Supplementary Table~4.

We used a normalized net-gain criterion for checkpoint selection, assigning unit benefit to each true-positive detection of a label-defined correction target and unit cost to each false-positive prediction. Checkpoints were selected on chr15--16 by maximizing
\begin{equation}
S=R\left(2-\frac{1}{P}\right)
\end{equation}
over 81 score thresholds from 0.01 to 0.99, subject to $R\ge0.005$, where $P$ and $R$ are precision and recall for the label-defined error class. This expression applies for $P>0.5$; otherwise the score was $P-1$. The criterion serves as a proxy for correction utility (Supplementary Method~4). Checkpoint details and classification metrics for validation and held-out chromosomes are reported in Supplementary Table~4.

Two label definitions were trained, each with five random seeds (42, 43, 44, 1337 and 3407): benchmark variants matched to the calls by position, and matched by position, REF and ALT, which leaves calls whose alleles differ from the benchmark unlabelled. All other settings were identical. Each of the ten models was applied to the 60 HG002 nanopore SNV-only runs at 10--20$\times$, and its deployment threshold $B$ was set on the validation chromosomes to the same cost of correction, 6.35 phased SNVs withheld per removed switch error, that of the model of the earlier release v2.1 at $B=0.30$. At that cost, the position-matched labels removed 23.0\% of switch errors on the held-out chromosomes (mean of five seeds; range 22.4--23.9\%) and the REF/ALT-matched labels 21.6\% (20.8--22.9\%), although the latter had higher held-out classification scores (Supplementary Table~18). The position-matched labels were therefore kept, and the released model is the one that removed the most switch errors on the validation chromosomes (seed 42). The threshold selected on the validation chromosomes was 0.34 and was rounded to $B=0.35$ for deployment. In the released implementation, it removed 34.3\% of switch errors at 7.98 withheld SNVs per removed error over the whole genome, and 23.5\% at 9.26 on the held-out chromosomes (Supplementary Method~4 and Supplementary Table~15).

Inference was implemented in C++ without external runtime dependencies; the export procedure and numerical agreement checks are described in Supplementary Method~4.

\subsection*{Haplotagging}
Reads were tagged from the phased SNV, indel, SV and 5mC alleles they carried.  Each informative observation contributed one vote, and a read was assigned to the majority haplotype when its fraction was at least 0.60; reads whose informative variants belonged to multiple phase sets or lacked a majority were left untagged.  Tagged reads received HP, PS and phasing-quality fields.  SV alleles were re-derived from the CIGAR and 5mC evidence was matched by read name.  Full evidence and quality definitions are in Supplementary Method~5.

\subsection*{Benchmark data}
The benchmark used HG002 (NA24385) Oxford Nanopore R10.4.1 reads from the ONT \texttt{giab\_lsk114\_2022.12} release: two PromethION flow cells (PAG65784 and PAG68757), SQK-LSK114, 400 bases per second and 4-kHz sampling, basecalled with Dorado \texttt{dna\_r10.4.1\_e8.2\_400bps\_sup@v4.0.0} and \texttt{5mCG\_5hmCG@v2}; their read N50 values were 29.2 and 29.4~kb.  Reads were aligned to the GRCh38 no-alt analysis set by Oxford Nanopore with minimap2 v2.24 (\texttt{-y -ax map-ont}), retaining reads with mean base quality $\ge 10$.  The 60$\times$ data set is a random 91.14\% subset of the 70.4$\times$ alignment (samtools v1.15, \texttt{view -s 0.9114}), and the 10--50$\times$ data sets were down-sampled from it with samtools v1.23.1\cite{danecek2021samtools} \texttt{view -s} \textit{seed.fraction}.  Ten replicates were used at 10--20$\times$ and one at 30--60$\times$; variants were called separately at every coverage and replicate.  SNVs and indels came from PEPPER-Margin-DeepVariant r0.8, which reports unphased genotypes, SVs from Sniffles2 v2.8.0, and allele-specific 5mC sites from \toolname.
The seeds were 1--9 and 867 at 10$\times$ (fraction 0.1666), 12--21 at 12$\times$ (0.200), 22--31 at 14$\times$ (0.233), 32--41 at 16$\times$ (0.266), 42--51 at 18$\times$ (0.300), 1--10 at 20$\times$ (0.3333), and 42 at 30, 40 and 50$\times$ (0.5, 0.6667 and 0.8333).  PEPPER-Margin-DeepVariant used the \texttt{--ont\_r10\_q20} model for nanopore data and \texttt{--hifi} for HiFi data.
HG002 PacBio HiFi (Revio) reads were taken from a 48$\times$ GIAB alignment (Supplementary Table~5), which was used as 50$\times$; 10--40$\times$ data sets were down-sampled from it with samtools v1.23.1 \texttt{view --subsample} (seed 1; fractions 0.2083, 0.4167, 0.6250 and 0.8333), and variants were called separately at every coverage.

Accuracy was scored with \texttt{longphase compare} on autosomes 1--22 against two HG002 truth sets: GIAB v4.2.1\cite{wagner2022giab}, in the version with phase transferred from a hifiasm assembly (the v4.2.1 benchmark VCF itself is unphased), restricted to biallelic heterozygous records; and GIAB v5.0q\cite{olson2026giabv5}, derived from the diploid T2T-HG002 assembly\cite{hansen2026q100}, in which every heterozygous variant is phased.  The truth VCFs were not restricted to their benchmark regions, and a call was scored only where the truth VCF holds the same REF and ALT alleles in a heterozygous genotype.  Both truth VCFs list heterozygous variants outside their benchmark regions (9.3\% of the autosomal heterozygous records of v5.0q and 3.4\% of v4.2.1), so the error counts include sites that the benchmark regions do not cover, among them satellite and segmental-duplication sequence that the T2T-HG002 assembly resolves\cite{hansen2026q100}.  To locate switch errors and to score indels, every consecutive variant pair in an intersected block was recorded with a re-implementation of \texttt{longphase compare}, which reproduced its counts exactly in all 1,024 scored runs.  A pair was assigned to a region when both variants lay in it; regions were the intersection and the differences of the two sets of benchmark regions, and the GIAB v3.6 genome stratifications\cite{dwarshuis2024stratifications}.  Indel switch errors were counted over consecutive pairs that included at least one indel, and the indel Hamming distance was computed in the block orientation chosen on all variants.

\subsection*{Comparison with other methods}
\whatshap~v2.8\cite{patterson2015whatshap} and \hapcut~v1.3.4\cite{edge2017hapcut2} were run on the same alignments and variant calls; cross-tool SNV comparisons used SNV-only phasing, with indel phasing disabled in \whatshap.  \longphase~1.0\cite{lin2022longphase} and \toolname were run with the nanopore setting, and GNN correction used PE trigger 0.8, score threshold 0.35, no bridge protection and block splitting.  Co-phasing experiments separately evaluated the seven variant-class combinations listed in Supplementary Table~10, with and without correction.  Indel co-phasing was also compared with \whatshap's default indel phasing.  Margin v2.3.1\cite{shafin2021margin} and Ralphi\cite{battistella2025ralphi} were evaluated at 10--20$\times$, with ten down-sampling replicates per coverage; these tools were not evaluated at 30--60$\times$. Their configurations and that of MethPhaser\cite{fu2024methphaser} are listed in Supplementary Table~5.  MethPhaser was given the SNV-only output of \toolname without GNN correction instead of the \whatshap or \hapcut phasing its authors recommend as input. MethPhaser and the joint SNV and 5mC phasing of \toolname started from the same SNV phasing. Without GNN correction, differences between them reflect how methylation is used, after phasing to bridge existing blocks (MethPhaser) or during phasing as additional graph evidence (\toolname); with GNN correction, they also include its effect.  GCphase\cite{luo2024gcphase} was excluded from the genome-wide comparison because it ignored the FILTER field and phased homozygous-reference calls as heterozygous variants when given the full input VCF (about 1.2 million such calls in the 10$\times$ replicate-1 output). GCphase was not evaluated with input restricted to heterozygous SNVs.  The HiFi call sets were phased SNV-only with \toolname (\texttt{--pb}), with and without correction, with \whatshap, with \hapcut and with HiPhase (see below); the GNN weights were trained on nanopore data.  Four further phasers were run on replicate~1, at 10--60$\times$ in steps of 10$\times$ on nanopore data and at 10--50$\times$ on HiFi data, with default parameters or the workflow their documentation describes (Supplementary Table~5).  On nanopore data, HapBridge\cite{chen2025hapbridge} was given the same input as MethPhaser, and LongHap\cite{pfennig2026longhap} phased SNVs with and without 5mC and, from the same reads, SNVs, indels and SVs with 5mC; because LongHap requires sequence-resolved alleles, Sniffles2 deletions and insertions were given to it with their allele sequences, and inversions, duplications and breakends were omitted.  TinkerHap\cite{hartmann2025tinkerhap} was run on both platforms and HiPhase\cite{holt2024hiphase} on HiFi data.  Both phase indels by default and have no SNV-only mode, so each was run on the full call sets and on the call sets restricted to SNVs, the variants that \whatshap phases with \texttt{--only-snvs}.  For the comparison with indels, \toolname, \whatshap and \hapcut were also run with indel phasing on HiFi data, and \hapcut on nanopore data.  These runs were scored in the same way as all others (Supplementary Table~18).
\whatshap was run as \texttt{whatshap phase --ignore-read-groups --reference} with \texttt{--only-snvs} for SNV comparisons, and \hapcut as \texttt{extractHAIRS --ont 1} (\texttt{--pacbio 1} for HiFi) followed by \texttt{HAPCUT2} with default parameters, including its default likelihood-based pruning, on the PASS heterozygous SNVs.  \hapcut rejects genotypes other than two alleles from 0, 1 and 2, so the HiFi call sets given to it omitted such records (134--204 uncalled reference candidates per coverage and one multi-allelic indel at 50$\times$); none of them was a heterozygous SNV.  \longphase~1.0 was re-run on every down-sampling replicate.  All tools ran on the same workstation.

\subsection*{Evaluation metrics}
Metrics (Supplementary Fig.~8) were computed by intersecting heterozygous query and truth records by position and REF/ALT alleles, then partitioning the intersection into blocks phased in both files; singleton blocks were ignored.  Within each block, switch errors were the Hamming distance between truth and query switch-encoded haplotype strings, and the switch-error rate divided their number by the assessed consecutive SNV pairs.  Isolated switches and two-consecutive-switch flips were reported separately.  Hamming distance was the block-wise minimum over the two orientations, divided by the number of compared variants; unlike switch rate, it included indels when present in both files, so it is not comparable between configurations with and without indels, which we therefore compared by SNV switch error rate.  Because a longer block can contain a longer segment of wrong orientation, block-wise Hamming distance tends to increase with block length, so it does not compare tools with different block lengths on equal terms.  Lower Hamming distances were also observed when \toolname produced longer blocks than \whatshap.  In SNV-only phasing at 30--60$\times$, \toolname had 2--5\% longer blocks than \whatshap and 1.4--1.8-fold lower Hamming distances.  In joint SNV and indel phasing, the blocks of \whatshap were 12--33\% longer, but its indel Hamming distance was 1.8--2.7-fold higher, and this difference increased with coverage as the difference in block length decreased.  MethPhaser lengthens its input blocks by joining them without changing phase within them; these joins increased the Hamming distance 1.8--2.8-fold over that of its input at 20--60$\times$.  Phased fraction was the number of truth heterozygous SNVs or indels that were phased by the query divided by all truth heterozygous variants of that class.  Block N50 was the span-weighted median of query phase-set spans of at least two variants, pooled over chromosomes.  Switch-error positions were exported for the unphased-SNV analysis.  The exact block and denominator definitions are in Supplementary Method~6.

\subsection*{Analysis of unphased SNVs}
An SNV was counted as unphased by GNN correction when it was phased in the pre-correction output and unphased afterwards.  Such sites were classified against the v5.0q benchmark VCF, matched by chromosome and position and without a benchmark BED, as absent, homozygous or heterozygous (Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}f); the heterozygous count was also reported with matching REF and ALT alleles, the rule used by \texttt{compare}.  The analysis used the 10--60$\times$ outputs of replicate~1 with the final GNN model.

At 60$\times$, SNVs unphased by GNN correction were also classified against the T2T-HG002 assembly projected to GRCh38 as outside the 1:1 assembly BED, inside without an assembly variant at the same start coordinate, or inside with one; alleles were not compared.  All chromosomes, including chrX and chrY, were analysed, and autosome-only values were derived from the per-chromosome counts.  A random sample of 50,000 SNVs phased before correction, which included sites that GNN correction later unphased, provided the background; enrichment was summarized by chromosome and 1-Mb bins.

To compare \toolname and \whatshap, the heterozygous SNV calls with single-base alleles and genotype 0/1, which were identical in the two output VCFs, were grouped by the tool that phased them: both tools, \toolname only, \whatshap only or neither; calls phased by \whatshap only were further split by whether \toolname had already left them unphased before GNN correction. Each call was classified against the v5.0q chr1--22 VCF, without a benchmark BED, as heterozygous with the same position and alleles, heterozygous with other alleles, homozygous or absent; calls on chrX and chrY were counted separately. The calls phased by a tool were those phased by both tools plus those phased by that tool only; phased calls at positions with a homozygous v5.0q record were counted as genotype errors (Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}e). For every SNV-only run of both tools, and on HiFi data also of HiPhase, the share of the phased heterozygous SNV calls on chr1--22 that matched a benchmark heterozygous SNV (precision) was computed from the \texttt{compare} count of matched calls, and the F1 score as the harmonic mean of precision and the phased fraction. For the matched calls phased by one tool only, the phase assigned by that tool was scored as in \texttt{compare}, and a call was counted as wrongly phased when its phase was opposite to the majority orientation of its block relative to the truth. A switch-error interval extends from the first SNV of a switch-error pair to the base before the second; the share of the calls phased by one tool only inside that tool's intervals was compared with the share of all heterozygous SNV calls phased by that tool. Calls were also annotated by read depth, by the v5.0q benchmark regions, by GIAB v3.6 segmental duplications and by the one-to-one dipcall alignment (Supplementary Data~1).

\subsection*{Runtime and memory}
Wall-clock time, CPU time and peak resident memory were measured with \texttt{/usr/bin/time}; reported \toolname times included alignment reading, graph construction, phasing, GNN correction and VCF writing, and excluded alignment, variant calling and down-sampling.  All runs used one workstation (AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, 16 cores and 32 threads, 249~GB of memory, Ubuntu 24.04, input and output on a hard-disk drive).  \toolname was compiled with g++ 13.3 at \texttt{-O3}.  Times were taken once per coverage, on replicate~1 at 10--20$\times$ and on the single data sets at 30--60$\times$.  \whatshap and \hapcut ran on one thread and were timed in the runs that produced the phased VCFs.  \toolname was timed with 24 threads at every coverage and on one thread at 10 and 60$\times$, in separate runs made one at a time with no other jobs running; each produced output identical to the reported VCF.  The logs of the \hapcut runs at 30 and 40$\times$ were not retained, so these two runs were repeated with the same commands and inputs, one at a time with no other jobs running; their phased output was identical to that of the original runs.  On HiFi data (10--50$\times$, one run per coverage), \toolname was timed in the same way with 24 threads, and \hapcut in the runs that produced its output.  The \whatshap runs at 10 and 20$\times$ had shared the workstation with other jobs; they were therefore repeated one at a time with no other jobs running, each after reading its alignment file once, and each produced output identical to the original run.  \whatshap and \hapcut have no thread option; running them on each chromosome in parallel would shorten their wall-clock time but not their CPU time, so CPU time is reported next to wall-clock time (Supplementary Fig.~10 and Supplementary Table~16).

\subsection*{Statistics and reproducibility}
Ten nanopore down-sampling replicates at 10--20$\times$ were summarized by their mean and standard deviation, while 30--60$\times$ and HiFi used one down-sampling per coverage.  Subsamples for each platform were drawn from the same source sequencing data set of HG002. The reported standard deviations therefore describe variation due to read subsampling, conditional on that data set, rather than variation among independent sequencing experiments. Subsampling variability was not estimated at 30--60$\times$ or for HiFi. Tools compared at each coverage and replicate used the same sampled reads, so fold differences between tools and the effect of GNN correction at 10--20$\times$ were also computed within each replicate (Supplementary Table~8).  No statistical tests, sample-size calculation, randomization or blinding were used.  GNN inference used fixed model weights and no sampling.
