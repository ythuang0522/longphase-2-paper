\subsection*{Overview of \toolname}
\toolname is a diploid germline phaser that takes long-read alignments, a reference genome and heterozygous variant calls, and returns phased VCFs and haplotagged alignments (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).  SNVs, small indels, structural variants (SVs) and allele-specific 5-methylcytosine (5mC) observations are represented together in one weighted graph per chromosome.  Initial haplotypes are resolved from weighted multi-neighbour votes, reads are used for a second phase-consistency pass, and a graph neural network (GNN) then scores candidate phase-disagreement onsets.  Selected variants are unphased, never flipped; if this disconnects a haplotype block, the surviving components receive updated phase-set identifiers.  The detailed module rules are given in Supplementary Methods~1--6.  The method extends the graph formulation of \longphase~1\cite{lin2022longphase}.

\subsection*{Input variant calls}
Heterozygous SNVs and small indels were taken from PEPPER-Margin-DeepVariant\cite{shafin2021margin}; homozygous and filtered records were carried through unchanged but did not enter the graph.  Indels in short tandem repeats were retained as evidence but down-weighted, because a misplaced repeat indel can link the wrong alleles.  SVs were taken from Sniffles2\cite{smolka2024sniffles2} or cuteSV\cite{jiang2020cutesv} with supporting read names; homozygous SVs, duplicated positions and SVs coincident with SNVs were excluded.  The exact eligibility and repeat annotation rules are in Supplementary Method~2.

\subsection*{Calling allele-specific methylation}
Modification probabilities were read from the \texttt{MM}/\texttt{ML} tags produced by Dorado\cite{ont2025dorado} and carried through alignment.  Each read-level CpG observation was classified as methylated when its 5mC probability was at least 0.8, unmethylated when it was at most 0.2, and ambiguous otherwise.  Forward- and reverse-strand observations were mapped to one CpG coordinate.  A site was retained as a candidate allele-specific site when methylated and unmethylated reads were sufficiently balanced and the ambiguous fraction was small:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\min(M_R,U_R)}{\max(M_R,U_R)} \ge 0.6,
\qquad
\frac{A_R}{M_R+U_R+A_R} \le 0.2,
\label{eq:hetmeth}
\end{equation}
where $M_R$, $U_R$ and $A_R$ are the numbers of methylated, unmethylated and ambiguous reads, respectively.  Candidates were then required to co-segregate with a neighbouring heterozygous marker.  For a pair of sites, writing $RR$, $RA$, $AR$ and $AA$ for the four observed state combinations, linkage required
\begin{equation}
\frac{\max(RR+AA,\;RA+AR)}{RR+AA+RA+AR} \ge 0.9.
\label{eq:linkmeth}
\end{equation}
The same criterion was used for SNV-anchored and methylation-only linking.  Only CpG dinucleotides were considered.  Weak candidates were admitted in a later expansion pass, and consecutive CpGs were merged into one locus with a shared read list for both strands.  The resulting methylated and unmethylated read names and depths were supplied to the phasing graph.  The depth bound and expansion rules are in Supplementary Method~1, and the procedure is summarized in Supplementary Fig.~11.

\subsection*{Read-level allele observations and filtering}
Unmapped, secondary and duplicate alignments, and alignments with mapping quality below 1, were excluded.  SNV alleles came from the aligned base and its Phred quality; indel alleles from the CIGAR; SV alleles from a matching CIGAR event, the junction of a split alignment or the caller's read list; and 5mC alleles from the methylation read lists (Supplementary Fig.~12).  Supplementary alignments were retained as evidence, so when two alignments of the same read overlapped by more than 20\% of their combined span, the shorter alignment was discarded.  Under the nanopore setting, a downstream SNV was removed when two SNVs at most 2~bp apart both lay in homopolymers of length at least three.

A copy-number-aware filter identified intervals bounded by excess front- or back-clipping and removed variants whose alternate-supporting reads had an unusually high mismatch load relative to reference-supporting reads.  Specifically, a read's mismatch load $R_m$ counted the non-SV heterozygous sites in the interval at which it carried the alternate allele, and each variant was scored by
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MR}(v) = \frac{\bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{ALT}}}{\bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{REF}} + \bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{ALT}}},
\label{eq:cnvmr}
\end{equation}
where the barred terms are the mean loads of reads supporting the ALT and REF alleles of $v$. MR was evaluated only when the two mean loads were above zero.  Sites with $\mathrm{MR}(v)\ge 0.7$ were removed from all reads, except SVs, which were retained.  The three-state interval scanner and its constants are specified in Supplementary Method~2.

\subsection*{Weighted phasing graph}
Each heterozygous variant contributed reference and alternate allele nodes.  For each read, every allele was connected to the alleles that the read carried at the next 35 heterozygous variants in chromosome order, with no cap on genomic distance. Variants followed by a gap of more than 300~kb to the next heterozygous variant were skipped during haplotype resolution, casting no votes and receiving no haplotype or entropy.  A read contributed weight 1 when both observations had quality at least 12 and weight 0.1 otherwise.  Indel and 5mC observations and SV alternate observations were assigned fixed quality 60; reference-allele SV observations were assigned 30.  Thus all such observations have full weight at the default threshold, while reference-SV evidence is down-weighted only at a higher threshold.

For a variant pair $(u,v)$, the four allele-pair supports were accumulated as $w_{rr}$, $w_{ra}$, $w_{ar}$ and $w_{aa}$.  The two possible pairings have support $P=w_{rr}+w_{aa}$ and $Q=w_{ra}+w_{ar}$, and their ambiguity is $s=\min(P,Q)/\max(P,Q)$.

\subsection*{Haplotype resolution by multi-neighbour voting}
Variants were processed in coordinate order (Supplementary Fig.~13).  Each variant cast a vote to each downstream neighbour for the pairing with larger support, except when $s>0.7$; SNV--5mC pairs used a 0.3 threshold and required at least one read-equivalent of support.  Votes had weight 1 by default, 20 for near-unanimous support ($s\le0.1$ with total support at least 1, or one pairing below 1 while the other was at least 1), and 0.1 when the voting variant was a tandem-repeat indel.

The vote was translated using the already assigned haplotype of the upstream variant, producing totals $h_1(v)$ and $h_2(v)$.  When more than three votes had support of at most 1 read-equivalent, and at least one qualifying vote remained, only votes with support above 1 read-equivalent, $s<0.2$, weight at least 1 and a non-indel voting variant were retained.  The variant was assigned to the haplotype with the larger total.

A tie beyond the last accepted edge opened a new block. When a tie occurred within an existing block, the variant remained unphased and cast no votes.  A variant whose votes all came from SVs, beyond the last variant reached by a vote of a small variant, also opened a new block, so that an SV was never the only link between two blocks; SVs still voted and were phased within a block.  The first variant position defined the phase-set identifier.

The constants were fixed by a pre-study sweep on HG002 at 10--60$\times$ and were not re-tuned for the reported experiments; the parameter tuning is described in Supplementary Method~3.

\subsection*{Read-based correction and phasing entropy}
After graph assignment, each read was assigned to a haplotype by a weighted majority of its phased SNV and SV alleles (weight 1) and indel alleles (weight 0.1). 5mC observations were excluded from this pass (Supplementary Fig.~14). Reads were retained only when the majority fraction exceeded 0.65 and the total evidence weight exceeded 1.  For each variant, the consistency of the two read populations was
\[
\rho=\frac{\max(n_1,n_2)}{n_1+n_2},
\]
where $n_1$ counts haplotype-1 reads carrying REF plus haplotype-2 reads carrying ALT, and $n_2$ counts the converse. The majority pairing assigned the phase only when $\rho>0.75$; otherwise the variant was left unphased.

For each phased variant, phasing entropy was calculated from the weighted haplotype totals after the single-read guard:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PE}(v)=-\sum_{i\in\{1,2\}}p_i\log_2p_i,
\qquad p_i=\frac{h_i}{h_1+h_2}.
\end{equation}
It ranges from 0 for unanimous support to 1 bit for equal support.  When $h_1+h_2=0$, PE was set to 0 and the case was identified from the accompanying $h_1,h_2$ fields; this occurs only at the first variant of a block.  PE, $h_1$ and $h_2$ were written to the VCF, and accepted voting edges with their accumulated weights formed the GNN input graph.

\subsection*{GNN inference for phase-error detection}
Phasing entropy was used to select candidate windows for refinement. Within each window, the GNN combined phasing evidence, genomic context and graph structure to identify phase-disagreement onsets for selective unphasing, as defined by the training labels. Positive targets include isolated disagreements and the first positions of consecutive disagreement runs. The resulting onset scores are not calibrated probabilities.

The GNN operated on merged SNV/indel, SV and 5mC records (Supplementary Fig.~15). A window of at most 41 variants (82 allele nodes) was opened around every phased variant with $\mathrm{PE}\ge0.8$, extending 20 variants on either side (Supplementary Fig.~16).

Each allele node carried 31 features in four groups: phasing evidence, such as phasing entropy and weighted haplotype totals; genomic context, such as homopolymer length, GC content and variant density; graph structure, such as out-degree and phase-set size; and four one-hot variant-class indicators. Six edge features summarized vote weight, genomic distance, allele matching and phase-set relations. Edges were restricted to the window, symmetrized and supplemented with mean-feature self-loops. Exact feature definitions and normalizations are given in Supplementary Tables~13 and~14.

The network used four GPS graph-transformer layers\cite{rampasek2022gps}, each combining a local four-head GATv2 branch\cite{velickovic2018gat,brody2022gatv2} and a global four-head self-attention branch\cite{vaswani2017attention}, followed by residual normalization and a 128--256--128 feed-forward block. The local GATv2 branch aggregates information along edges of the phasing graph, using edge features to weight the contributions of neighbouring allele nodes. The global self-attention branch allows each node to access information from all nodes within the same window, including those without a direct graph connection. A two-layer classifier produced the two-class softmax output. Architecture and inference details are in Supplementary Method~4, Table~14 and Supplementary Fig.~17.

Only variants no further from the trigger than half the largest trigger distance in the window were scored; the same rule defined the nodes used for training loss. Nodes outside that zone supplied context only. Because the zone is defined on genomic distance and not on variant index, the number of variants it holds depends on the local spacing. The two allele-node scores were averaged, followed by averaging across the windows in which the variant lay inside the scoring zone.

A variant with mean score at least 0.35 was unphased while preserving its genotype, except deletions of at least 10~kb with at least one vote and $\mathrm{PE}=0$, which kept their phase. Bridge protection was disabled. If removing these variants disconnected a block, the surviving connected components became separate phase sets. A variant that unphasing or block splitting left alone in its phase set was also unphased, whatever its score (Supplementary Method~4).

Inference was implemented in C++ without external runtime dependencies; the export procedure and numerical agreement checks are described in Supplementary Method~4.

\subsection*{GNN training and model selection}
Training windows came from the same HG002 nanopore co-phasing runs used for evaluation at 10--20$\times$ (six coverages and ten down-sampling replicates, 60 runs).  Of 722,116 windows, 602,788 from chr1--14 and chr18--20 were used for training and 67,880 from chr15--16 for validation. The remaining 51,448 windows from chr17, chr21 and chr22 were used for evaluation. These chromosome sets were disjoint. Windows from chr17, chr21 and chr22 were excluded from model fitting, batch-normalization statistics and checkpoint selection. However, results from these chromosomes contributed to the choice of label definition, as described below.  Windows contained all four variant classes, but only benchmarked SNV and indel nodes had labels, and SV and 5mC correction relies on the model trained on those labelled small variants.  The training and evaluation truth was the v5.0q small-variant benchmark.  Training windows were limited to 10--20$\times$, whereas results also included 30--60$\times$ and PacBio HiFi data.

Labels described local phase orientation.  Within each window and phase set, the first benchmarked variant anchored the orientation; later disagreements were labelled as a single-variant flip or unphase event, or as a break at the first site of a longer run.  Benchmark-absent variants and SV/5mC records were masked from the loss but retained as graph context.  The released model was trained and applied in binary mode. The binary classifier merged the flip, unphase and break labels into one error class; predicted targets were subsequently unphased.  Error labels accounted for 2.96\% of labelled central nodes in the training split.

Correct nodes were sampled independently in each minibatch at a ratio of at most four per error node, and the resulting cross-entropy used focal weighting\cite{lin2017focal} with $\gamma=1.5$ and label smoothing 0.05.  Training used haplotype-swap and coverage-scaling augmentations; after a swap, every edge took the vote weight of the allele node it then left. The optimizer, stopping rule and environment are listed in Supplementary Table~16.

We used a normalized net-gain criterion for checkpoint selection, assigning unit benefit to each true-positive detection of a label-defined correction target and unit cost to each false-positive prediction. Checkpoints were selected on chr15--16 by maximizing
\begin{equation}
S=R\left(2-\frac{1}{P}\right)
\end{equation}
over 81 score thresholds from 0.01 to 0.99, subject to $R\ge0.005$, where $P$ and $R$ are precision and recall for the label-defined error class. This expression applies for $P>0.5$; otherwise the score was $P-1$. The criterion serves as a proxy for correction utility (Supplementary Method~4). Checkpoint details and classification metrics for validation and held-out chromosomes are reported in Supplementary Table~16.

Models were trained using two label definitions, each with five random seeds (42, 43, 44, 1337 and 3407). The first matched benchmark variants to calls by position; the second required matching position, REF and ALT and left calls with different alleles unlabelled. All other settings were identical. Each of the ten models was applied to the 60 HG002 nanopore SNV-only runs at 10--20$\times$. Its deployment threshold $B$ was set on the validation chromosomes to match a correction cost of 6.35 phased SNVs withheld per removed switch error. This was the cost of the earlier release v2.1 model at $B=0.30$.

At that cost, the position-matched labels removed 23.0\% of switch errors on the chromosomes excluded from training (mean of five seeds; range 22.4--23.9\%), compared with 21.6\% (20.8--22.9\%) for the REF/ALT-matched labels. The latter nevertheless had higher classification scores on these chromosomes (Supplementary Table~17). The position-matched labels were retained on the basis of this comparison. Among the five position-matched models, seed 42 removed the most switch errors on the validation chromosomes and was selected for release. Its validation-selected threshold of 0.34 was rounded to $B=0.35$ for deployment.

In the released implementation, correction removed 34.3\% of switch errors at a cost of 7.98 withheld SNVs per removed error genome-wide. On chr17, chr21 and chr22, it removed 23.5\% at a cost of 9.26 (Supplementary Method~4, Supplementary Note~1, Supplementary Fig.~18e and Supplementary Table~18).

\subsection*{Haplotagging}
Reads were tagged from the phased SNV, indel, SV and 5mC alleles they carried.  Each informative observation contributed one vote, and a read was assigned to the majority haplotype when its fraction was at least 0.60; reads whose informative variants belonged to multiple phase sets or lacked a majority were left untagged.  Tagged reads received HP, PS and phasing-quality fields.  SV alleles were re-derived from the CIGAR and 5mC evidence was matched by read name.  Full evidence and quality definitions are in Supplementary Method~5.

\subsection*{Benchmark data}
The benchmark used HG002 (NA24385) R10.4.1 reads from the Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) \texttt{giab\_lsk114\_2022.12} release: two PromethION flow cells (PAG65784 and PAG68757), SQK-LSK114, 400 bases per second and 4-kHz sampling, basecalled with Dorado \texttt{dna\_r10.4.1\_e8.2\_400bps\_sup@v4.0.0} and \texttt{5mCG\_5hmCG@v2}; their read N50 values were 29.2 and 29.4~kb.  Reads were aligned to the GRCh38 no-alt analysis set by ONT with minimap2 v2.24 (\texttt{-y -ax map-ont}), retaining reads with mean base quality $\ge 10$.  The 60$\times$ data set is a random 91.14\% subset of the 70.4$\times$ alignment (samtools v1.15, \texttt{view -s 0.9114}), and the 10--50$\times$ data sets were down-sampled from it with samtools v1.23.1\cite{danecek2021samtools} \texttt{view -s} \textit{seed.fraction}.  Ten replicates were used at 10--20$\times$ and one at 30--60$\times$; variants were called separately at every coverage and replicate (Supplementary Table~19).  SNVs and indels came from PEPPER-Margin-DeepVariant r0.8, which reports unphased genotypes, SVs from Sniffles2 v2.8.0, and allele-specific 5mC sites from \toolname.
The seeds were 1--9 and 867 at 10$\times$ (fraction 0.1666), 12--21 at 12$\times$ (0.200), 22--31 at 14$\times$ (0.233), 32--41 at 16$\times$ (0.266), 42--51 at 18$\times$ (0.300), 1--10 at 20$\times$ (0.3333), and 42 at 30, 40 and 50$\times$ (0.5, 0.6667 and 0.8333).  PEPPER-Margin-DeepVariant used the \texttt{--ont\_r10\_q20} model for nanopore data and \texttt{--hifi} for HiFi data.
HG002 PacBio HiFi (Revio) reads were taken from a 48$\times$ GIAB alignment (Supplementary Table~20), which was used as 50$\times$; 10--40$\times$ data sets were down-sampled from it with samtools v1.23.1 \texttt{view --subsample} (seed 1; fractions 0.2083, 0.4167, 0.6250 and 0.8333), and variants were called separately at every coverage.

Accuracy was scored with \texttt{longphase compare} on autosomes 1--22 against two HG002 truth sets: GIAB v4.2.1\cite{wagner2022giab}, in the version with phase transferred from a hifiasm assembly (the v4.2.1 benchmark VCF itself is unphased), restricted to biallelic heterozygous records; and GIAB v5.0q\cite{olson2026giabv5}, derived from the diploid T2T-HG002 assembly\cite{hansen2026q100}, in which every heterozygous variant is phased.  The truth VCFs were not restricted to their benchmark regions, and a call was scored only where the truth VCF holds the same REF and ALT alleles in a heterozygous genotype.  Both truth VCFs list heterozygous variants outside their benchmark regions (9.3\% of the autosomal heterozygous records of v5.0q and 3.4\% of v4.2.1), so the error counts include sites that the benchmark regions do not cover, among them satellite and segmental-duplication sequence that the T2T-HG002 assembly resolves\cite{hansen2026q100}.

To locate switch errors and to score indels, every consecutive variant pair in an intersected block was recorded with a re-implementation of \texttt{longphase compare}, which reproduced its counts exactly in all 1,024 scored runs.  A pair was assigned to a region when both variants lay in it; regions were the intersection and the differences of the two sets of benchmark regions, the remainder outside both sets, and the GIAB v3.6 genome stratifications\cite{dwarshuis2024stratifications}, which were applied inside the v5.0q benchmark regions.

A switch error was shared between tools when it lay at the same pair of SNV positions on the same chromosome under the same truth set (replicate~1); individual switch-error pairs were not tracked across the two truth sets.

Indel switch errors were counted over consecutive pairs that included at least one indel, and the indel Hamming distance was computed in the block orientation chosen on all variants.

\subsection*{Comparison with other methods}
\whatshap~v2.8\cite{patterson2015whatshap} and \hapcut~v1.3.4\cite{edge2017hapcut2} were run on the same alignments and variant calls as \toolname; cross-tool SNV comparisons used SNV-only phasing, with indel phasing disabled in \whatshap.  \toolname was run with the nanopore setting and the GNN settings given above.  Co-phasing experiments separately evaluated the seven variant-class combinations listed in Supplementary Table~7, with and without correction.  Indel co-phasing was also compared with \whatshap's default indel phasing and, in replicate~1, with \hapcut run with indel phasing (Supplementary Table~4).

The HiFi call sets were phased SNV-only with \toolname (\texttt{--pb}), with and without correction, with \whatshap, with \hapcut and with HiPhase v1.7.0\cite{holt2024hiphase}; the GNN weights were trained on nanopore data.  HiPhase phases indels by default and has no SNV-only mode, so its SNV-only runs used the call sets restricted to SNVs, the variants that \whatshap phases with \texttt{--only-snvs}.  For the comparison with indels, \toolname, \whatshap and \hapcut were run with indel phasing and HiPhase on the full call sets (Supplementary Table~4).

MethPhaser\cite{fu2024methphaser} was given the SNV-only output of \toolname without GNN correction instead of the \whatshap or \hapcut phasing its authors recommend as input (configuration in Supplementary Table~20).  Both analyses used the same read alignments and SNV calls.  \toolname phased SNVs and 5mC jointly in a new graph and did not reuse the SNV-only phasing.  Without GNN correction, differences between them reflect how methylation is used, after phasing to bridge existing blocks (MethPhaser) or during phasing as additional graph evidence (\toolname); with GNN correction, they also include its effect.

\subsection*{Evaluation metrics}
Metrics (Supplementary Fig.~19) were computed by intersecting heterozygous query and truth records by position and REF/ALT alleles, then partitioning the intersection into blocks phased in both files; singleton blocks were ignored.  Within each block, switch errors were the Hamming distance between truth and query switch-encoded haplotype strings, and the switch-error rate was the number of switch errors divided by the number of assessed consecutive SNV pairs.  Isolated switches and two-consecutive-switch flips were reported separately.

Hamming distance was the block-wise minimum over the two orientations, divided by the number of compared variants; unlike switch rate, it included indels when present in both files, so it is not comparable between configurations with and without indels, which we therefore compared by SNV switch error rate.  Because a longer block can contain a longer segment of wrong orientation, block-wise Hamming distance tends to increase with block length, so it does not compare tools with different block lengths on equal terms.

Completeness (phased fraction) was the number of truth heterozygous SNVs or indels that were phased by the query divided by all truth heterozygous variants of that class.  Block N50 was the span-weighted median of query phase-set spans of at least two variants, pooled over chromosomes.  Switch-error positions were exported for the unphased-SNV analysis.

For each co-phasing configuration, we defined \emph{blocks joined} as the net decrease in block count relative to SNV-only phasing in the same replicate and at the same GNN setting. Individual merge events were not tracked. We defined \emph{sites added} as the total number of phased indels, SVs and 5mC sites. We calculated switch errors per join as the increase in SNV switch-error count relative to SNV-only phasing divided by the net decrease in block count.  The exact block and denominator definitions are in Supplementary Method~6.

\subsection*{Evaluation in other individuals}
The released model (weights and threshold $B=0.35$) was applied without change to nanopore data from two further individuals; truth construction and scoring rules were fixed before any output was scored.  HG008-N reads were the GIAB normal-pancreas R10.4.1 data (three PromethION flow cells, basecalled with Dorado v0.5.3 \texttt{sup@v4.3.0}), retained at mean base quality $\ge 10$ and aligned to the GRCh38 no-alt analysis set with minimap2 v2.24\cite{li2018minimap2} (\texttt{-y -ax map-ont}); the 56.5$\times$ alignment was down-sampled with samtools \texttt{view -s} \textit{seed.fraction} to 10 and 20$\times$ (seeds 1--3) and 30$\times$ (seed~1), and variants were called with PEPPER-Margin-DeepVariant r0.8 (\texttt{--ont\_r10\_q20}).  COLO829BL reads were the ONT \texttt{colo829\_2024.03} R10.4.1 data of flow cell PAU59807 (Dorado v0.5.2 \texttt{sup@v4.3.0}), in the GRCh38 alignment provided by ONT, retained at mean base quality $\ge 10$ and down-sampled with one seed to 10, 20 and 30$\times$ (measured 10.8, 21.7 and 32.5$\times$); variants were called with Clair3 v1.2.0\cite{zheng2022clair3} (model \texttt{r1041\_e82\_400bps\_sup\_v430}, unphased output).  \toolname with and without GNN correction, \whatshap and \hapcut were run SNV-only on the same alignments and calls, with the settings used for HG002.

Truth phase was derived from diploid assemblies: the GIAB HG008-N v6.3 assembly and the SMaHT COLO829BL donor-specific assembly v3.0.0, both assembled with Verkko\cite{rautiainen2023verkko}.  Each pair of haplotypes was aligned to GRCh38 with dipcall\cite{li2018dipcall}; COLO829BL contigs were cut into 20-Mb pieces before alignment.  On autosomes, inside the dipcall confidence regions, records with a dipcall filter (heterozygous or uncalled on one haplotype) were removed.  A truth block was a maximal GRCh38 interval covered by one alignment of each haplotype with no assembly gap in either; a block was also ended at every run of more than 100~kb without a heterozygous record, across which the assembly's phase does not rest on read linkage, and, for COLO829BL, at every cut.  Heterozygous records were kept with the block as phase set: 2,781 blocks and 2,260,719 biallelic heterozygous SNVs for HG008-N, and 2,723 blocks and 2,254,361 SNVs for COLO829BL.  Switch errors and Hamming distances were counted within truth blocks as for HG002; completeness was the percentage of these SNVs phased.  The truth sets are not independent of the evaluation data: the corrected nanopore reads used to assemble HG008-N include reads from the same flow cells, and whether the COLO829BL evaluation reads contributed to its assembly was not determined.

\subsection*{SV phase evaluation}
SV phase was evaluated on chr1--22 against haplotype-resolved variants called by dipcall from the T2T-HG002 Q100 v1.1 assembly aligned to GRCh38\cite{li2018dipcall,hansen2026q100}. Truth records with a missing allele on either haplotype were excluded. SVs were called with Sniffles2 v2.8.0 using the reference genome and retaining supporting read names on both platforms. Evaluation used replicate~1 at every coverage and the same heterozygous deletions and insertions of at least 50~bp for every tool.

\whatshap~v2.8 (\texttt{--indels}) and HiPhase v1.7.0 read SVs only as sequence-resolved records in the variant VCF. For these tools, deletions were given the reference sequence as REF and insertions the inserted sequence as ALT. These records were merged with the SNV (and indel) calls. \toolname received the unmodified SV calls. Inversions, duplications, breakends, symbolic insertions and records with an unknown reference base (296--580 per run) could not be converted. They were not given to \whatshap or HiPhase and were not scored for any tool.

A truth match required the same SV type and size similarity of at least 70\%. It also required start positions within 1~kb or, for deletions, reciprocal overlap of at least 50\%. The nearest eligible truth SV was selected. Each block was oriented by the majority phase agreement of its SNVs with truth SNVs matched by position. An SV matched to a heterozygous truth SV was correct when its oriented phase agreed with the assembly.

Phased SVs matched to a homozygous truth SV were counted separately as genotype errors. Homozygous matches, phased SVs without a truth match and SVs in blocks that could not be oriented were excluded from the phase-error rate. The SV phasing error rate was the number of incorrectly phased SVs divided by the total number of correctly and incorrectly phased SVs.

\subsection*{Analysis of unphased SNVs}
At 10, 30 and 60$\times$ coverage, we compared \toolname and \whatshap using replicate~1. The two output VCFs contained the same biallelic heterozygous SNV calls. We grouped these calls according to whether they were phased by both tools, \toolname only, \whatshap only or neither. Calls phased by \whatshap only were further split according to whether \toolname had left them unphased before GNN correction.

Each call was classified against the v5.0q chr1--22 VCF, without a benchmark BED, as heterozygous with the same position and alleles, heterozygous with other alleles, homozygous or absent. Calls on chrX and chrY were counted separately. The calls phased by a tool comprised those phased by both tools and those phased by that tool only. Phased calls at positions with a homozygous v5.0q record were counted as genotype errors (Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}c).

Precision and F1 were calculated for every SNV-only run of \toolname and \whatshap, and on HiFi data also of HiPhase. Precision was the fraction of phased heterozygous SNV calls on chr1--22 that matched a benchmark heterozygous SNV, using the matched-call count from \texttt{compare}. F1 was the harmonic mean of precision and completeness. Precision here is a benchmark-match fraction: an unmatched call outside the benchmark regions is unclassified, not a confirmed error. F1 therefore combines benchmark matching with completeness and does not measure phase correctness.

For benchmark-matched calls phased by one tool only, that tool's phase assignments were scored as in \texttt{compare}. A call was counted as wrongly phased when its phase was opposite to the majority orientation of its block relative to the truth. A switch-error interval extended from the first SNV of a switch-error pair to the base before the second. The fraction of calls phased by one tool only that fell inside that tool's switch-error intervals was compared with the corresponding fraction among all heterozygous SNV calls phased by that tool.

Read depth was taken from the variant caller's \texttt{FORMAT/DP} field, which is capped near 125$\times$. Calls were also annotated by the v5.0q benchmark regions, GIAB v3.6 segmental duplications and the one-to-one dipcall alignment (Supplementary Data~1).

An SNV was counted as unphased by GNN correction when it was phased before correction and unphased afterwards. This is the subset described as left unphased at high phasing uncertainty.  The subset includes variants near a high-entropy variant and variants left alone in their phase set, so not every SNV in it has high phasing entropy.  Such sites were classified against the v5.0q benchmark VCF, matched by chromosome and position and without a benchmark BED, as absent, homozygous or heterozygous (Supplementary Fig.~7c); the heterozygous count was also reported with matching REF and ALT alleles, the rule used by \texttt{compare}.  The analysis used the 10--60$\times$ outputs of replicate~1 with the final GNN model.

At 60$\times$, SNVs unphased by GNN correction were also classified against the T2T-HG002 assembly projected to GRCh38 as outside the 1:1 assembly BED, inside without an assembly variant at the same start coordinate, or inside with one; alleles were not compared.  All chromosomes, including chrX and chrY, were analysed, and autosome-only values were derived from the per-chromosome counts.  A random sample of 50,000 SNVs phased before correction, which included sites that GNN correction later unphased, provided the background; enrichment was summarized by chromosome.

\subsection*{Runtime and memory}
Wall-clock time, CPU time and peak resident memory of SNV-only phasing were measured on one workstation (AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, 16 cores, 249~GB of memory, Ubuntu 24.04, input and output on an NVMe solid-state drive), one run at a time with the page cache cleared before each run.  \toolname was run with 24 threads and on one thread; \whatshap and \hapcut have no thread option and ran on one thread (Supplementary Table~12).

\subsection*{Statistics and reproducibility}
Ten nanopore down-sampling replicates at 10--20$\times$ were summarized by their mean and standard deviation, while 30--60$\times$ and HiFi used one down-sampling per coverage.  The SV evaluation, MethPhaser comparison, runs of \hapcut with indels, and runtime measurements used replicate~1 only. Each runtime configuration was timed once. Call composition, phase errors among calls phased by one tool only, switch-error interval enrichment, read depth and assembly-alignment status were analysed using replicate~1. Precision and F1 were calculated for all SNV-only runs.  In HG008-N, three down-sampling seeds were used at 10 and 20$\times$ and one at 30$\times$; in COLO829BL, one seed per coverage.

Subsamples for each platform were drawn from the same source sequencing data set of HG002. The reported standard deviations therefore describe variation due to read subsampling, conditional on that data set, rather than variation among independent sequencing experiments. Subsampling variability was not estimated at 30--60$\times$ or for HiFi. Tools compared at each coverage and replicate used the same sampled reads, so fold differences between tools and the effect of GNN correction at 10--20$\times$ were also computed within each replicate (Supplementary Table~1).  No statistical tests, sample-size calculation, randomization or blinding were used.  GNN inference used fixed model weights and no sampling.
