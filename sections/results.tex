\subsection*{\toolname combines joint phasing with learned error detection}
\toolname constructs a single phasing graph per chromosome from SNVs, small indels, SVs and allele-specific 5mC sites identified by the built-in \texttt{modcall} module (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). Quality-dependent weighting and context-specific filters reduce the influence of unreliable observations, including those in homopolymers, tandem repeats and candidate copy-number-altered regions (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}a,b). After haplotype assignment, each phased variant receives a phasing-uncertainty score. A graph neural network (GNN) then evaluates variants in local windows centred on sites with high uncertainty (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}c). Variants predicted to be misphased are unphased while their genotypes are retained; blocks disconnected by this removal are split. We refer to this step as GNN correction. The method outputs phased VCFs for each variant class and haplotagged alignments (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}d).

We evaluated \toolname using HG002 nanopore R10.4.1 data down-sampled to 10--60$\times$ and PacBio HiFi data from the same individual at 10--50$\times$ (Methods). Nanopore results at 10--20$\times$ are means of ten down-sampling replicates. Results at 30--60$\times$, all HiFi results and analyses of unphased variants use one down-sampling per coverage. We assessed phasing against two HG002 benchmarks: GIAB v4.2.1, integrated from mapping-based call sets, and GIAB v5.0q, in which every heterozygous variant is phased using the complete diploid telomere-to-telomere (T2T) assembly. The benchmarks share 2.50~Gb of autosomal sequence. Relative to v4.2.1, v5.0q covers more tandem-repeat and homopolymer sequence but less segmental-duplication sequence (Methods). Unless otherwise stated, results describe SNV phasing against v5.0q, and \toolname denotes the complete method, including GNN correction. We first compare \toolname with existing phasers, then assess its individual components. Per-run values and summary metrics are provided in Supplementary Data~1 and Supplementary Tables~7--12.

\paragraph{Benchmark choice changes the measured separation between phasers.}
Against GIAB v4.2.1, \toolname, \whatshap and \hapcut produced 1,600--2,200 switch errors at 30--60$\times$, with a 1.2--1.3-fold difference between the highest and lowest rates. Against v5.0q, the same phased VCFs showed a 4.3--4.8-fold difference (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmarks}a,b and Supplementary Table~9). The change was tool-dependent. \hapcut had more switch errors against v5.0q at every coverage, and \whatshap at every coverage below 60$\times$; both had 1.6-fold more at 10$\times$. By contrast, \toolname had fewer switch errors against v5.0q (1,518 versus 1,820 at 10$\times$; 496 versus 1,676 at 60$\times$). Hamming distance changed little between benchmarks (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmarks}c,d). The contributions of benchmark-region coverage, variant representation and phase discordance remain unresolved because the error locations have not been compared directly\todo{Score the regions and alleles common to both benchmarks separately from the benchmark-specific ones, and intersect switch-error locations across tools and benchmarks}.

\paragraph{\toolname retains lower SNV switch error rates when co-phasing indels.}
Enabling indel phasing in \whatshap increased block N50 from 2.72 to 4.20~Mb at 60$\times$, but increased SNV switch error rates by 17--20\% across coverages (Fig.~\ref{fig:cophasing}a,b). With the same indels, \toolname had 3.0--3.7-fold lower SNV switch error rates, whereas \whatshap phased 2.4--4.5 percentage points more benchmark heterozygous indels (Fig.~\ref{fig:cophasing}c) and achieved 14--28\% higher block N50 values. Adding SV and 5mC evidence to \toolname reduced this contiguity difference: its block N50 was 2--19\% lower than that of \whatshap with indels (4.10 versus 4.20~Mb at 60$\times$), while its SNV switch error rates remained 3.0--3.7-fold lower (Fig.~\ref{fig:cophasing}b,d and Supplementary Table~10). MethPhaser\cite{fu2024methphaser}, which adds methylation evidence to existing SNV blocks, left the block N50 and switch-error counts of its input \toolname SNV blocks unchanged, whereas joint SNV and 5mC phasing increased block N50 by 8\% (Supplementary Fig.~11).

\paragraph{\toolname achieves 2.5- to 3.5-fold lower switch error rates on PacBio HiFi data.}
On HG002 PacBio HiFi data, \toolname had 2.5--3.5-fold lower SNV switch error rates than \whatshap without retraining the nanopore-derived network (Fig.~\ref{fig:hifi}a,b and Supplementary Table~11). Rates were 0.052\% versus 0.128\% at 10$\times$ and 0.042\% versus 0.125\% at 50$\times$. \toolname also had lower Hamming distances at every coverage (Fig.~\ref{fig:hifi}c). Block N50 differed by less than 3\% between the tools, while \whatshap phased 0.4--0.7 percentage points more benchmark SNVs (Fig.~\ref{fig:hifi}d,e)\todo{HiFi call sets are being regenerated per coverage (Methods); update these values}. At 50$\times$, the \toolname block N50 was 4.6-fold lower for HiFi than for nanopore data (0.55 versus 2.51~Mb), consistent with the shorter HiFi reads.

\paragraph{\toolname and \whatshap leave distinct, partly overlapping sets of SNVs unphased.}
At 60$\times$, \toolname left 210,683 heterozygous SNV calls unphased, compared with 142,313 for \whatshap. Of these, 88,692 were unphased in both outputs, 121,991 in \toolname alone and 53,621 in \whatshap alone (Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}a). Each tool-specific set was enriched in intervals containing switch disagreements between the other tool and the benchmark. Enrichment increased from 4.3-fold at 10$\times$ to 9.6-fold at 60$\times$ for SNVs unphased by \toolname alone, and from 2.0-fold to 9.5-fold for those unphased by \whatshap alone (Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}b). These enrichments describe regional co-location and do not establish the correctness of individual phase assignments. The sets also differed in read depth (Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}c and Supplementary Fig.~12). At 60$\times$, SNVs unphased by \whatshap alone had a median depth of 18$\times$, whereas 15.6\% of those unphased by \toolname alone reached the variant caller's depth cap\todo{the median depths and the zero-entropy share among \whatshap-only sites (57.6\%) are quoted from JHL's commit message of 2026-09-27 and are not in any committed table; add the per-set summary to Supplementary Data~1}. This contrast indicates that the tools withhold phase under different read-depth conditions; capped depths alone do not identify the source of excess read coverage.

\subsection*{Contribution of the internal modules}

\paragraph{GNN correction reduces switch error rates with a modest loss of phased SNVs and contiguity.}
Before GNN correction, \toolname had SNV switch error rates of 0.121\% at 10$\times$ and 0.034\% at 60$\times$, already 1.9--2.5-fold lower than those of \whatshap and 2.2--3.3-fold lower than those of \hapcut across coverages (Supplementary Fig.~13 and Supplementary Table~8). GNN correction reduced these rates by a further 31--33\%, while decreasing the phased fraction by 0.1--0.3 percentage points. Block N50 decreased by 5--9\% at 10--20$\times$ and 2--5\% at 30--60$\times$. On HiFi data, GNN correction reduced switch-error counts by 19--28\%, with decreases of 0.1 percentage points in phased fraction and 1--2\% in block N50 (Supplementary Fig.~13e,f).

\paragraph{Methylation increases contiguity at similar switch error rates, whereas indels increase both contiguity and error.}
We evaluated seven combinations of variant classes, each with and without GNN correction (Supplementary Figs.~14 and~15 and Supplementary Table~10). Without correction, adding 5mC increased block N50 at 60$\times$ by 8\%, SVs by 1\%, indels by 49\% and all three by 75\% (2.91 to 5.10~Mb). Only indels increased the SNV switch error rate, from 0.034\% to 0.043\% at 60$\times$ and from 0.121\% to 0.155\% at 10$\times$\todo{For configurations that phase indels, \texttt{compare} computes the Hamming distance over all common heterozygous variants, indels included (\texttt{CompareProcess.cpp}:579), whereas switch errors are SNV-only. Every Hamming value of an indel-containing configuration in this section, in Figs.~3c,d and~4d and in Supplementary Tables~9 and~10, therefore mixes SNV and indel phasing. Re-score these runs with SNV-only Hamming distance and report indel phasing accuracy separately}. GNN correction reduced switch error rates by 30--42\% across configurations and reduced the additional error associated with indels by 41--67\%. Block N50 decreased by 12--22\% in indel-containing configurations as unphasing disconnected previously bridged blocks.

After GNN correction, SNV+5mC phasing had a switch error rate similar to that of SNV-only phasing (497 versus 496 switch errors at 60$\times$), with an 8\% higher block N50. Four-class phasing yielded a switch error rate of 0.027\% and a 43\% higher block N50 than SNV-only phasing (4.10 versus 2.86~Mb). \toolname phased 30.8--45.5\% of benchmark heterozygous indels, 99.0--99.4\% of the heterozygous 5mC sites called by \texttt{modcall} and 68.1--70.1\% of the heterozygous SVs called by Sniffles2 in four-class phasing before GNN correction; GNN correction then unphased 1.4--4.6\% of the phased 5mC sites and 2.4--3.8\% of the phased SVs\todo{Add the phased SV and 5mC counts per configuration and coverage (audit table \texttt{q10\_sv\_mod.tsv}) to Supplementary Data~1}.

\paragraph{SNVs unphased by GNN correction are enriched outside regions with one-to-one assembly alignments.}
GNN correction unphased 55,756--58,162 SNVs per genome (one replicate per coverage), including 56,076 at 60$\times$. Classified by position against the v5.0q benchmark VCF, 83.0\% of those unphased at 10$\times$ were absent from it, 5.1\% were homozygous and 11.9\% (6,650) were heterozygous; at 60$\times$ the shares were 93.0\%, 3.0\% and 4.0\% (2,215) (Supplementary Fig.~16a). Requiring matching REF and ALT alleles left 6,405 and 2,055 heterozygous benchmark SNVs; the 60$\times$ value equals the loss of phased benchmark SNVs measured by \texttt{compare} (2,193,366 before and 2,191,311 after GNN correction, Supplementary Data~1). Absence from this VCF does not by itself establish that a call is erroneous, because the benchmark depends on assembly alignment and variant representation.

We next examined the assembly alignments in an exploratory analysis. Of the 56,076 SNVs unphased at 60$\times$, 11.8\% lay in regions where both assembled haplotypes aligned one-to-one to GRCh38, compared with 92.6\% of a random sample of SNVs phased before correction (odds ratio for lying outside these regions, 94; Supplementary Fig.~16b and Supplementary Table~12). Enrichment outside one-to-one alignments was observed on every autosome (Supplementary Fig.~16c). Sites outside these regions clustered in pericentromeric satellites, particularly at chr11:51--54~Mb, chr12:35--36~Mb and chr18:15--20~Mb.
