\subsection*{\toolname places SNVs, indels, SVs and methylation in one phasing graph and scores its own confidence}
\toolname resolves haplotypes in three stages (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). All heterozygous evidence carried by the same long reads, comprising SNVs, small indels, SVs and allele-specific 5mC sites called by the built-in \texttt{modcall} module, enters a single weighted phasing graph per chromosome, in which each observation is calibrated by base quality and by the reliability of its sequence context so that homopolymers, tandem repeats and copy-number-altered regions contribute less (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}a,b). For every phased variant the graph reports the weighted votes for the two haplotypes and their normalized Shannon entropy, the phasing entropy (PE), which ranges from 0 for unanimous support to 1 for equivocal support. An optional graph neural network then inspects a local window around every high-entropy variant (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}c); variants it predicts to be misphased are unphased rather than flipped, and any phase set that becomes disconnected is split, so that genotypes are preserved and no new phase assignment is ever introduced. The outputs are phased VCFs for every variant class and haplotagged alignments (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}d).

We evaluated \toolname on HG002 nanopore R10.4.1 whole-genome data down-sampled from 10$\times$ to 60$\times$ coverage (Methods). Throughout, values quoted at 10--20$\times$ are means over ten independent down-sampling replicates, and values quoted at 30--60$\times$ come from a single replicate\todo{report s.d.\ or range for every 10--20$\times$ value quoted below, and add error bars/bands to Figs.~2--5; state explicitly in the text or a supplementary table how much of the 30--60$\times$ ranking is reproducible, e.g.\ by adding two further seeds at 30$\times$ and 60$\times$}. Phasing accuracy was scored against two independent truth sets: the GRCh38-based Genome in a Bottle (GIAB) v4.2.1 benchmark and the telomere-to-telomere (T2T)-based HG002 Q100 benchmark (referred to here as v5.0q), which extends evaluation into difficult regions that v4.2.1 excludes. Unless stated otherwise, results refer to the v5.0q benchmark and to SNVs, the only variant class that all compared tools phase.

\subsection*{\toolname phases SNVs 3--5-fold more accurately and 6--8-fold faster than \whatshap and \hapcut}
On identical Clair3 variant calls and SNV-only phasing, \toolname made several-fold fewer switch errors than \whatshap~2.8 and \hapcut~1.3.4 at every coverage (Fig.~\ref{fig:snv}). The switch error rate of \toolname without the neural network was approximately 0.045\% at 30$\times$ and 0.035\% at 60$\times$, compared with 0.115\% and 0.085\% for \whatshap and 0.15\% and 0.11\% for \hapcut, a 2.5-fold reduction relative to \whatshap and a 3.2-fold reduction relative to \hapcut (Fig.~\ref{fig:snv}b)\todo{exact rates from TSV; the two fold-changes are coverage averages (\whatshap 2.6$\times$ at 30$\times$ and 2.4$\times$ at 60$\times$; \hapcut 3.3$\times$ and 3.1$\times$) --- decide whether to quote ranges instead, as elsewhere in this section}. Applying the neural network reduced the rate further, to approximately 0.032\% at 30$\times$ and 0.022\% at 60$\times$, so that \toolname with correction made 3.6--3.9-fold fewer switch errors than \whatshap and 4.7--5.0-fold fewer than \hapcut across 30--60$\times$, and 2.6-fold and 3.1-fold fewer at 10$\times$\todo{quote the \whatshap and \hapcut switch error rates at 10$\times$ as well, so that the 2.6- and 3.1-fold claims are verifiable from the reported numbers}. Correction helped most where evidence is sparsest in absolute terms: at 10$\times$ the rate fell by 0.038 percentage points, from 0.125\% to 0.087\%, against 0.013 percentage points at both 30$\times$ and 60$\times$\todo{in relative terms the reduction is in fact smallest at 30$\times$ (29\%) and largest at 60$\times$ (37\%); confirm which framing the exact numbers support}. The block-wise Hamming distance, which counts misassigned variants rather than switch points and therefore penalizes long-range flips, showed the same ordering: at 30$\times$ it was approximately 2.6\% for \whatshap and \hapcut, 2.4\% for \toolname and 1.8\% after correction, and at 10$\times$ it fell from 7.7--7.8\% for the two competing tools to 5.3\% after correction (Fig.~\ref{fig:snv}c).

The accuracy gain cost neither completeness nor haplotype length. All tools phased a similar fraction of benchmark heterozygous SNVs, rising from roughly 78\% at 10$\times$ to a plateau above 91\% from 20$\times$ onwards, with \whatshap phasing about one percentage point more than \toolname at every coverage (approximately 92.5\% versus 91.6\% at 30--60$\times$), consistent with the more conservative read-based correction of \toolname (Fig.~\ref{fig:snv}a). Block N50 grew almost linearly with coverage for all tools and was highest for \toolname (approximately 2.9~Mb at 60$\times$, versus 2.75~Mb for \whatshap and \hapcut); the neural network shortened blocks only marginally (2.87~Mb at 60$\times$), and only at 10--14$\times$ did unphasing reduce N50 noticeably (0.84~Mb versus 0.93~Mb at 10$\times$) (Fig.~\ref{fig:snv}d). \toolname was also substantially faster: phasing took 3~min at 10$\times$ and 15~min at 60$\times$ on the whole genome, compared with 26 and 95~min for \hapcut and 26 and 115~min for \whatshap, a 6--8-fold speed-up at 30--60$\times$; the neural network added 1--2~min (Fig.~\ref{fig:snv}e)\todo{threads and hardware; exact times; the 6--8-fold figure is derived from the 60$\times$ point only---give the 30$\times$ times, or restate the range as ``at 60$\times$''. Add peak memory alongside runtime (Supplementary table), which Methods already states was recorded}.

\subsection*{A telomere-to-telomere benchmark exposes switch errors hidden by the GRCh38 truth set}
Scoring the same phased VCFs against the two truth sets changed the apparent separation between methods by more than fourfold (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmarks}). Under GIAB v4.2.1, all methods converged to a narrow band of 1,600--2,200 switch errors genome-wide from 30$\times$ upwards, with \toolname with correction lowest (approximately 1,650), followed by \toolname without correction (1,700), \whatshap (1,800), \longphase~1.0 (2,000) and \hapcut (2,100) (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmarks}b)\todo{exact counts}. Under v5.0q the same VCFs separated four to five times more widely: at 60$\times$ \hapcut made approximately 2,330 switch errors, \whatshap 1,790, \longphase~1.0 1,380, \toolname 740 and \toolname with correction 490, and at 10$\times$ the corresponding counts were 5,030, 4,390, 4,030, 2,260 and 1,530 (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmarks}c). The additional errors that \whatshap and \hapcut accumulate therefore lie predominantly in regions that v4.2.1 does not assess, including segmental duplications, satellite arrays and other repeat-rich sequence that the T2T-based benchmark newly covers, and the evidence-calibration steps of \toolname are most effective precisely there\todo{support this attribution directly: stratify the v5.0q switch errors by genomic context (segmental duplication, satellite, tandem repeat, unique), and add an ablation in which the homopolymer, overlapping-alignment and copy-number-aware filters are disabled one at a time. As written, the causal claim rests on inference rather than on an experiment, and a reviewer will ask for it}.

The two benchmarks are far less discriminating on the metrics that do not depend on them. The fraction of benchmark heterozygous SNVs phased (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmarks}a) and the phase-block N50 (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmarks}d), which is benchmark independent, separate the five methods much less than the error counts do\todo{quote the phased fraction and N50 of \longphase~1.0 at 10$\times$ and 60$\times$; \longphase~1.0 appears only in Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmarks} and is currently never described quantitatively for these two panels}. Hamming distance was also nearly identical under the two benchmarks for \hapcut, \whatshap and \toolname with and without correction (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmarks}e,f), indicating that the additional v5.0q switch errors are mostly isolated switches rather than long-range flips. \longphase~1.0 was the exception: it showed a Hamming distance of 6--12\% under both benchmarks despite an intermediate number of switch errors, revealing long-range flips within blocks that the read-based correction and copy-number-aware filtering introduced in \toolname eliminate.

\subsection*{Co-phasing indels, SVs and methylation lengthens blocks by up to 70\% but raises the SNV switch error rate}
Joint phasing of all four variant classes extended haplotypes well beyond the SNV-only limit at a measurable cost in SNV accuracy, which correction then more than repaid (Figs.~\ref{fig:cophasing} and~\ref{fig:combinations}). Indels and methylation sites provide new links across SNV-poor regions, and block N50 at 60$\times$ increased from approximately 2.9~Mb for SNV-only phasing to 4.3~Mb with indels and 5.0~Mb with all four classes, and to 3.1~Mb with methylation alone (Fig.~\ref{fig:combinations}, right column). At the same time, indel observations are noisier than SNV observations: the SNV switch error rate of uncorrected co-phasing rose from 0.036\% (SNV only) to 0.048\% (all classes) at 60$\times$ and from 0.125\% to 0.156\% at 10$\times$, while the Hamming distance rose from 1.5\% to 7.2\% at 60$\times$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:cophasing}e,f)\todo{0.036\% here versus 0.035\% quoted for the identical quantity (uncorrected SNV-only switch error rate at 60$\times$) in the Fig.~\ref{fig:snv}b paragraph; reconcile against the TSV and use one value throughout}. Decomposing the combinations showed that the additional error is carried almost entirely by indels: SNV+SV and SNV+methylation had switch error rates and Hamming distances indistinguishable from SNV-only phasing, whereas SNV+indel matched the four-class configuration (Fig.~\ref{fig:combinations}).

Co-phasing did not reduce how completely each class was phased. The fraction of phased SNVs was unchanged relative to SNV-only phasing (Fig.~\ref{fig:cophasing}a). The fraction of benchmark heterozygous indels that were phased rose from approximately 30\% at 10$\times$ to 45\% at 60$\times$ and was unaffected by the neural network (Fig.~\ref{fig:cophasing}b)\todo{clarify denominator: all GIAB indels or those called by Clair3}. Of the allele-specific 5mC sites identified by \texttt{modcall}, more than 99\% were phased at every coverage, and the neural network unphased 1.5--5\% of them, mostly at low coverage (Fig.~\ref{fig:cophasing}c). Roughly 68--70\% of heterozygous SVs called by Sniffles2 were phased, decreasing slightly with coverage as more low-support SVs were called, and correction removed a further 2 percentage points (Fig.~\ref{fig:cophasing}d).

\subsection*{Neural network correction reduces switch errors in every variant-class configuration}
The neural network reduced the SNV switch error rate in all five configurations and at every coverage (Fig.~\ref{fig:combinations}, left column). For SNV-only phasing the rate fell by 35--40\% (for example from 0.036\% to 0.022\% at 60$\times$); for SNV+indel and for all four classes it fell from 0.048\% to 0.027\% at 60$\times$ and from 0.156\% to 0.092\% at 10$\times$. Corrected co-phasing was therefore more accurate than uncorrected SNV-only phasing at every coverage while retaining substantially longer blocks (Fig.~\ref{fig:cophasing}e). The correction was even more effective on the Hamming distance of indel-containing configurations, which fell from 7.2\% to 3.3\% at 60$\times$ and from 12.5\% to 7.5\% at 12$\times$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:combinations}, middle column)\todo{confirm the 12$\times$ point: every other low-coverage value in this section is quoted at 10$\times$}, indicating that a large share of the indel-induced errors are long-range flips that the network recognizes from the surrounding vote structure. The price was a moderate reduction in block N50 for indel-containing configurations, from 4.3~Mb to 3.7~Mb (SNV+indel) and from 5.0~Mb to 4.1~Mb (all classes) at 60$\times$, because unphased variants that bridged two blocks split them; for configurations without indels the N50 was essentially unchanged. The trade-off therefore resolves in favour of co-phasing: even after correction, four-class co-phasing yielded blocks approximately 40\% longer than SNV-only phasing at 60$\times$ together with a lower switch error rate than uncorrected SNV-only phasing\todo{verify 4.1 vs 2.9~Mb}.

\subsection*{Unphased variants are predominantly unsupported heterozygous calls}
Most of what the network removes was never real phasing information. We classified every SNV that \toolname phased and the network subsequently unphased (SNV-only phasing, single replicate, seed 1) against the v5.0q benchmark (Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}). The network unphased approximately 60,000 SNVs at every coverage, but 86--96\% of them were calls that the benchmark does not support as heterozygous, either because the site is absent from the benchmark (80--94\%) or because it is homozygous there; only 4.0\% (2,411 variants) at 60$\times$ and 14\% at 10$\times$ were genuine heterozygous sites (Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}a).

Cross-tabulating each unphased variant by read depth and by phasing informativeness, the number of informative reads linking it to its neighbours, showed that genuine heterozygotes were removed overwhelmingly at normal depth with weak phasing evidence (61--81\%), whereas benchmark-absent calls shifted towards low depth as coverage increased (12.5\% at 10$\times$ to 58.6\% at 60$\times$), the signature of hard-to-map regions in which reads are lost to alternative loci (Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}b). No variant was unphased on the basis of high depth alone. Among the genuine heterozygotes that were removed, 43\% at 10$\times$ had a phasing entropy of exactly zero, that is, unanimous but sparse votes, compared with 17\% of benchmark-absent calls, and this share fell to 22\% at 60$\times$ as evidence accumulated (Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}c); entropy alone would therefore not have identified them, whereas the network, which sees the votes of all neighbours in the window, did\todo{Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}c also plots the PE $\ge 0.8$ category, which the text does not report; add the corresponding prevalences for genuine heterozygous and benchmark-absent calls, or drop the category from the panel}. Conversely, clustering within 50~bp and low genotype quality were present in 46--49\% of benchmark-absent calls at every coverage but were far less frequent among genuine heterozygotes, marking them as variant-caller artefacts rather than phasing failures.

The loss of true phasing information caused by correction is therefore small, about 2,400 SNVs at 60$\times$ out of \todo{N} phased, and most of what is removed are false heterozygous calls whose spurious phase would otherwise propagate into haplotagged reads and downstream haplotype-aware callers.
