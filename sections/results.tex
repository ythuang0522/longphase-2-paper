\subsection*{\toolname co-phases SNVs, indels, SVs and methylation in one graph and withholds phase it predicts to be wrong}
\toolname resolves haplotypes in three stages (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). All heterozygous evidence carried by the same long reads (SNVs, small indels, SVs and allele-specific 5mC sites called by the built-in \texttt{modcall} module) enters a single phasing graph per chromosome, in which each observation is weighted by base quality and by the reliability of its sequence context, down-weighting homopolymers, tandem repeats and copy-number-altered regions (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}a,b). Each phased variant receives a phasing-uncertainty score, and a graph neural network inspects a local window around every variant whose uncertainty is high (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}c). Variants it predicts to be misphased are unphased rather than flipped, a step we call GNN correction, and a haplotype block that becomes disconnected is split; genotypes are preserved and no new phase is introduced. The outputs are phased VCFs for every variant class and haplotagged alignments (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}d).

We evaluated \toolname on HG002 nanopore R10.4.1 data down-sampled to 10--60$\times$ and on HG002 PacBio HiFi data at 10--50$\times$ (Methods). Values at 10--20$\times$ are means of ten down-sampling replicates; at 30--60$\times$ and for HiFi data and the analyses of unphased variants, one down-sampling was analysed (Methods). Phasing was scored against the GRCh38-based GIAB v4.2.1 benchmark and against the benchmark derived from the complete diploid telomere-to-telomere (T2T) assembly of the same individual (v5.0q), which extends evaluation into segmental duplications, satellites and other regions that v4.2.1 excludes. Unless stated otherwise, results refer to v5.0q and to SNVs, the only class that every compared tool phases, and \toolname denotes the complete method, GNN correction included. We first compare \toolname with existing phasers and then analyse the contribution of its internal modules. Per-run values are in Supplementary Data~1 and Supplementary Tables~7--12.

\paragraph{The separation between phasers depends on the benchmark.}
Scored against GIAB v4.2.1, the three tools converged on 1,600--2,200 switch errors from 30$\times$ upwards, a ratio of 1.2--1.3 between the highest and lowest rate; scored against v5.0q, the same VCFs differed 4.3--4.8-fold (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmarks}a,b and Supplementary Table~9). \hapcut made more switch errors under v5.0q at every coverage and \whatshap at every coverage below 60$\times$ (both 1.6-fold more at 10$\times$), whereas \toolname made fewer (1,518 versus 1,820 at 10$\times$; 496 versus 1,676 at 60$\times$). Hamming distance was nearly unchanged between benchmarks (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmarks}c,d), consistent with the additional v5.0q switch errors being isolated switches; because error locations have not been intersected, the contributions of regional coverage, variant representation and phase discordance remain unresolved\todo{Score the regions and alleles common to both benchmarks separately from the benchmark-specific ones, and intersect switch-error locations across tools and benchmarks}.

\paragraph{With indels, \toolname phases SNVs 3.0- to 3.7-fold more accurately than \whatshap at similar contiguity.}

MethPhaser\cite{fu2024methphaser}, which adds methylation evidence to existing SNV blocks, left the block N50 and switch errors of the \toolname SNV phasing it was given unchanged, whereas joint SNV and 5mC phasing lengthened blocks by 8\% (Supplementary Fig.~11)\todo{The MethPhaser values in MethPhaserCompare.jsx are identical, to every printed digit and at all six coverages, to the SNV-only \toolname run without GNN correction given to MethPhaser as input (Supplementary Data~1, replicate~1). \paragraph{\toolname and \whatshap leave different SNVs unphased.}
\toolname left more heterozygous SNV calls unphased than \whatshap (210,683 versus 142,313 at 60$\times$), and the two sets overlapped only partly: 88,692 were left unphased by both, 121,991 by \toolname alone and 53,621 by \whatshap alone (Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}a). Each tool's set was enriched in genomic intervals containing switch disagreements between the other tool and the truth set, increasingly with coverage: 4.3- to 9.6-fold (10 to 60$\times$) for SNVs left unphased by \toolname alone and 2.0- to 9.5-fold for those left unphased by \whatshap alone (Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}b). This measures regional co-location, not site-level correctness. The two sets also differed in depth (Fig.~\ref{fig:unphased}c and Supplementary Fig.~12): at 60$\times$, SNVs left unphased by \whatshap alone had a median depth of 18$\times$, whereas 15.6\% of those left unphased by \toolname alone lay at the depth cap of the variant caller, a pile-up typical of reads mismapped from paralogous sequence\todo{the median depths and the zero-entropy share among \whatshap-only sites (57.6\%) are quoted from JHL's commit message of 2026-09-27 and are not in any committed table; add the per-set summary to Supplementary Data~1}.

\subsection*{Contribution of the internal modules}

\paragraph{GNN correction removes a third of the remaining switch errors at a small cost in contiguity.}
Without GNN correction, the graph algorithm alone had a switch error rate of 0.121\% at 10$\times$ and 0.034\% at 60$\times$, already 1.9--2.5-fold below \whatshap and 2.2--3.3-fold below \hapcut (Supplementary Fig.~13 and Supplementary Table~8). GNN correction lowered it by a further 31--33\% at every coverage, at a cost of 0.1--0.3 percentage points of phased SNVs and of 5--9\% of block N50 at 10--20$\times$ and 2--5\% above. On HiFi data it removed 19--28\% of the switch errors at a cost of 0.1 percentage points of phased SNVs and 1--2\% of block N50 (Supplementary Fig.~13e,f).

\paragraph{Methylation lengthens blocks at a similar error rate, and indels trade accuracy for contiguity.}
We phased seven combinations of variant classes, each with and without GNN correction (Supplementary Figs.~14 and~15 and Supplementary Table~10). Without GNN correction, adding 5mC lengthened the 60$\times$ block N50 by 8\%, SVs by 1\%, indels by 49\% and all three by 75\% (2.91 to 5.10~Mb). Only indels raised the SNV switch error rate, from 0.034\% to 0.043\% at 60$\times$ and from 0.121\% to 0.155\% at 10$\times$\todo{For configurations that phase indels, \texttt{compare} computes the Hamming distance over all common heterozygous variants, indels included (\texttt{CompareProcess.cpp}:579), whereas switch errors are SNV-only.
