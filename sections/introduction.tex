Haplotype phasing, the assignment of heterozygous variants to their parental chromosomes, is essential for interpreting the diploid human genome. Whether two variants act in \textit{cis} or in \textit{trans} decides compound heterozygosity in recessive disease\cite{eisfeldt2025clinical}, and allele-specific methylation on a phased haplotype reveals its parent of origin\cite{akbari2023parentoforigin}. Long-read sequencing has made read-based phasing practical at clinical and population scale. Individual reads span tens to hundreds of kilobases, link distant heterozygous sites directly and carry native base-modification signals; a single library thus yields haplotype blocks orders of magnitude longer than short reads allow\cite{logsdon2020longread,kolmogorov2023scalable}. Read-based phasing also needs nothing beyond the sample's own reads: no sequencing of the parents, no population reference panel of the kind statistical phasing depends on\cite{hofmeister2023shapeit5}, and none of the additional Hi-C or Strand-seq libraries otherwise used to phase whole chromosomes\cite{porubsky2021fully}.

Read-based phasing is classically formulated as minimum error correction and solved by dynamic programming or graph partitioning, as in \whatshap\cite{patterson2015whatshap}, \hapcut\cite{edge2017hapcut2} and Margin\cite{shafin2021margin}. These methods centre on single-nucleotide variants (SNVs), although \whatshap, by re-aligning each read to both alleles, also co-phases small and large indels with them\cite{martin2016whatshap}. We previously introduced \longphase\cite{lin2022longphase}, which recasts phasing as a weighted allele graph, resolves haplotypes at chromosome scale and co-phases SNVs with structural variants (SVs)\cite{smolka2024sniffles2,jiang2020cutesv}. \whatshap and \longphase now sit beneath much of the long-read analysis stack. Clair3 phases with \whatshap by default and with \longphase as an option, and the somatic callers ClairS and ClairS-TO use \longphase by default\cite{zheng2022clair3,zheng2026accelerated,zheng2026clairs,chen2025clairsto}; the EPI2ME wf-human-variation workflow phases every nanopore genome with \longphase\cite{ont2025wfhuman}; and the SV callers Sniffles2 and Severus take as input the haplotagged alignments that either phaser writes\cite{smolka2024sniffles2,keskus2025severus}. An error made by the phaser is therefore inherited, without any warning, by every analysis downstream.

Deployment at this scale has exposed three limitations of current phasers. The first is that 5-methylcytosine (5mC), which long reads report alongside the sequence, plays no part in deciding the phase. Where the two haplotypes differ in methylation, as at imprinted loci and at sites of sequence-dependent allele-specific methylation, a read's CpG states tie it to one haplotype even where it spans no heterozygous variant, which is where sequence-based blocks end\cite{akbari2021nanomethphase,fu2024methphaser}. Yet methylation-aware tools use that signal only once a sequence phase exists. NanoMethPhase assigns methylation to haplotypes that \whatshap has already resolved\cite{akbari2021nanomethphase}; \methphaser and HapBridge learn haplotype-specific CpGs from the reads an SNV phaser has tagged, then join adjacent blocks or flag suspected switches\cite{fu2024methphaser,chen2025hapbridge}; and LongHap, which co-phases SNVs, indels and SVs, adds methylation only to bridge the gaps its sequence phasing leaves\cite{pfennig2026longhap}. Because the informative CpGs are defined by the preceding sequence phase, its errors propagate into the methylation evidence, and methylation can revise a phase only after it is built. Allele-specific methylation is moreover confined to a small fraction of CpGs, varies between cells and tissues, and is read as a per-molecule probability rather than a base\cite{akbari2021nanomethphase,fu2024methphaser}; such sparse, noisy evidence is best weighed together with the sequence alleles on the same reads.

The second limitation is that read evidence is weighted uniformly even though the errors that break haplotypes are not uniformly distributed. Base-calling errors concentrate in homopolymers and tandem repeats, reads misplaced in segmental duplications create spurious linkages, and heterozygous calls in copy-number-altered regions mislead the graph\cite{olson2023benchmarking,dwarshuis2024stratifications}. What follows is the switch error, a flip of haplotype assignment within a block that inverts every inference downstream, from compound-heterozygosity calls to haplotype-aware somatic detection\cite{zheng2026clairs,keskus2025severus}.

The third limitation is that phase is mainly reported as a binary state. Statistical phasers have long attached a posterior probability to every phased genotype\cite{hofmeister2023shapeit5}, and \hapcut prunes variants whose posterior falls below a threshold\cite{edge2017hapcut2}. That posterior, however, comes from the model that made the phase and assumes correctly placed reads; it cannot flag errors from mis-mapped reads or collapsed paralogues. \whatshap and Margin report no confidence, and \longphase reports only simple read support, the number of a read's alleles on each haplotype and a Phred-scaled tagging quality derived from them, not the reliability of each variant's phase. Learning-based phasers, built on graph neural networks or deep reinforcement learning\cite{xue2022neurhap,battistella2025ralphi}, do not close this gap. They learn, partly from simulated reads, to optimize the same fragment-cut or minimum-error-correction objective as heuristic phasers, phase SNVs only and report no confidence, and their authors note that errors from mis-alignment in repetitive regions remain unresolved\cite{battistella2025ralphi}.

These limitations have been underestimated, because phasers are benchmarked against Genome in a Bottle (GIAB) truth sets built largely by mapping reads to GRCh38\cite{zook2019giab,wagner2022giab}, which can assess only the regions where mapping-based methods already work\cite{hansen2026q100}. The HG002 v4.2.1 release omits both sex chromosomes and 12\% of the autosomes, mostly satellite arrays and segmental duplications\cite{wagner2022giab,hansen2026q100}, where mis-mapped reads and collapsed paralogues create false linkages\cite{olson2023benchmarking,dwarshuis2024stratifications}. Its phase is also partial and partly circular: it was recorded only for the 74\% of heterozygous variants on which trio-based phasing agreed with Strand-seq and HiFi phasing by \whatshap\cite{wagner2022giab}, leaving the variants hardest for a read-based phaser the least likely to be scored. Extending benchmarks into difficult sequence has changed conclusions before. GIAB's growth from 85\% to 92\% of GRCh38 exposed eight times more false negatives in a short-read call set\cite{wagner2022giab}, and the complete CHM13 reference removed tens of thousands of spurious variant calls per sample\cite{aganezov2022complete}. The complete diploid T2T-HG002 assembly now lifts this ceiling for phasing\cite{hansen2026q100}. It resolves both haplotypes of every chromosome, adds 701~Mb of autosomal sequence absent from v4.2.1 and phases every heterozygous site. Projected onto GRCh38\cite{giab2025q100}, it provides a benchmark on which phasers can be compared where they are most likely to fail.

Here we present \toolname, which addresses each of these limitations. It places 5mC sites in the same weighted phasing graph as SNVs, small indels and SVs, letting methylation inform the phase while it is being built. It weights every read observation by base quality and sequence context, giving less weight to homopolymers, tandem repeats and copy-number-altered regions. It scores the phasing uncertainty of every variant, and an optional graph neural network unphases the variants it predicts to be misphased, leaving their genotypes unchanged. We evaluate it against both GIAB v4.2.1 and the T2T-HG002-derived benchmark, and it runs as a drop-in replacement for \longphase in existing pipelines.
