Haplotype phasing, the assignment of heterozygous variants to their parental chromosomes, is essential for interpreting the diploid human genome. Whether two variants act in \textit{cis} or in \textit{trans} decides compound heterozygosity in recessive disease\cite{eisfeldt2025clinical}, and allele-specific methylation on a phased haplotype reveals its parent of origin\cite{akbari2023parentoforigin}. Long-read sequencing has made read-based phasing practical at clinical and population scale. Individual reads span tens to hundreds of kilobases, link distant heterozygous sites directly and carry native base-modification signals, so a single library yields haplotype blocks orders of magnitude longer than short reads allow\cite{logsdon2020longread,kolmogorov2023scalable}. One sample suffices, without the trios, chromosome-conformation or single-cell strand data that chromosome-scale phasing otherwise requires\cite{porubsky2021fully}, and without the reference panels on which statistical phasing depends\cite{hofmeister2023shapeit5}.

Read-based phasing is classically formulated as minimum error correction and solved by dynamic programming or graph partitioning, as in \whatshap\cite{patterson2015whatshap}, \hapcut\cite{edge2017hapcut2} and Margin\cite{shafin2021margin}, all of which were designed for single-nucleotide variants (SNVs). We previously introduced \longphase\cite{lin2022longphase}, which recasts phasing as a weighted allele graph, resolves haplotypes at chromosome scale and co-phases SNVs with structural variants (SVs)\cite{smolka2024sniffles2,jiang2020cutesv}. Much of the long-read analysis stack now rests on that graph. Clair3 can use \longphase, and the somatic callers ClairS and ClairS-TO use it by default, for the phasing that precedes variant calling\cite{zheng2022clair3,zheng2026accelerated,zheng2026clairs,chen2025clairsto}; the EPI2ME wf-human-variation workflow phases every nanopore genome with it\cite{ont2025wfhuman}; the SV caller Severus consumes its haplotagged alignments\cite{keskus2025severus}; and published work on cancer evolution, rapid rare-disease diagnostics and imprinting inherits its haplotype blocks\cite{gabbutt2025fluctuating,oneill2024longread,laflamme2024diagnostic,iwasaki2025gnas,bioinformatics2026primer} (Supplementary Table~6). An error made by the phaser is therefore inherited, without any warning, by every analysis downstream.

Deployment at this scale has exposed three limitations of current phasers. The first concerns evidence beyond SNVs. Small indels, SVs and 5-methylcytosine (5mC) each carry haplotype linkage that SNVs cannot supply, and that linkage matters most where heterozygosity is low and blocks terminate. Methylation-aware methods such as NanoMethPhase\cite{akbari2021nanomethphase} and \methphaser\cite{fu2024methphaser} nevertheless operate as post-processing on an SNV phaser's output, published joint phasing of indels and SVs has been restricted to high-accuracy HiFi reads\cite{holt2024hiphase}, and no published method places all four evidence classes in one model. Nor is the extra evidence free: nanopore indel calls are markedly less accurate than SNV calls\cite{zheng2022clair3}, so unweighted indel links can introduce errors as well as contiguity. The second limitation, which compounds the first, is that read evidence is weighted uniformly even though the errors that break haplotypes are not uniformly distributed. Base-calling errors concentrate in homopolymers and tandem repeats, reads misplaced in segmental duplications create spurious linkages, and heterozygous calls in copy-number-altered regions mislead the graph\cite{olson2023benchmarking,dwarshuis2024stratifications}. What follows is the switch error, a flip of haplotype assignment within a block that inverts every inference downstream, from compound-heterozygosity calls to haplotype-aware somatic detection\cite{zheng2026clairs,keskus2025severus}.

The third limitation is that phase is reported as a binary state. Statistical phasers have long attached a posterior probability to every phased genotype\cite{hofmeister2023shapeit5}, and \hapcut prunes variants whose posterior falls below a threshold\cite{edge2017hapcut2}. That posterior, however, comes from the same likelihood that produced the phase, a model that treats reads as independent and correctly placed; it is not designed to detect the correlated errors that mis-mapped reads and collapsed paralogues introduce. \whatshap, Margin and \longphase report no confidence at all, leaving a phase supported by many concordant reads indistinguishable from one supported by two conflicting reads. Learning-based phasers\cite{battistella2025ralphi,luo2024gcphase} replace the haplotype-assembly algorithm rather than diagnose where phasing goes wrong, and handle only one variant class.

These limitations have been easy to underestimate, because phasers are benchmarked against GRCh38-based Genome in a Bottle (GIAB) truth sets\cite{zook2019giab} that exclude most segmental duplications and satellite sequence\cite{olson2023benchmarking,dwarshuis2024stratifications}, the regions in which uniform evidence weighting is expected to fail. The complete diploid assembly of HG002\cite{hansen2026q100} now provides a truth set that assesses those regions\cite{giab2025q100}, much as the complete CHM13 reference did for variant calling\cite{aganezov2022complete}.

Here we present \toolname, a germline phaser that co-phases SNVs, small indels, SVs and 5mC in one weighted phasing graph and weights each read observation by base quality and sequence context. Every phased variant receives an entropy-based uncertainty score, and a graph neural network examines the phasing graph around the most uncertain variants; variants it predicts to be misphased are unphased instead of flipped, so genotypes are preserved (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). On HG002 nanopore data at 10--60$\times$, scored against the T2T-HG002-derived benchmark, \toolname with neural-network correction makes 2.6- to 5-fold fewer SNV switch errors than \whatshap and \hapcut, a margin that narrows to 1.1- to 1.3-fold under GIAB v4.2.1, and runs six- to eightfold faster at 60$\times$. Co-phasing all four variant classes lengthens haplotype blocks by 40\% at 60$\times$ while making fewer switch errors than uncorrected SNV-only phasing. \toolname is a drop-in replacement for the \longphase versions deployed in Clair3, ClairS, Severus and the EPI2ME workflows, and the neural-network correction is an optional sub-command, so those pipelines gain the new phasing without any change to their interfaces.
