Haplotype phasing, the assignment of heterozygous variants to their parental chromosomes, is essential for interpreting the diploid human genome. Whether two variants act in \textit{cis} or in \textit{trans} decides compound heterozygosity in recessive disease\cite{eisfeldt2025clinical}. Allele-specific expression and methylation report imprinting and \textit{cis}-regulatory variation, and the haplotype context of somatic mutations reveals clonal structure and loss of heterozygosity in tumours. Long-read sequencing has made read-based phasing practical at clinical and population scale\cite{logsdon2020longread,kolmogorov2023scalable}. Individual reads span tens to hundreds of kilobases, link distant heterozygous sites directly and carry native base-modification signals, so the haplotype blocks recovered from a single library are orders of magnitude longer than short reads allow. One sample suffices, without the trios, chromosome-conformation or single-cell strand data that chromosome-scale phasing otherwise requires\cite{porubsky2021fully}, and without the reference panels on which statistical phasing depends\cite{hofmeister2023shapeit5}.

Read-based phasing is classically formulated as minimum error correction and solved by dynamic programming or graph partitioning, as in \whatshap\cite{patterson2015whatshap}, \hapcut\cite{edge2017hapcut2} and Margin\cite{shafin2021margin}, all of which were designed for single-nucleotide variants (SNVs). We previously introduced \longphase\cite{lin2022longphase}, which recasts phasing as a weighted allele graph, resolves haplotypes at chromosome scale and co-phases SNVs with structural variants (SVs)\cite{smolka2024sniffles2,jiang2020cutesv}. Much of the long-read analysis stack now rests on that graph. Clair3 distributes \longphase alongside \whatshap and runs with either for the intermediate phasing that precedes full-alignment calling\cite{zheng2022clair3,zheng2026accelerated}. The somatic callers ClairS and ClairS-TO default to \longphase for the germline phasing that precedes somatic calling\cite{zheng2026clairs,chen2025clairsto}, the EPI2ME wf-human-variation workflow phases every nanopore genome with the same binary\cite{ont2025wfhuman}, and the somatic SV caller Severus consumes haplotagged alignments from either \longphase or \whatshap\cite{keskus2025severus}. Published work on cancer evolution, rapid rare-disease diagnostics and imprinting inherits these haplotype blocks\cite{gabbutt2025fluctuating,oneill2024longread,laflamme2024diagnostic,iwasaki2025gnas,bioinformatics2026primer} (Supplementary Table~6), and new phasing algorithms are routinely benchmarked against \longphase\cite{holt2024hiphase,battistella2025ralphi,luo2024gcphase}. An error made by the phaser is therefore inherited, without any warning, by every analysis downstream.

Deployment at this scale has exposed three limitations shared by current phasers. The first is an SNV-centric design. Small indels, SVs and 5-methylcytosine (5mC) each carry haplotype linkage that SNVs cannot supply, and that linkage matters most where heterozygosity is low and blocks terminate. Methylation-aware methods such as NanoMethPhase\cite{akbari2021nanomethphase} and \methphaser\cite{fu2024methphaser} nevertheless operate as post-processing on an SNV phaser's output, joint phasing of small and structural variants has been restricted to high-accuracy HiFi reads\cite{holt2024hiphase}, and no method places all four evidence classes in one model. The second limitation is uniform weighting of read evidence, even though the errors that break haplotypes are not uniformly distributed. Base-calling errors concentrate in homopolymers and tandem repeats, misplaced reads in segmental duplications create spurious linkages, and heterozygous calls in copy-number-altered regions systematically mislead the graph. What follows is the switch error, a flip of haplotype assignment within a block that inverts every inference downstream, from compound-heterozygosity calls to haplotype-aware somatic detection\cite{zheng2026clairs,keskus2025severus}.

The third limitation is that phase is reported as a binary state. Statistical phasers have long attached a posterior probability to every phased genotype\cite{hofmeister2023shapeit5}, and \hapcut prunes variants whose posterior falls below a threshold\cite{edge2017hapcut2}. That posterior, however, comes from the same likelihood that produced the phase, a model that treats reads as independent and correctly placed and so cannot flag the correlated errors introduced by mis-mapped reads and collapsed paralogues. \whatshap, Margin and \longphase report no confidence at all, leaving a phase supported by many concordant reads indistinguishable from one supported by two conflicting reads. Learning-based phasers\cite{battistella2025ralphi,luo2024gcphase} replace the haplotype-assembly algorithm rather than diagnose where phasing goes wrong, and handle only one variant class. Underestimating these limitations has been easy, because phasers are benchmarked against GRCh38-based Genome in a Bottle (GIAB) truth sets\cite{zook2019giab} that exclude most segmental duplications and satellite sequence\cite{olson2023benchmarking,dwarshuis2024stratifications}, exactly the regions in which uniform evidence weighting fails. The complete diploid assembly of HG002\cite{hansen2026q100} now provides a truth set that does assess those regions\cite{giab2025q100}, much as the complete CHM13 reference did for variant calling\cite{aganezov2022complete}.

Here we present \toolname, a germline phaser that co-phases SNVs, small indels, SVs and 5mC in one weighted phasing graph and calibrates each read observation by base quality and sequence context. A graph neural network then scores the reliability of every phased variant; variants predicted to be misphased are unphased instead of flipped, so genotypes are preserved (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). On HG002 nanopore data at 10--60$\times$, \toolname makes three- to fivefold fewer switch errors than \whatshap and \hapcut, runs six- to eightfold faster and lengthens haplotype blocks by up to 75\%. Scoring the same output against a telomere-to-telomere truth set exposes most of the errors that GRCh38-based benchmarks conceal. A drop-in replacement for the \longphase versions deployed in Clair3, ClairS, Severus and the EPI2ME workflows, \toolname reaches those pipelines without any change to their interfaces.
