\subsection*{Supplementary Method 1. Allele-specific methylation calling (\code{modcall})}

Methods gives the genotyping criterion for a candidate CpG and the co-segregation criterion that a linked pair must satisfy. This section gives the parts of \code{modcall} that decide which candidates survive to the phasing graph.

\paragraph{Linking modes and the depth bound on the neighbour scan.} In \emph{SNV-anchored} mode, used whenever an SNV VCF was supplied, a candidate CpG was accepted as a strong anchor if it satisfied the co-segregation criterion of Methods against an SNV among the next 20 variants (\code{--connectAdjacent}). In \emph{methylation-only} mode, used where no informative SNV is in range, which is where methylation is most valuable for bridging, the same criterion was applied between two candidate CpGs. In both modes a pair was considered only while the number of reads spanning it exceeded $\max(6,\,(n_1+n_2)/4)$, where $n_1$ and $n_2$ are the numbers of reads recorded at the two sites; a pair falling to or below that bound terminates the neighbour scan for the current site rather than merely being skipped, so that support is never accumulated across a sparsely covered gap. Candidates never satisfying that criterion were held back as weak points and admitted only if they satisfied that criterion on at least six reads against an already accepted CpG, in an iterative expansion of two rounds (\code{--iterCount}, default 2).

\paragraph{Merging runs of CpGs.} Runs of consecutive CpG coordinates are merged into a single locus represented by the first position. The reason is practical. 5mC is called on one strand at a time, so a CpG contributes methylated reads on the forward strand and unmethylated-looking reads on the reverse; merging consolidates both strands of a run into one locus carrying one read list, which removes the need for \code{phase} to reason about strand at all and lets methylation loci be voted on concurrently with SNVs by the unmodified graph algorithm. The resulting VCF records the methylated and unmethylated read names (\code{MR}, \code{NR}), the read strand and the per-site depths, and is passed to \code{phase} through \code{--mod-file}.

\subsection*{Supplementary Method 2. Read-level allele observations and copy-number interval detection}

\paragraph{Per-class allele extraction.} SNV alleles were read from the aligned base and inherited its Phred base quality. Insertion and deletion alleles were called from the CIGAR string: a read supports the ALT allele if a CIGAR insertion or deletion begins immediately after the variant position, and the REF allele otherwise; when a read deletion spans an SNV in a homopolymer of length $\ge 3$, the SNV allele was recovered from the base following the deletion. SV alleles were assigned by scanning a window of 20 CIGAR operations (\code{--svWindow}) around the SV start for an insertion or deletion whose length is within 10\% of the SV length (\code{--svThreshold}); reads listed by the SV caller as supporting the SV were also assigned the ALT allele. 5mC alleles were taken from the \code{modcall} read lists (methylated~$=$~allele~0, unmethylated~$=$~allele~1) restricted to reads whose strand matched the recorded strand. Soft- and hard-clipping operations longer than 5~bp were counted per reference position, separately for clips at the front (left) and back (right) end of an alignment, as evidence of alignment breakpoints for copy-number detection (below).

The copy-number-aware filter (Methods; Supplementary Fig.~1e,f) needs the boundaries of segments that recruit reads from more than one locus. Those boundaries appear in the alignments as clusters of soft- and hard-clips, and \code{longphase phase} finds them with a scanner that walks the per-position clip counts of one chromosome in coordinate order.

\paragraph{Input.} For every reference position at which at least one alignment carries a clipping operation longer than 5~bp, two counts are recorded: $u$, the number of alignments clipped at their front (left) end, and $d$, the number clipped at their back (right) end. Positions with no clip are absent from the map. A sentinel position 30~kb beyond the last observed position is appended before the scan and removed afterwards, so that an interval left open at the end of the chromosome is still closed.

\paragraph{State.} The scanner is in one of three states: \emph{idle}, \emph{step} (a copy-number transition, entered on a strong front-clip cluster) and \emph{ramp} (the progressively nested breakpoints left by breakage--fusion--bridge cycles, entered on a weak front-clip excess). In the two active states it carries a running excess $c$ of front- over back-clips, a candidate start, a candidate end and three adaptive thresholds derived from the front-clip count $n$ that opened or re-anchored the interval:
\[
r = n, \qquad
p = \begin{cases} \lfloor n/2 \rfloor, & n \ge 10\\ 5, & n < 10\end{cases},
\]
\[
f = \begin{cases} 5, & n \ge 20\\ \lfloor n/4 \rfloor, & 10 \le n < 20\\ 2, & n < 10, \end{cases}
\]
where $r$ is the re-anchor threshold, $p$ the pull-down threshold and $f$ the decay floor.

\paragraph{Transitions.} With constants $\Pi = 5$ (push threshold), $A = 30{,}000$~bp (look-ahead), $E = 30$ (extend threshold), $\Lambda = 200{,}000$~bp (maximum interval length) and $\Sigma = 20$ (ramp commit threshold), the scanner at position $x$ with counts $(u,d)$ behaves as follows:

{\small
\begin{verbatim}
idle:
  if u >= PI and c == 0:
      enter STEP;  c = u - d;  start = x;  end = x + A
      set r, p, f from u
  elif u > d and c == 0:
      enter RAMP;  c = u - d;  start = x;  end = x + A

step:
  if u > r:                      # stronger breakpoint: re-anchor
      start = x;  end = x + A;  set r, p, f from u
  c += u - d
  if c > E:  end = x + A
  if d >= p:                     # pull-down reached
      emit [start, x];  reset to idle
  elif c <= f and x <= end:      # decayed to the floor in range
      emit [start, x];  reset to idle
  if x > end or c <= 0 or x - start >= LAMBDA:
      reset to idle              # candidate discarded

ramp:
  if (c > SIGMA and d >= c/4) or (c <= SIGMA and d >= PI):
      emit [start, x];  reset to idle
  elif u >= PI:                  # promote to STEP
      c = u - d;  start = x;  end = x + A;  set r, p, f from u
  else:
      c += u - d
      if c > E:  end = x + A
      if x > end or c <= 0 or x - start >= LAMBDA:
          reset to idle          # candidate discarded
\end{verbatim}
}

Two behaviours are worth stating explicitly because they are not evident from the outline in the main Methods. First, a \emph{step} interval is re-anchored, not merely extended, when a stronger front-clip cluster than the one that opened it is met: the candidate start moves to the new position and the three thresholds are recomputed, so the interval reported is the one that begins at the strongest breakpoint. Second, the decay-floor exit applies only while the scan is still inside the look-ahead window ($x \le \text{end}$); beyond it the interval is discarded rather than emitted, so a candidate that decays slowly over a long distance yields nothing.

\paragraph{Site removal.} Within each emitted interval the mismatch-rate statistic $\mathrm{MR}(v)$ defined in Methods is computed and heterozygous variants with $\mathrm{MR}(v) \ge 0.7$ are removed from all reads. A variant is scored only when both allele populations have a non-zero mean load, so a site at which one population carries no other alternate allele is never removed on this basis.

\paragraph{Why $\mathrm{MR}$ separates the two cases.} The statistic distinguishes the two situations of interest. At a genuine heterozygous site both allele populations are ordinary reads of the locus and carry comparable loads, so $\mathrm{MR} \approx 0.5$. At a paralogue-specific difference the alternate allele is carried by reads that originate elsewhere and therefore mismatch the reference at many other sites in the same interval as well, which drives $\mathrm{MR}$ towards~1.

\subsection*{Supplementary Method 3. Calibration of the voting and read-filtering constants}

The constants in this section were set empirically. Writing the ambiguity of a pair as $s = \min(P,Q)/\max(P,Q)$, which ranges from 0 for unanimous support to 1 for an even split, the voting rule has three levels: a pair votes at weight $w = 20$ when it is near-certain, that is, when $s \le \alpha = 0.1$ with total support $\ge 1$ or, irrespective of $s$, when one pairing has accumulated less than 1 unit of support while the other has at least 1. The pair votes at unit weight otherwise, and does not vote at all when $s > \beta = 0.7$. Each of $\alpha$, $\beta$, $w$, the base-quality threshold $\theta = 12$ and the single-read guard parameters ($N = 3$ votes, ambiguity cut $\mu = 0.2$) was fixed by a sweep on HG002 at 10--60$\times$ before the experiments reported here, and none was re-tuned afterwards. \todo{Add these sweeps as a Supplementary Figure (see Supplementary Fig.~9), and state here whether the tuning data are disjoint from the evaluation data reported in Figs.~2--6, or overlap them. This matters: five constants tuned on HG002 and then evaluated on HG002 is the same circularity objection the GNN faces, and a reviewer who notices it for one will apply it to the other. If the sweeps used the same HG002 alignments, say so plainly and argue why five scalar thresholds chosen from coarse grids do not constitute fitting.}

\subsection*{Supplementary Method 4. Graph neural network: features, architecture, labels, training and export}

This section expands the outline given in Methods. Supplementary Tables~1 and~2 list the node and edge features with their exact definitions, Supplementary Table~3 the architecture and inference hyperparameters, Supplementary Table~4 the training configuration, and Supplementary Figs.~4--6 illustrate window construction and the layer.

\paragraph{Node features.} Each allele node carried 31 features in four groups (Supplementary Tables~1 and~2; architecture in Supplementary Table~3 and Supplementary Fig.~6). \emph{Phasing evidence}: $\mathrm{PE}$; the allele's genotype index; the reference and alternate genotype indices; whether the variant is the window trigger; whether it shares the trigger's phase set; the allele index; the signed position offset from the trigger normalized by the largest absolute offset in the window; $h_1$, $h_2$, $h_1+h_2$, the vote ratio $\max(h_1,h_2)/(h_1+h_2)$; $\log_2(1+h_1+h_2)$, $\ln(1+h_1)$, $\ln(1+h_2)$; and $\log_2$ of the total vote weight divided by its mean over the 21 variants centred on the variant. Here $h_1$ and $h_2$ were taken from the first outgoing DOT edge of each allele when one existed and from \code{INFO/H1}, \code{INFO/H2} otherwise. (Supplementary Table~1 lists the features by group.) \emph{Genomic context} (computed from the reference when \code{-r} is given, and zero otherwise): homopolymer length at the variant (capped at 20); GC content in $\pm 50$~bp; the longest tandem-repeat unit count with unit size 2--4 in $\pm 15$~bp, divided by 10 and capped at 1; Shannon entropy of the $\pm 15$~bp sequence; the number of variants within $\pm 500$~bp, minus one and divided by 20; and $\log_{10}(1+d)$ for the distance $d$ to the nearest indel, capped at $\log_{10}(10001)$. \emph{Graph structure}: the number of distinct target positions of the variant's outgoing DOT edges, divided by 10 and capped at 1; $\ln(1+n_{\mathrm{PS}})/\ln(101)$ for the number $n_{\mathrm{PS}}$ of variants in the variant's phase set; $\log_{10}(1+|\text{offset}|)$ from the trigger; the standard deviation of the variant's outgoing DOT edge weights, divided by 100 and capped at 1; and an indicator of whether the variant is a bridge vertex, i.e.\ whether deleting it increases the number of connected components of the position-level, undirected window graph. \emph{Variant class}: four one-hot indicators (SNV, indel, SV, 5mC), the SNV/indel distinction being made on $|\,|\mathrm{ALT}|-|\mathrm{REF}|\,|>0$.

\paragraph{Edge features.} Edges were taken from the DOT graph, restricted to pairs both of whose endpoints lie inside the window, symmetrized by copying the features of whichever direction was present, and augmented with a self-loop per node whose features are the mean of its non-self incident edges. Each edge carried six features: the logistic-transformed vote weight $1/(1+e^{-w})$; $\log_{10}(1+\text{genomic distance})$; $\ln(1+w)$; whether the two endpoints carry the same allele index; whether the two variants belong to different phase sets; and $w$ divided by the total outgoing weight of the source allele node within the window. \todo{Earlier drafts glossed the fourth feature as ``whether the two alleles are on the same haplotype''. It is an allele-index match, not a haplotype match (\code{GNNProcess.cpp}:481, \code{e.src\_allele == e.dst\_allele}); corrected above, and Supplementary Table 2 already states it correctly.} \todo{Stale code comment, harmless but confusing to reimplementers: the header of \code{GNNModel.h} (lines 6--7 and 43) describes the edge tensor as \code{[N, N, 7]} and the per-layer edge encoder as \code{[7,128]}, whereas \code{kEdgeFeat} is 6 (\code{GNNWeights.h}:21) and six features are what the code writes. Fix the comment.}

\paragraph{Architecture.} The network follows the general, powerful, scalable (GPS) graph-transformer recipe\cite{rampasek2022gps}. Node features were standardized with a batch-normalization layer whose statistics are frozen at inference, linearly projected to a 128-dimensional embedding and passed through a GELU non-linearity. Four identical layers then each combine a local and a global branch. The local branch is a four-head GATv2 message-passing layer\cite{velickovic2018gat,brody2022gatv2} in which the attention logit of head $t$ for edge $j \rightarrow i$ is
\begin{equation}
\alpha^{(t)}_{ij} \;\propto\; \exp\!\Big(\mathbf{a}_t^{\top}\,\mathrm{LeakyReLU}_{0.2}\big(\big[\mathbf{W}_r \mathbf{x}_i + \mathbf{W}_l \mathbf{x}_j + \mathbf{W}_e \mathbf{e}_{ij}\big]_t\big)\Big),
\end{equation}
where $[\cdot]_t$ selects the 32 coordinates of head $t$, $\mathbf{W}_e$ is a per-layer edge encoder without bias and the normalization is a softmax over the incoming edges of $i$ (a node with no incoming edge instead receives the uniform average of $\mathbf{W}_l\mathbf{x}_j$ over all window nodes); the head outputs are concatenated and a bias is added. The global branch is four-head scaled dot-product self-attention over all nodes of the window\cite{vaswani2017attention}, with no masking. The two branch outputs are added to the residual stream, followed by layer normalization, a two-layer feed-forward block (128$\rightarrow$256$\rightarrow$128, GELU) with a second residual connection and layer normalization. The final node embedding is concatenated with two edge-context scalars, the sum and the non-negative maximum, over the node's incoming edges excluding its self-loop, of the first edge feature, i.e.\ of the logistic-transformed vote weight $1/(1+e^{-w})$ rather than of $w$ itself. The pooled vector is then passed through a two-layer classifier (130$\rightarrow$128$\rightarrow$2) with softmax output.

The model comprises 687,358 float32 values (2,749,432~bytes), namely 124 in the input batch-normalization layer (scale, shift and the two frozen running statistics over 31 features), 4,096 in the input projection, 166,528 in each of the four GPS layers, and 17,026 in the classifier. Inference is implemented in plain C++ with dense $N\times N$ adjacency and edge tensors and no external runtime dependencies; the exact erf-based GELU must be used, because the tanh approximation changes the output by $\sim 10^{-3}$ and does not reproduce the reference implementation. Predictions for the two allele nodes of a variant were averaged. Only variants in the central zone of each window were retained, that is those whose normalized absolute offset $r$ from the trigger satisfies $r \le \min\!\big(1,\,10/\max(1,\mathrm{round}(20\,r_{\max}))\big)$, where $r_{\max}$ is the largest normalized absolute offset in the window; because offsets are normalized by that same largest offset, $r_{\max}=1$ for every window containing a variant more than 1~bp from the trigger, so in practice the retained zone is the central half of the window's genomic span. A variant covered by several windows received the running mean of its predictions. The same rule, with the same constant 10, defines which nodes carry a training loss, so the zone scored at inference is the zone the network was trained to score; nodes outside that zone are read as context but never scored. Because $r_{\max} \equiv 1$ in every window that contains a variant more than 1~bp from the trigger, the expression collapses to the constant 0.5 in practice and the $r_{\max}$ dependence is inert. \todo{The criterion is written on the genomic-distance ratio $r$, but the form of the constant (\code{center\_k}$/20$, with 20 the half-window in variants) suggests a variant-index criterion was intended (the central $\pm 10$ variants of the $\pm 20$-variant window); the two coincide only under uniform variant spacing. Either restate the intent and keep the code, or replace both sides with the index form and re-run; do not leave the dead $r_{\max}$ term in the published description.}

\paragraph{Training augmentations.} Two label-preserving augmentations were applied to training windows only, each independently with probability 0.5: a haplotype swap, which exchanges the two allele nodes of every variant and complements the HP feature, an operation the phasing problem is invariant under; and a coverage down-scaling, which multiplies $h_1$, $h_2$, their sum, the outgoing-weight standard deviation and the raw edge weights by a factor drawn uniformly from $[0.4, 0.7]$ and recomputes the derived logarithmic features, applied only to windows containing at least one error node.

\paragraph{Labels.} Labels were derived from the v5.0q benchmark restricted to chr1--chr22 small variants, the same truth set used for evaluation, and describe the phase of a variant relative to its neighbours rather than in absolute terms. Within each window the variants were grouped by phase set; in each group the first variant present in the benchmark fixed the orientation of that group and was labelled \emph{correct}, and every later variant was labelled \emph{correct} if its allele-to-haplotype orientation agreed with that anchor and \emph{disagreeing} otherwise. Maximal runs of consecutive disagreeing variants were then resolved so that a run is charged to the event that created the run, not to each member. A run of length one became \emph{unphase} if that variant's PE was at least 0.9 and \emph{flip} otherwise, whereas a run of length two or more had its first variant, the switch point, relabelled \emph{break}, and all remaining members reset to \emph{correct}. Variants absent from the benchmark, and all SV and 5mC records, for which the benchmark carries no phase truth, were left unlabelled: they are masked from the loss but remain in the graph as context, which is why a four-class window can be scored at all. The released model was trained in binary mode, in which \emph{flip}, \emph{unphase} and \emph{break} are merged into a single \emph{error} class; this matches the single action the module is allowed to take. Only labelled nodes inside the central zone of a window contribute to the loss, under the same \code{center\_k}~$=10$ rule used at inference.

\paragraph{Class balance and loss.} Among the labelled central nodes of the training split, 17,812,710 (97.04\%) were \emph{correct} and 544,170 (2.96\%) \emph{error}, and 21.5\% of windows contained at least one error node; the prevalence is far above the genome-wide switch-error rate because windows are opened only at high-entropy triggers and because the label is assigned relative to a window-local anchor rather than to a whole phase block. The imbalance is handled by subsampling, not by re-weighting the full distribution. Each minibatch keeps all of its error nodes and draws correct nodes uniformly at random to at most four times that number (\code{--ratio\_corr 4}), so the gradient is taken on an approximately 1:4 subset, and because the draw is independent at every step the network still sees the whole set of correct nodes over training. The cross-entropy on that subset is focal-weighted\cite{lin2017focal} by $(1-p_t)^{\gamma}$ with $\gamma = 1.5$ and uses label smoothing of 0.05. A differentiable surrogate for the selection metric below is implemented in the training script but was disabled for the released model ($\alpha = 0$), so the reported weights were trained on the focal loss alone.

\paragraph{Model selection.} Checkpoints were selected on the validation chromosomes by a net-gain criterion instead of validation loss or F1. At each epoch the predicted error probability was thresholded at each of 81 values spanning $[0.01, 0.99]$ and the score
\begin{equation}
S = R\,\Big(2 - \frac{1}{P}\Big)
\end{equation}
was maximized over those thresholds subject to a recall of at least $0.005$, where $P$ and $R$ are the precision and recall of the error class; $S$ is positive only for $P > 0.5$ and is the net fraction of true errors removed under the accounting in which a detection is worth one error and a false detection costs one, so epochs with $P \le 0.5$ were instead ordered by $P - 1$. The best checkpoint was epoch 44, with $S = 0.0235$ at $P = 0.626$, $R = 0.058$ and a threshold of 0.868; no later epoch improved on that checkpoint before early stopping. On the held-out chromosomes chr17, chr21 and chr22 that checkpoint attained an accuracy of 0.941 with error-class precision, recall and F1 of 0.258, 0.499 and 0.340 under the argmax decision, and 0.946 with 0.273, 0.468 and 0.345 at the decision threshold tuned on the validation chromosomes, over 41,378 labelled error nodes. The threshold applied by \code{gnn}, 0.30, is far more permissive than either threshold, because the module's only action is to withhold phase: a false detection costs one phased variant rather than one switch error, so the break-even precision built into $S$ governs checkpoint ranking but not deployment.

\paragraph{Export and numerical agreement.} The released weights are a single training checkpoint. A dense re-implementation replaces the sparse graph operators with $N \times N$ adjacency and edge tensors, transfers the trained tensors (splitting the fused attention projection into separate Q, K and V matrices), and exports to ONNX at opset 17 with $N$ as a dynamic axis. The logistic transform of the vote weight and the self-loop features are folded into the input convention at this point, so the exported graph and the compiled implementation both receive edge features with those already applied. The ONNX initializers are then read, the architecture dimensions recovered from the tensor shapes, and both written into a generated C++ header as a base64-encoded little-endian float32 blob in a fixed tensor order; the compiled forward pass reads the blob in that order and refuses to run if its length disagrees with the expected parameter count. Agreement was checked at each link of the chain: the dense rewrite against the PyTorch Geometric model, and the ONNX graph against the dense rewrite, on a real training window, with an acceptance bound of $10^{-3}$ on the absolute difference in predicted error probability; and the compiled C++ against ONNX Runtime on six synthetic graphs of 3--120 nodes at edge densities 0.1--0.4, including one graph with a single edge and no other incoming edges, where the largest absolute difference was $3\times 10^{-7}$ against an acceptance bound of $10^{-5}$. \todo{Report the achieved maximum differences for the first two links as well; the export script prints both, but the export log was not retained.}

\subsection*{Supplementary Method 5. Read haplotagging}

\code{longphase haplotag} assigns each alignment to a haplotype using the phased VCFs produced by \code{phase} (and, optionally, corrected by \code{gnn}). The main Methods gives the decision rule; this section gives the evidence model, which differs from the one used inside \code{phase} in ways that matter to anyone comparing haplotagged fractions between tools.

\paragraph{Alignment selection.} Primary alignments with mapping quality $\ge 1$ (\code{-q, --qualityThreshold}) are considered. Supplementary alignments are tagged only under \code{--tagSupplementary}; secondary alignments never are. Any pre-existing \code{HP}, \code{PS} and \code{PQ} tags are removed from every record before tagging, so re-tagging an already-tagged file is safe. Unmapped records are passed through unchanged.

\paragraph{Evidence collection.} Each alignment is traversed once along its CIGAR, and four classes of evidence are collected into two per-haplotype tallies $(n_1, n_2)$:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \emph{SNVs} are read from \code{M}, \code{=} and \code{X} operations; a read carrying the allele phased to haplotype $k$ adds 1 to $n_k$.
\item \emph{Small indels} are scored by allele length rather than by sequence comparison. Writing $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ for the lengths of the two phased alleles at the site, a read that carries an indel there supports the haplotype whose allele has $\ell \neq 1$, and a read that does not supports the haplotype whose allele has $\ell = 1$. When both or neither allele has length 1 the site casts no vote.
\item \emph{SVs} are re-derived from the alignment, not taken from the caller's read list: a window of $\pm$\code{--svWindow} CIGAR operations (default 20) around the current operation is scanned for an insertion or deletion whose length is within \code{--svThreshold} (default 0.10) of the SV length, and the first match adds 1 to the haplotype the SV is phased to.
\item \emph{Allele-specific 5mC} sites are matched by read name, not by position: the methylated-read list \code{INFO/MR} of the phased 5mC VCF adds 1 to the haplotype that record is phased to, for each read it names. The unmethylated list \code{NR} is not consulted, so a 5mC site contributes evidence only to the reads that are methylated at it.
\end{itemize}

Every informative observation contributes exactly 1, whatever its class. This is deliberately different from the read-based correction inside \code{phase}, where SNV and SV alleles contribute 1, indel and tandem-repeat-indel alleles 0.1 and 5mC alleles nothing (Methods). A read can therefore be tagged in \code{haplotag} on indel and methylation evidence that would have been discounted during phasing.

\paragraph{Decision and phasing quality.} Let $m = \max(n_1,n_2)$ and $\mu = \min(n_1,n_2)$. The read is assigned to the majority haplotype when $m/(m+\mu) \ge$ \code{-p, --percentageThreshold} (default 0.6) and is otherwise left untagged. A Phred-scaled phasing quality is computed for every alignment as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PQ} = \begin{cases}
0 & m = 0,\\
40 & \mu = 0,\\
\left\lfloor -10\log_{10}\dfrac{\mu}{m+\mu} \right\rfloor & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
so a read whose alleles are unanimous receives the ceiling value 40 and a read split 2:1 receives 4. \code{PQ} is written as an integer auxiliary tag \code{PQ:i} alongside \code{HP:i} and \code{PS:i}, and only on tagged reads; untagged reads carry none of the three. \code{PQ} is not reported in the main text and no analysis in this paper uses it.

\paragraph{Phase-set veto.} The phase sets of all informative variants on the read are counted. If more than one distinct \code{PS} is represented, the read is left untagged regardless of how one-sided its haplotype tally is, because a majority computed across two independent phase sets has no meaning. This veto bounds the taggable fraction from above and is the main reason a read spanning a block boundary is not tagged. The \code{PS} value written on a tagged read is that of the first phased variant at or after the alignment start.

\subsection*{Supplementary Method 6. Evaluation metrics}

Metrics were computed with \code{longphase compare} (Supplementary Fig.~8), a C++ re-implementation of the \code{whatshap compare} algorithm\cite{patterson2015whatshap}.

\paragraph{Block intersection.} For each chromosome, heterozygous records common to the truth and a query are intersected by position and by REF/ALT alleles. The intersection is partitioned into maximal runs that lie in one truth phase set and one query phase set simultaneously; runs of length one carry no phase information and are discarded. Only variants inside the surviving blocks enter any metric, so the denominators of the switch error rate and the Hamming distance are the same for every tool scored in the same run.

\paragraph{Switch errors and flips.} Each block yields two binary strings, the truth haplotype assignment $p_0$ and the query assignment $q_0$ of its variants. Both are converted to \emph{switch encoding}, the string of relative changes between consecutive positions, which removes the arbitrary orientation of the block. Switch errors are the Hamming distance between the two switch-encoded strings. The decomposition into isolated switches and flips follows WhatsHap's \code{compute\_switch\_flips}: a maximal run of $k$ consecutive disagreements in switch space is charged as $\lfloor k/2 \rfloor$ flips and $k \bmod 2$ switches, so that a single variant assigned to the wrong haplotype counts as one flip rather than two switches. The \emph{switch error rate} is the total number of switch errors divided by the number of assessed consecutive pairs.

\paragraph{Hamming distance.} For each block the query strings $(q_0,q_1)$ are compared with the truth strings $(t_0,t_1)$ in both orientations, and the smaller of $\mathrm{H}(t_0,q_0)+\mathrm{H}(t_1,q_1)$ and $\mathrm{H}(t_0,q_1)+\mathrm{H}(t_1,q_0)$ is halved to give the number of misassigned variants in that block. Block values are summed and expressed as a percentage of compared variants.

\paragraph{Block N50.} Block spans in bp are sorted in decreasing order and the N50 is the span at which the cumulative sum first reaches half the total, which is the span-weighted median. It is computed twice: once per chromosome, and once over the block spans of all chromosomes pooled. The values plotted in Figs.~2d, 3d and 5 are the pooled, genome-wide N50 of the query, which does not depend on the truth set.

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 1. Reading the phasing graph export}
With \code{--dot}, \code{longphase phase} writes one file per chromosome, \code{<prefix>.<chrom>.dot}, containing one line per voting pair, \code{P.a -> Q.b [label=w]}, where \code{P} and \code{Q} are 1-based positions, \code{a}, \code{b} $\in \{1, 2\}$ index the reference (1) and alternate (2) allele, and \code{w} is the weighted haplotype vote accumulated at \code{P} for that allele at the time the vote was cast. The companion VCF carries, for every phased variant, the fields \code{INFO/H1}, \code{INFO/H2} (weighted votes for haplotype 1 and 2) and \code{INFO/PE} (their normalized Shannon entropy). The \code{gnn} sub-command reads both files; users who wish to inspect why a variant was unphased can therefore reconstruct its local window without re-running the phaser.

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 2. Reproducing the demonstration}
The repository ships a 500-kb demonstration (chr1:121.5--122.0~Mb; \code{demo/demo.sh}) that runs \code{modcall}, SNV-only and four-class \code{phase}, \code{gnn} correction of both outputs and \code{compare} against a truth VCF, and prints switch errors, phased SNV counts and Hamming distance for the four configurations. Total runtime is under two minutes on four threads.

\centering\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{figures/supp/suppfig1_observations.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Read-level allele observations and pre-graph filters.}
\textbf{a}, For every alignment, each overlapped variant yields an allele observation with an associated quality: SNVs from the aligned base (Phred quality), indels from CIGAR operations starting at the site, SVs from CIGAR operations of matching length or from the caller's read list, and 5mC from the \code{modcall} read lists. The table summarizes how each class enters the edge weights.
\textbf{b}, Homopolymer SNV filter, applied under \code{--ont}: when two SNVs both lie in homopolymers of length $\ge 3$ and are $\le 2$~bp apart, the downstream SNV is removed.
\textbf{c}, Overlapping-alignment filter for supplementary alignments of one read.
\textbf{d}, Indels in tandem repeats are flagged and vote with weight 0.1.
\textbf{e}, Copy-number-aware filter, interval detection. Per-position counts of alignments starting (front-clipped, above the axis) and ending (back-clipped, below) are scanned by a three-state detector (idle, step, ramp; right): a \emph{step} boundary ($\ge 5$ front-clips at one position) marks a copy-number transition; a \emph{ramp} boundary (front-clips exceeding back-clips without reaching that threshold) marks the nested breakpoints left by breakage--fusion--bridge cycles. Both states carry a running excess $c$ of front- over back-clips and extend the candidate end by a 30~kb look-ahead while $c > 30$; a step closes at an adaptive pull-down threshold ($n/2$ for an opening front-clip count $n \ge 10$, else 5) or when $c$ decays to its floor, a ramp closes when back-clips reach $c/4$. Candidates longer than 200~kb are abandoned.
\textbf{f}, Copy-number-aware filter, site removal. Inside an interval, each read is scored by its mismatch load $R_m$, the number of heterozygous sites in the interval at which it carries the alternate allele, and each heterozygous site by $\mathrm{MR} = \bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{ALT}}/(\bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{REF}}+\bar{R}_m^{\,\mathrm{ALT}})$. At a genuine heterozygous site both allele populations are ordinary reads of the locus and $\mathrm{MR} \approx 0.5$; at a paralogue-specific difference the alternate allele is carried by reads that originate elsewhere and mismatch the reference at many other sites in the same interval, so $\mathrm{MR} \rightarrow 1$. Sites with $\mathrm{MR} \ge 0.7$ are removed from all reads; sites at which either mean is zero are never removed.
\textbf{g}, Order in which the filters are applied; the copy-number-aware filter (\textbf{e},\textbf{f}) acts last, immediately before graph construction.}
\end{figure}

\caption{\textbf{Pairwise allele support, weighted voting and phasing entropy.}
\textbf{a}, Between two variants $u$ and $v$ the reads accumulate four weights ($w_{rr}, w_{ra}, w_{ar}, w_{aa}$), each read adding 1 when both bases have quality $\ge$~Q12 and 0.1 otherwise. $P = w_{rr}+w_{aa}$ and $Q = w_{ra}+w_{ar}$ are the supports for the two possible pairings and $s = \min(P,Q)/\max(P,Q)$ their ambiguity.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/supp/suppfig6_gnn_architecture.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Graph neural network architecture.} Node features are standardized and projected to 128 dimensions. Each of four GPS layers combines a local GATv2 branch over the window graph (edge features enter through a per-layer edge encoder) with a global self-attention branch over all nodes, fused additively into the residual stream and followed by layer normalization and a feed-forward block. The final embedding is concatenated with the sum and the non-negative maximum, over the node's incoming edges excluding its self-loop, of the logistic-transformed edge weight $1/(1+e^{-w})$, and classified into phased-correctly versus misphased; the probabilities of the two allele nodes of a variant are averaged.}
