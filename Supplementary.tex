\subsection*{Supplementary Note 1. Read-level allele observations and copy-number interval detection}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Supplementary Note 1. Read-level allele observations and copy-number interval detection}

Each read contributes one allele observation at each eligible heterozygous variant that it covers (Supplementary Fig.~10). \toolname selects the eligible variants, extracts the allele of each variant class from the alignment and removes sites in intervals in which reads are probably misaligned.

\paragraph{Variant eligibility and repeat annotation.} Homozygous and filtered small-variant records are carried through to the output but do not enter the phasing graph. An optional indel-quality filter excludes indels below a user-specified QUAL threshold and marks their FILTER field; the default threshold of 0 disables this filter. An indel is flagged as a tandem-repeat indel when the dinucleotide immediately downstream of its position occurs in five consecutive copies. Flagged indels remain in the graph and cast votes at one-tenth weight. Homozygous SVs, SVs whose breakpoint coincides with an SNV and SVs with duplicated positions are excluded. The length of each retained SV is recorded, with its supporting read names when the caller reports them.

\paragraph{Allele extraction.} SNV alleles are read from the aligned base and inherit its Phred base quality. Under the nanopore setting, when two SNVs at most 2~bp apart both lie in homopolymers of length at least three, the downstream SNV is removed.

Insertion and deletion alleles are called from the CIGAR string: a read supports the ALT allele if a CIGAR insertion or deletion begins immediately after the variant position, and the REF allele otherwise. When a read deletion spans an SNV in a homopolymer of length $\ge 3$, the SNV allele is recovered from the base that follows the deletion.

SV alleles are assigned by scanning a window of 20 CIGAR operations (\code{--sv-window}) around the SV start for an insertion or deletion whose length is within 20\% (nanopore) or 10\% (HiFi) of the SV length (\code{--sv-threshold}). A read whose alignment ends at the SV is assigned from the junction to its supplementary alignment, and reads that the SV caller lists as supporting the SV are also assigned the ALT allele.

5mC alleles are taken from the \code{modcall} read lists (methylated~$=$~allele~0, unmethylated~$=$~allele~1), restricted to reads whose strand matches the recorded strand.

Indel observations, 5mC observations and alternate-allele SV observations have a fixed quality of 60, and reference-allele SV observations a quality of 30. All of these observations therefore have full weight at the default base-quality threshold, and reference-allele SV evidence is down-weighted only at a higher threshold.

\paragraph{Copy-number-aware filter.} Reads misaligned from other loci can produce false heterozygous sites. The copy-number-aware filter identifies candidate intervals that may contain such reads (Methods; Supplementary Fig.~10c). Candidate boundaries are inferred from clusters of soft- and hard-clips by scanning the per-position clipping counts of each chromosome in coordinate order.

\paragraph{Clipping counts.} For every reference position at which at least one alignment carries a soft- or hard-clipping operation longer than 5~bp, two counts are recorded: $u$, the number of alignments clipped at their front (left) end, and $d$, the number clipped at their back (right) end. An interval still open at the end of the chromosome is evaluated at a position 30~kb beyond the last observed clip.

\paragraph{Scanner states.} The scanner has three states. In the \emph{idle} state no candidate interval is open. The \emph{step} state follows a copy-number transition, which appears as a strong cluster of front-clips. The \emph{ramp} state follows progressively nested breakpoints, which appear as a weak excess of front-clips. In the two active states the scanner keeps a candidate start, a look-ahead window of length $A$ that begins at the candidate start, and a running excess $c$ of front-clips over back-clips, which is updated at each position by $u-d$. The constants are listed in the table below.

\paragraph{Opening an interval.} In the idle state, a position with $u \ge \Pi$ opens a step interval, and any other position with $u > d$ opens a ramp interval. The candidate start is the current position and $c = u - d$.

\paragraph{Step intervals.} A step interval uses three thresholds that are derived from the front-clip count $n$ of the position that opened it: a re-anchor threshold $r$, a pull-down threshold $p$ and a decay floor $f$. A position with $u > r$ is a stronger breakpoint and re-anchors the interval: the candidate start and the look-ahead window move to this position, and the thresholds are recomputed. The reported interval therefore begins at the strongest breakpoint. While $c > E$, the look-ahead window is moved forward to begin at the current position. The interval is emitted, from the candidate start to the current position, when $d \ge p$ (a back-clip cluster closes the interval) or when $c \le f$ inside the look-ahead window (the excess has decayed).

\paragraph{Ramp intervals.} A ramp interval is emitted when the back-clip count is large relative to the accumulated excess: $d \ge \lfloor c/4 \rfloor$ when $c > \Sigma$, and $d \ge \Pi$ otherwise. If the interval is not emitted and $u \ge \Pi$, it becomes a step interval that starts at the current position. Otherwise $c$ is updated and the look-ahead window is moved forward as for a step interval.

\paragraph{Discarding a candidate.} A candidate that is not emitted is discarded, and the scanner returns to the idle state, when the current position is beyond the look-ahead window, when $c \le 0$, or when the interval reaches the maximum length $\Lambda$. The emission conditions are tested before the discard conditions.

\paragraph{Site removal.} Within each emitted interval, the mismatch-load statistic $\mathrm{MR}(v)$ defined in Methods is computed. Comparable alternate-allele loads in the two read groups give $\mathrm{MR}$ near 0.5. Larger loads in the reads that carry the alternate allele increase $\mathrm{MR}$ and may indicate reads misaligned from paralogous sequence. Heterozygous variants with $\mathrm{MR}(v) \ge 0.7$ are therefore removed from all reads; SV alleles are not removed. A variant is scored only when both allele populations have a non-zero mean load; a site at which one population carries no other alternate allele is not removed on this basis.

\paragraph{Choice of constants.} The constants of the scanner ($\Pi$, $A$, $E$, $\Lambda$ and $\Sigma$, those of the adaptive thresholds and the 5-bp minimum clip length) were set by inspection of clipping profiles in copy-number-variable segments of HG002, the individual evaluated here, in which many false heterozygous SNVs had been called; they were not tuned by a parameter sweep. The look-ahead window $A$ approximates the read length (read N50 of 29~kb; Methods). The thresholds are absolute alignment counts applied unchanged at every coverage, and the sensitivity of the results to them was not assessed.

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 2. Allele-specific methylation calling (\code{modcall})}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Supplementary Note 2. Allele-specific methylation calling (\code{modcall})}

\code{modcall} identifies allele-specific 5mC sites in the four steps of Methods: read classification, candidate selection, the linkage test, and merging and output (Supplementary Fig.~9). The candidate criterion and the linkage criterion are equations~1 and~2 of Methods.

\paragraph{Read classification.} A read-level CpG observation is methylated when its 5mC probability is at least 0.8 (\code{--modThreshold}), unmethylated when it is at most 0.2 (\code{--unModThreshold}) and ambiguous otherwise. Observations are classified solely by their 5mC probability (modification code \code{m}); 5hmC probabilities (code \code{h}) are not used. Thus, under a 5mCG\_5hmCG model, a cytosine called as 5hmC is counted as unmethylated whenever its 5mC probability is $\le 0.2$. Reverse-strand calls are mapped to the forward-strand CpG coordinate, and observations from the two strands are classified jointly. Non-CpG modification calls are discarded.

\paragraph{Linkage test: modes and depth bound.} In \emph{SNV-anchored} mode, used whenever an SNV VCF is supplied, a candidate CpG is accepted as a strong anchor if it satisfies the linkage criterion against an SNV among the next 20 variants (\code{--connectAdjacent}). In \emph{methylation-only} mode, used where no informative SNV is in range, the same criterion is applied between two candidate CpGs.

In both modes a pair is considered only while the number of reads that span it exceeds $\max(6,\,(n_1+n_2)/4)$, where $n_1$ and $n_2$ are the numbers of reads recorded at the two sites. The neighbour scan for a site stops at the first pair at or below this bound.

\paragraph{Linkage test: expansion and final filter.} Candidates that never satisfy the linkage criterion are retained as weak candidates. They are admitted if they satisfy the criterion against an already accepted CpG on more reads than the pair-specific bound defined above, in two expansion rounds (\code{--iterCount}, default 2).

Finally, an accepted CpG is kept only if it satisfies the criterion on at least six reads with the preceding and the following accepted site; SNVs and the first and last sites are exempt.

\paragraph{Merging and output.} Runs of consecutive CpG coordinates are merged into a single locus represented by the first position, and the strand-specific observations of the run are consolidated into one read list. These methylation loci are phased jointly with sequence variants. The resulting VCF records the methylated and unmethylated read names (\code{MR}, \code{NR}), the read strand and the per-site depths, and is passed to \code{phase} through \code{--mod-file}.

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 3. Voting rules, read-based correction and the tuning of their constants}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Supplementary Note 3. Voting rules, read-based correction and the tuning of their constants}

The vote weights, the single-read guard and the read filter of Methods use the constants below. The user-adjustable parameters are listed with their options in Supplementary Table~12; the other constants are fixed in the program.

\paragraph{Vote weights.} A pair with ambiguity $s>\tau$ (default 0.7) casts no vote; SNV--5mC pairs use a threshold of 0.3 and require support of at least one read-equivalent. A vote has weight 1 by default. A near-unanimous pair ($s\le0.1$ with total support of at least 1, or one phasing below 1 while the other is at least 1) votes with weight 20. A tandem-repeat indel (Supplementary Note~1) votes with weight 0.1. A read adds weight 1 to an edge when both observations have a base quality of at least 12, and weight 0.1 otherwise.

\paragraph{Single-read guard.} When more than three votes have support of at most 1 read-equivalent, and at least one qualifying vote remains, only votes with support above 1 read-equivalent, $s<0.2$, weight of at least 1 and a non-indel voting variant are counted.

\paragraph{Read-based correction.} SNV and SV alleles have weight 1 and indel alleles weight 0.1. A read is used only when its majority fraction exceeds $\theta_{\mathrm{read}}$ (default 0.65) and its total evidence weight exceeds 1. The majority pairing assigns the phase of a variant only when $\rho>\theta_{\mathrm{var}}$ (default 0.75).

\paragraph{Tuning.} The voting and read-filtering constants were set empirically: the near-certain and exclusion ambiguity thresholds of 0.1 and 0.7, respectively, the near-certain vote weight of 20, the base-quality threshold of 12, and the single-read guard thresholds of 3 votes and 0.2 ambiguity. Each constant was fixed by a sweep on HG002 at 10--60$\times$ before the experiments reported here, and none was re-tuned afterwards. The sweeps were run during the development of \longphase in 2023--2024 on whole-genome phasing of earlier HG002 nanopore alignments, before the alignments evaluated here were generated and before the chromosome split used for GNN training was defined. The parameters were therefore tuned on data from the same individual as the evaluation, and chr17, chr21 and chr22 are held out from GNN training but not from this tuning.

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 4. Graph neural network training and inference}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Supplementary Note 4. Graph neural network training and inference}

The GNN predicts label-defined phase-disagreement onsets, comprising isolated disagreements and the first positions of consecutive disagreement runs. Its error-class output is used as an onset score for selective unphasing.

\paragraph{Node features.} Each allele node carries 31 features in four groups, phasing evidence, genomic context, graph structure and variant class, defined in Supplementary Table~13 (window construction in Supplementary Figs.~13 and~14). Among small variants, unequal REF and ALT lengths identify indels.

\paragraph{Edge features.} Edges are taken from the DOT graph and restricted to pairs with both endpoints inside the window. They are symmetrized by copying the features of whichever direction is present. Each node receives a self-loop whose features are the mean of its non-self incident edges. The vote weight $w$ of an edge is the weighted haplotype vote recorded for the source allele when the vote was cast. Each edge carries six features (Supplementary Table~14).

\paragraph{Architecture.} The network uses the GPS graph-transformer architecture\cite{rampasek2022gps} (Supplementary Table~15). Node features are standardized with a batch-normalization layer whose statistics are frozen at inference, linearly projected to a 128-dimensional embedding and passed through a GELU non-linearity. Four layers with the same architecture then each combine a local and a global branch. The local branch is a four-head GATv2 message-passing layer\cite{velickovic2018gat,brody2022gatv2} in which the attention coefficient of head $t$ for edge $j \rightarrow i$ is
\begin{equation}
\alpha^{(t)}_{ij} \;\propto\; \exp\!\Big(\mathbf{a}_t^{\top}\,\mathrm{LeakyReLU}_{0.2}\big(\big[\mathbf{W}_r \mathbf{x}_i + \mathbf{W}_l \mathbf{x}_j + \mathbf{W}_e \mathbf{e}_{ij}\big]_t\big)\Big),
\end{equation}
where $[\cdot]_t$ selects the 32 coordinates of head $t$, $\mathbf{W}_e$ is a per-layer edge encoder without bias and the normalization is a softmax over the incoming edges of $i$; the head outputs are concatenated and a bias is added. The global branch is four-head scaled dot-product self-attention over all nodes of the window\cite{vaswani2017attention}, with no masking. The two branch outputs are added to the residual stream, followed by layer normalization, a two-layer feed-forward block (128$\rightarrow$256$\rightarrow$128, GELU) with a second residual connection and layer normalization.

Two edge-context features are concatenated with the final node embedding: the sum and the non-negative maximum of the logistic-transformed vote weights over incoming edges, excluding the self-loop. For an edge with vote weight $w$, the transformed weight is $1/(1+e^{-w})$. The augmented node embedding is then passed through a two-layer classifier (130$\rightarrow$128$\rightarrow$2) with softmax output. The model comprises 687,358 stored values, including the frozen batch-normalization statistics.

\paragraph{Training data.} Training windows came from the same HG002 nanopore co-phasing runs used for evaluation at 10--20$\times$ (six coverages and ten down-sampling replicates, 60 runs).  Of 722,116 windows, 602,788 from chr1--14 and chr18--20 were used for training and 67,880 from chr15--16 for validation. The remaining 51,448 windows from chr17, chr21 and chr22 were used for evaluation. These chromosome sets were disjoint. Windows from chr17, chr21 and chr22 were excluded from model fitting, batch-normalization statistics and checkpoint selection. However, results from these chromosomes contributed to the choice of label definition, as described under Label definitions.  Windows contained all four variant classes, but only benchmarked SNV and indel nodes had labels, and the unphasing of SVs and 5mC sites relies on the model trained on those labelled small variants.  The training and evaluation truth was the v5.0q small-variant benchmark.  Training windows were limited to 10--20$\times$, whereas results also included 30--60$\times$ and PacBio HiFi data.

\paragraph{Labels.} Labels were derived from the v5.0q benchmark restricted to chr1--chr22 small variants, the same truth set used for evaluation, and describe the phase of a variant relative to its neighbours rather than in absolute terms. Within each window the variants were grouped by phase set; in each group the first variant present in the benchmark fixed the orientation of that group and was labelled \emph{correct}, and every later variant was labelled \emph{correct} if its allele-to-haplotype orientation agreed with that anchor and \emph{disagreeing} otherwise.

Each maximal run of consecutive disagreeing variants was attributed to its initiating event rather than counted as a separate error at each variant. A run of length one became \emph{unphase} if that variant's PE was at least 0.9 and \emph{flip} otherwise, whereas a run of length two or more had its first variant, the switch point, relabelled \emph{break}, and all remaining members reset to the \emph{correct} training label. This label marks non-targets for onset detection; it does not imply agreement with the benchmark at those positions. Variants absent from the benchmark, and all SV and 5mC records, for which the benchmark carries no phase truth, were left unlabelled: they remained in the graph as context but did not contribute to the loss.

The released model was trained and applied in binary mode. The binary classifier merged \emph{flip}, \emph{unphase} and \emph{break} into a single \emph{error} class; predicted targets were subsequently unphased.

\paragraph{Class balance and loss.} Among the labelled central nodes of the training split, 17,812,710 (97.04\%) were \emph{correct} and 544,170 (2.96\%) \emph{error}, and 21.5\% of windows contained at least one error node. These are node-label and window prevalences, respectively, whereas the genome-wide switch-error rate uses consecutive SNV pairs. The training windows were enriched for uncertain phasing by selection at high-entropy triggers, and labels were assigned relative to a window-local anchor. Each minibatch kept all of its error nodes and a uniform random sample of correct nodes of at most four times that number (\code{--ratio\_corr 4}), drawn again for every minibatch. The cross-entropy on that subset was focal-weighted\cite{lin2017focal} by $(1-p_t)^{\gamma}$ with $\gamma = 1.5$, where $p_t$ is the predicted probability of the target class. Label smoothing was 0.05.

\paragraph{Training augmentations.} Two label-preserving augmentations were applied to training windows only, each independently with probability 0.5. Haplotype swapping exchanged the two allele nodes of every variant and complemented the HP feature. After the exchange, each edge received the vote weight $w$ of its new source node. Coverage down-scaling multiplied $h_1$, $h_2$, their sum, the outgoing-weight standard deviation and the raw edge weights by a factor drawn uniformly from $[0.4, 0.7]$, then recomputed the derived logarithmic features; it was applied only to windows containing at least one error node. In release v2.1, swapped windows retained the pre-exchange source-node weights on their edges, so the edge weights did not match their source nodes. The model was retrained with the corrected augmentation for v2.1.1.

\paragraph{Model selection.} Checkpoints were selected on the validation chromosomes by a normalized net-gain criterion. At each epoch the predicted onset score was thresholded at each of 81 values spanning $[0.01, 0.99]$. For $P>0.5$, the score was
\begin{equation}
S = R\,\Big(2 - \frac{1}{P}\Big) = \frac{\mathrm{TP}-\mathrm{FP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN}},
\end{equation}
where $P=\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP})$ and $R=\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN})$ are precision and recall for the label-defined error class. For a fixed evaluation set, $\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN}$ is constant. Thus, maximizing $S$ is equivalent to maximizing $\mathrm{TP}-\mathrm{FP}$, assigning unit benefit to each true-positive detection and unit cost to each false-positive prediction. This score serves as a proxy for the utility of unphasing, rather than an exact fraction of switch errors removed from the output VCF. The score was maximized over thresholds subject to recall of at least $0.005$; for $P\le0.5$, the score was instead $P-1$. The selected checkpoint was epoch 30 ($S = 0.0221$ at $P = 0.621$, $R = 0.057$ and a threshold of 0.574); its classification metrics on the validation and held-out chromosomes are given in Supplementary Table~16. The score $S$ governed checkpoint selection; the deployment threshold was chosen separately using the end-to-end phasing trade-off described below.

\paragraph{Label definitions, seeds and deployment threshold.} For each of two label definitions, five models were trained with the settings above and seeds 42, 43, 44, 1337 and 3407. The first definition matched benchmark variants to calls by position, as described under Labels. The second matched by position, REF and ALT, leaving calls whose alleles differ from the benchmark unlabelled. Every model was applied to 60 HG002 nanopore SNV-only runs, comprising ten down-sampling replicates at each of 10, 12, 14, 16, 18 and 20$\times$, and scored relative to phasing without GNN-based unphasing by the fraction of switch errors removed and by the cost of unphasing, the number of phased SNVs withheld divided by the number of switch errors removed.

Because the fraction removed and the cost of correction trade off against each other through the onset-score threshold $\theta_{\mathrm{GNN}}$, each model's threshold was set on the validation chromosomes chr15 and chr16 to the same cost, 6.35 withheld SNVs per removed error (the cost of the release v2.1 model at $\theta_{\mathrm{GNN}}=0.30$), and the models were compared at that cost (Supplementary Table~17).

The REF/ALT-matched labels gave higher classification scores on the held-out chromosomes (error-class $F_1$ 0.367--0.382 against 0.345--0.355) but removed fewer switch errors there (mean of five seeds 21.6\%, range 20.8--22.9\%, against 23.0\%, range 22.4--23.9\%), and over the whole genome (33.5\% against 33.8\%). The position-matched labels were therefore kept. Among their five seeds, the released model is the one that removed the most switch errors on the validation chromosomes (seed 42, 50.8\%); the threshold selected on the validation chromosomes was 0.34 and was rounded to $\theta_{\mathrm{GNN}}=0.35$ for deployment.

With the released implementation and $\theta_{\mathrm{GNN}}=0.35$, GNN-based unphasing removed 34.3\% of switch errors at a cost of 7.98 withheld SNVs per removed error (60 runs pooled, whole genome), and 23.5\% at a cost of 9.26 on the held-out chromosomes chr17, chr21 and chr22 (Supplementary Note~7, Supplementary Fig.~15e and Supplementary Table~18).

The seed and the threshold were selected on the validation chromosomes only. The choice between the two label definitions also used the held-out and whole-genome results, so the held-out chromosomes are not independent of that choice. This criterion concerns the final phasing output and is distinct from the classifier-based criterion used to rank training checkpoints.

\paragraph{Scoring zone and inference.} Inference is implemented in C++ without external runtime dependencies and uses the erf-based GELU. Predictions for the two allele nodes of a variant are averaged. Only variants in the central zone of each window are scored: those whose genomic distance from the trigger is at most half the largest such distance in the window, $|p - p_c| \le \tfrac{1}{2}\max_u |p_u - p_c|$. Only labelled variants in this zone contributed to the training loss, and the same zone is scored at inference; the other variants supply graph context. Because the zone is defined on genomic distance and not on variant index, the number of variants that it holds depends on the local spacing. A variant scored in several windows receives the arithmetic mean of its predictions from those windows.

\paragraph{Phase-set update.} A variant with a mean onset score $\ge 0.35$ is unphased while preserving its alleles: both \code{0|1} and \code{1|0} become \code{0/1}, and the PS tag is removed. Bridge protection was disabled in the reported runs. Deletions of at least 10~kb with at least one vote and PE~$=0$ keep their phase. Connectivity is then evaluated over the surviving graph edges within each original phase set. The largest connected component retains the original PS identifier; each other component receives the 1-based position of its first variant as its identifier. A variant that unphasing or block splitting leaves as the only variant of its phase set is also unphased, whatever its score; phase sets that held one variant before unphasing are not changed. SNVs, indels, SVs and 5mC sites are counted together. Corrected records are written separately for small variants, SVs and methylation sites.

\paragraph{Export and numerical agreement.} The trained model was rewritten with dense $N \times N$ adjacency and edge tensors, exported to ONNX (opset 17, dynamic $N$) and compiled into the C++ module; the logistic transform of the vote weight and the self-loop features are applied to the inputs before inference. The dense rewrite was compared with the PyTorch Geometric model, and the ONNX graph with the dense rewrite, on a real training window. Both comparisons used an acceptance bound of $10^{-3}$ on the absolute difference in onset score. The compiled C++ module was compared with ONNX Runtime on six synthetic graphs of 3--120 nodes at edge densities 0.1--0.4. The largest absolute difference was $3\times 10^{-7}$ (acceptance bound $10^{-5}$).

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 5. Read haplotagging}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Supplementary Note 5. Read haplotagging}

\code{longphase haplotag} assigns eligible alignments to a haplotype using the phased VCFs produced by phasing with or without GNN-based unphasing.

\paragraph{Alignment selection.} Primary alignments with mapping quality $\ge 1$ (\code{-q, --qualityThreshold}) are considered. Supplementary alignments are considered only under \code{--tagSupplementary}; secondary alignments are excluded from haplotagging. Existing \code{HP}, \code{PS} and \code{PQ} tags are cleared from records that enter the haplotagging decision, before new tags are written. Mapped records skipped by this decision retain any existing tags. The separate writer for the unmapped tail clears these three tags before passing those records to the output.

\paragraph{Evidence collection.} Each alignment is traversed once along its CIGAR, and evidence from the four variant classes is collected into two per-haplotype tallies $(n_1, n_2)$:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \emph{SNVs} are read from \code{M}, \code{=} and \code{X} operations; a read carrying the allele phased to haplotype $k$ adds 1 to $n_k$.
\item \emph{Small indels} are scored by allele length rather than by sequence comparison. At an insertion, a read carrying the insertion supports the haplotype with the longer allele; a read without the insertion supports the haplotype with the one-base allele. At a deletion, a read carrying the deletion supports the haplotype with the one-base allele; a read without the deletion supports the other haplotype. When both or neither allele has length 1 the site casts no vote.
\item \emph{SVs} are re-derived from the alignment, not taken from the caller's read list: a window of $\pm$\code{--svWindow} CIGAR operations (default 20) around the current operation is scanned for an insertion or deletion whose length is within \code{--svThreshold} (default 0.10) of the SV length, and the first match adds 1 to the haplotype the SV is phased to.
\item \emph{Allele-specific 5mC} sites are matched by read name, not by position: the methylated-read list \code{INFO/MR} of the phased 5mC VCF adds 1 to the haplotype that record is phased to, for each read it names. The unmethylated list \code{NR} is not consulted; a 5mC site contributes evidence only to the reads that are methylated at it.
\end{itemize}

Each informative observation contributes unit weight during haplotagging, whatever its class. During read-based correction inside \code{phase}, by contrast, SNV and SV alleles contribute 1, indel and tandem-repeat-indel alleles 0.1, and 5mC alleles are excluded (Methods).

\paragraph{Decision and phasing quality.} Let $m = \max(n_1,n_2)$ and $\mu = \min(n_1,n_2)$. Reads with no informative evidence ($m+\mu=0$) remain untagged. Otherwise, a read is assigned to the majority haplotype when $m/(m+\mu) \ge$ \code{-p, --percentageThreshold} (default 0.6) and is otherwise left untagged. For alignments entering the haplotagging decision, a Phred-scaled phasing quality is computed as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PQ} = \begin{cases}
0 & m = 0,\\
40 & \mu = 0,\\
\left\lfloor -10\log_{10}\dfrac{\mu}{m+\mu} \right\rfloor & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\code{PQ} is written as an integer auxiliary tag \code{PQ:i} alongside \code{HP:i} and \code{PS:i}, and only on tagged reads.

\paragraph{Phase-set veto.} The phase sets of all informative variants on the read are counted. Reads with informative variants in more than one phase set are left untagged, regardless of their haplotype tally. The \code{PS} value written on a tagged read is that of the first phased variant at or after the alignment start, whether or not that variant is informative on the read. The output \code{PS} normally matches the phase set of the informative variants because the veto requires all informative variants to share one phase set. It can differ when the first phased variant at or after the alignment start is uninformative on the read and belongs to another block.

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 6. Benchmark data, tool configurations and evaluation}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Supplementary Note 6. Benchmark data, tool configurations and evaluation}

The evaluations used HG002 on two sequencing platforms, the same input for every tool and two phased truth sets. The released model was also applied to two further individuals, HG008-N and COLO829BL (Supplementary Note~8).

\paragraph{Sequencing data and down-sampling.} The benchmark used HG002 (NA24385) R10.4.1 reads from the Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) \texttt{giab\_lsk114\_2022.12} release: two PromethION flow cells (PAG65784 and PAG68757), SQK-LSK114, 400 bases per second and 4-kHz sampling, basecalled with Dorado \texttt{dna\_r10.4.1\_e8.2\_400bps\_sup@v4.0.0} and \texttt{5mCG\_5hmCG@v2}; their read N50 values were 29.2 and 29.4~kb.  Reads were aligned to the GRCh38 no-alt analysis set by ONT with minimap2 v2.24 (\texttt{-y -ax map-ont}), retaining reads with mean base quality $\ge 10$.  The 60$\times$ data set is a random 91.14\% subset of the 70.4$\times$ alignment (samtools v1.15, \texttt{view -s 0.9114}), and the 10--50$\times$ data sets were down-sampled from it with samtools v1.23.1\cite{danecek2021samtools} \texttt{view -s} \textit{seed.fraction}.  Ten replicates were used at 10--20$\times$ and one at 30--60$\times$; variants were called separately at every coverage and replicate (Supplementary Table~19).  SNVs and indels came from PEPPER-Margin-DeepVariant r0.8, which reports unphased genotypes, SVs from Sniffles2 v2.8.0, and allele-specific 5mC sites from \toolname.
The seeds were 1--9 and 867 at 10$\times$ (fraction 0.1666), 12--21 at 12$\times$ (0.200), 22--31 at 14$\times$ (0.233), 32--41 at 16$\times$ (0.266), 42--51 at 18$\times$ (0.300), 1--10 at 20$\times$ (0.3333), and 42 at 30, 40 and 50$\times$ (0.5, 0.6667 and 0.8333).  PEPPER-Margin-DeepVariant used the \texttt{--ont\_r10\_q20} model for nanopore data and \texttt{--hifi} for HiFi data.
HG002 PacBio HiFi (Revio) reads were taken from a 48$\times$ GIAB alignment (Supplementary Table~20), which was used as 50$\times$; 10--40$\times$ data sets were down-sampled from it with samtools v1.23.1 \texttt{view --subsample} (seed 1; fractions 0.2083, 0.4167, 0.6250 and 0.8333), and variants were called separately at every coverage.

\paragraph{Truth sets and region assignment.} Accuracy was scored with \texttt{longphase compare} on autosomes 1--22 against two HG002 truth sets: GIAB v4.2.1\cite{wagner2022giab}, in the version with phase transferred from a hifiasm assembly (the v4.2.1 benchmark VCF itself is unphased), restricted to biallelic heterozygous records; and GIAB v5.0q\cite{olson2026giabv5}, derived from the diploid T2T-HG002 assembly\cite{hansen2026q100}, in which every heterozygous variant is phased.  The truth VCFs were not restricted to their benchmark regions, and a call was scored only where the truth VCF holds the same REF and ALT alleles in a heterozygous genotype.  Both truth VCFs list heterozygous variants outside their benchmark regions (9.3\% of the autosomal heterozygous records of v5.0q and 3.4\% of v4.2.1), so the error counts include sites that the benchmark regions do not cover, among them satellite and segmental-duplication sequence that the T2T-HG002 assembly resolves\cite{hansen2026q100}.

To locate switch errors and to score indels, every consecutive variant pair in an intersected block was recorded with a re-implementation of \texttt{longphase compare}, which reproduced its counts exactly in all 1,024 scored runs.  A pair was assigned to a region when both variants lay in it; regions were the intersection and the differences of the two sets of benchmark regions, the remainder outside both sets, and the GIAB v3.6 genome stratifications\cite{dwarshuis2024stratifications}, which were applied inside the v5.0q benchmark regions.

A switch error was shared between tools when it lay at the same pair of SNV positions on the same chromosome under the same truth set (replicate~1); individual switch-error pairs were not tracked across the two truth sets.

Indel switch errors were counted over consecutive pairs that included at least one indel, and the indel Hamming distance was computed in the block orientation chosen on all variants.

\paragraph{Tool configurations.} \whatshap~v2.8\cite{patterson2015whatshap} and \hapcut~v1.3.4\cite{edge2017hapcut2} were run on the same alignments and variant calls as \toolname; cross-tool SNV comparisons used SNV-only phasing, with indel phasing disabled in \whatshap.  \toolname was run with the nanopore setting and the GNN settings given in Methods.  Co-phasing experiments separately evaluated the seven variant-class combinations listed in Supplementary Table~6, with and without GNN-based unphasing.  Indel co-phasing was also compared with \whatshap's default indel phasing and, in replicate~1, with \hapcut run with indel phasing (Supplementary Table~4).

The HiFi call sets were phased SNV-only with \toolname (\texttt{--pb}), with and without GNN-based unphasing, with \whatshap, with \hapcut and with HiPhase v1.7.0\cite{holt2024hiphase}; the GNN weights were trained on nanopore data.  HiPhase phases indels by default and has no SNV-only mode, so its SNV-only runs used the call sets restricted to SNVs, the variants that \whatshap phases with \texttt{--only-snvs}.  For the comparison with indels, \toolname, \whatshap and \hapcut were run with indel phasing and HiPhase on the full call sets (Supplementary Table~4).

\whatshap was run as \texttt{whatshap phase --ignore-read-groups --reference} with \texttt{--only-snvs} for SNV comparisons, and \hapcut as \texttt{extractHAIRS --ont 1} (\texttt{--pacbio 1} for HiFi) followed by \texttt{HAPCUT2} with default parameters, including its default likelihood-based pruning, on the PASS heterozygous SNVs.  \hapcut rejects genotypes other than two alleles from 0, 1 and 2, so the HiFi call sets given to it omitted such records (134--204 uncalled reference candidates per coverage and one multi-allelic indel at 50$\times$); none of them was a heterozygous SNV.  All tools ran on the same workstation.

\paragraph{Metric computation.} Metrics were computed with \code{longphase compare} (Supplementary Fig.~16), a C++ re-implementation of the \code{whatshap compare} algorithm\cite{patterson2015whatshap}.

\paragraph{Block intersection.} For each chromosome, heterozygous records common to the truth and a query are intersected by position and by REF/ALT alleles. The intersection is partitioned into maximal runs that lie in one truth phase set and one query phase set simultaneously; runs of length one carry no phase information and are discarded. Switch errors and Hamming distance are computed within the surviving blocks.

\paragraph{Switch errors and flips.} Each block yields two binary strings, the truth haplotype assignment $t_0$ and the query assignment $q_0$ of its variants. Both are converted to \emph{switch encoding}, the string of relative changes between consecutive positions, which removes the arbitrary orientation of the block. Switch errors are the Hamming distance between the two switch-encoded strings. The decomposition into isolated switches and flips follows WhatsHap's \code{compute\_switch\_flips}: a maximal run of $k$ consecutive disagreements in switch space is charged as $\lfloor k/2 \rfloor$ flips and $k \bmod 2$ switches. An isolated misassigned internal SNV contributes two discordant adjacent SNV pairs to the switch-error count, but one flip in the separate switch/flip decomposition. The reported SNV \emph{switch error rate} is the total number of SNV switch errors divided by the number of assessed consecutive SNV pairs.

\paragraph{Hamming distance.} For each block the query strings $(q_0,q_1)$ are compared with the truth strings $(t_0,t_1)$ in both orientations, and the smaller of $\mathrm{H}(t_0,q_0)+\mathrm{H}(t_1,q_1)$ and $\mathrm{H}(t_0,q_1)+\mathrm{H}(t_1,q_0)$ is halved to give the number of misassigned variants in that block. Block values are summed and expressed as a percentage of all compared variants. This denominator includes every variant class present in both files, including indels when present, unlike the SNV-pair denominator of the reported switch error rate.

\paragraph{Recall (phasing completeness).} Recall is calculated before partitioning the truth--query intersection into blocks. Its numerator is the number of truth heterozygous variants whose position, REF and first ALT match a query record that is heterozygous and phased. Its denominator is all truth heterozygous variants of the same class. A matched variant contributes to recall regardless of whether its phase is correct.

\paragraph{Block N50.} Query phase sets with fewer than two variants are excluded. The span of each remaining block is the distance in bp between its first and last variants. Block spans are sorted in decreasing order, and N50 is the span at which the cumulative sum first reaches half the total, which is the span-weighted median. It is computed once per chromosome and once over the block spans of all chromosomes pooled. The values plotted in Figs.~2e--h and~3e are the pooled, genome-wide N50 of the query. This metric uses query phase sets independently of the truth set and does not require truth--query intersection.

\paragraph{Co-phasing summaries.} For each co-phasing configuration, we defined \emph{blocks joined} as the net decrease in block count relative to SNV-only phasing in the same replicate and at the same GNN setting. Individual merge events were not tracked. We defined \emph{sites added} as the total number of phased indels, SVs and 5mC sites. We calculated switch errors per join as the increase in SNV switch-error count relative to SNV-only phasing divided by the net decrease in block count.

\paragraph{SV phase evaluation.} SV phase was evaluated on chr1--22 against haplotype-resolved variants called by dipcall from the T2T-HG002 Q100 v1.1 assembly aligned to GRCh38\cite{li2018dipcall,hansen2026q100}. Truth records with a missing allele on either haplotype were excluded. SVs were called with Sniffles2 v2.8.0 using the reference genome and retaining supporting read names on both platforms. Evaluation used replicate~1 at every coverage and the same heterozygous deletions and insertions of at least 50~bp for every tool.

\whatshap~v2.8 (\texttt{--indels}) and HiPhase v1.7.0 read SVs only as sequence-resolved records in the variant VCF. For these tools, deletions were given the reference sequence as REF and insertions the inserted sequence as ALT. These records were merged with the SNV (and indel) calls. \toolname received the unmodified SV calls. Inversions, duplications, breakends, symbolic insertions and records with an unknown reference base (296--580 per run) could not be converted. They were not given to \whatshap or HiPhase and were not scored for any tool.

A truth match required the same SV type and size similarity of at least 70\%. It also required start positions within 1~kb or, for deletions, reciprocal overlap of at least 50\%. The nearest eligible truth SV was selected. Each block was oriented by the majority phase agreement of its SNVs with truth SNVs matched by position. An SV matched to a heterozygous truth SV was correct when its oriented phase agreed with the assembly.

Phased SVs matched to a homozygous truth SV were counted separately as genotype errors. Homozygous matches, phased SVs without a truth match and SVs in blocks that could not be oriented were excluded from the phase-error rate. The SV phasing error rate was the number of incorrectly phased SVs divided by the total number of correctly and incorrectly phased SVs.

\paragraph{Analysis of unphased SNVs.} At 10, 30 and 60$\times$ coverage, we compared \toolname and \whatshap using replicate~1. The two output VCFs contained the same biallelic heterozygous SNV calls. We grouped these calls according to whether they were phased by both tools, \toolname only, \whatshap only or neither. Calls phased by \whatshap only were further split according to whether \toolname had left them unphased before GNN-based unphasing.

Each call was classified against the v5.0q chr1--22 VCF, without a benchmark BED, as heterozygous with the same position and alleles, heterozygous with other alleles, homozygous or absent. Calls on chrX and chrY were counted separately. The calls phased by a tool comprised those phased by both tools and those phased by that tool only. Phased calls at positions with a homozygous v5.0q record were counted as genotype errors (Fig.~5c).

Precision and F1 were calculated for every SNV-only run of \toolname and \whatshap, and on HiFi data also of HiPhase. Precision was the fraction of phased heterozygous SNV calls on chr1--22 that matched a benchmark heterozygous SNV, using the matched-call count from \texttt{compare}. F1 was the harmonic mean of precision and recall. Precision here is a benchmark-match fraction: an unmatched call outside the benchmark regions is unclassified, not a confirmed error. Neither precision nor recall requires the matched call to have the correct phase, so F1 does not measure phase correctness.

For benchmark-matched calls phased by one tool only, that tool's phase assignments were scored as in \texttt{compare}. A call was counted as wrongly phased when its phase was opposite to the majority orientation of its block relative to the truth. A switch-error interval extended from the first SNV of a switch-error pair to the base before the second. The fraction of calls phased by one tool only that fell inside that tool's switch-error intervals was compared with the corresponding fraction among all heterozygous SNV calls phased by that tool.

Read depth was taken from the variant caller's \texttt{FORMAT/DP} field, which is capped near 125$\times$. Calls were also annotated by the v5.0q benchmark regions, GIAB v3.6 segmental duplications and the one-to-one dipcall alignment (Supplementary Data~1).

An SNV was counted as unphased by the GNN when it was phased before this step and unphased after it. This is the subset described as left unphased at high phasing uncertainty.  The subset includes variants near a high-entropy variant and variants left alone in their phase set, so not every SNV in it has high phasing entropy.  Such sites were classified against the v5.0q benchmark VCF, matched by chromosome and position and without a benchmark BED, as absent, homozygous or heterozygous (Supplementary Fig.~6c); the heterozygous count was also reported with matching REF and ALT alleles, the rule used by \texttt{compare}.  The analysis used the 10--60$\times$ outputs of replicate~1 with the final GNN model.

At 60$\times$, SNVs unphased by the GNN were also classified against the T2T-HG002 assembly projected to GRCh38 as outside the 1:1 assembly BED, inside without an assembly variant at the same start coordinate, or inside with one; alleles were not compared.  All chromosomes, including chrX and chrY, were analysed, and autosome-only values were derived from the per-chromosome counts.  A random sample of 50,000 SNVs phased before GNN-based unphasing, which included sites that the GNN later unphased, provided the background; enrichment was summarized by chromosome.

They use \code{lp\_only} for calls left unphased by \toolname only (phased by \whatshap only), and \code{wh\_only} for calls left unphased by \whatshap only (phased by \toolname only). In \code{Unphased\_composition}, \code{lp\_only\_phase} and \code{lp\_only\_gnn} divide \code{lp\_only} into calls left unphased by the phasing graph before GNN-based unphasing and calls unphased by the GNN, respectively.

The code \code{both}, present in the composition, depth and sets sheets, denotes calls left unphased by both tools (``neither tool'' in Supplementary Table~9). The code \code{background}, present in the composition and sets sheets, denotes calls phased by both tools (``both tools'' in Supplementary Table~9).

\caption{\textbf{Allele-specific methylation calling with \code{modcall}.}
\textbf{a}, Read classification: each read-level CpG call on forward- and reverse-strand reads is methylated (5mC probability $\ge 0.8$), unmethylated ($\le 0.2$) or ambiguous.
\textbf{b}, Candidate selection: a CpG is a candidate when its methylated ($M$) and unmethylated ($U$) read counts are balanced and its ambiguous ($A$) fraction is small (Methods, equation~1); the three sites are a candidate, an unbalanced site and an ambiguous site.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/supp/suppfig2_voting.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Weighted phasing graph and multi-neighbour voting.}
\textbf{a}, Graph construction: each heterozygous variant has a reference ($r$) and an alternate ($a$) allele node, and each read connects its alleles to its alleles at the next $k$ variants ($k = 2$ in this example; default 35). Edge width is the summed read support, a dotted edge has only the reduced weight of an observation below the base-quality threshold, and a variant beyond the maximum distance (default 300~kb) is skipped.
\textbf{b}, The four allele-pair supports of a variant pair $(u, v)$ give the supports $P$ and $Q$ of the two phasings and the ambiguity $s$.
\textbf{c}, Vote weight as a function of $s$: a near-unanimous pair votes with weight 20, a pair with $s > \tau$ casts no vote, an SNV--5mC pair casts no vote above 0.3, and a tandem-repeat indel votes with weight 0.1 (Supplementary Note~3).
\textbf{d}, Variant $v$ receives weighted votes from up to $k$ upstream variants; arc width is the vote weight and arc colour is the haplotype that the vote gives to the $r$ allele of $v$, and the totals $h_1$ and $h_2$ decide its haplotype.
\textbf{e}, Single-read guard: one long read that covers many upstream variants gives many votes, each supported by that read only and each with weight 20, and can outvote well-supported pairs (left); when more than three votes have the support of one read or less and a stronger vote remains, only the stronger votes are counted (right).
\textbf{f}, Phase blocks: a tie within a block leaves the variant unphased, and a tie beyond the last accepted edge opens a new block; the phase set (PS) is the position of the first variant of a block.}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/supp/suppfig3_read_correction.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Read-based correction and phasing entropy.}
\textbf{a}, Each read is assigned to a haplotype by the weighted majority of its phased alleles; a read with a majority fraction $\le \theta_{\mathrm{read}}$ (default 0.65) or a total evidence weight $\le 1$ is not assigned. Indels have a lower weight, and 5mC alleles are not used.

\textbf{c}, Phasing entropy (PE) of the vote totals $h_1$ and $h_2$ as a function of the vote split, with three examples; variants with $\mathrm{PE} \ge \theta_{\mathrm{PE}}$ (default 0.8, shaded) open a window for the GNN (Supplementary Fig.~13).}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/supp/suppfig4_gnn_overview.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Overview of the graph neural network unphasing module.}
\textbf{a}, Every phased variant with a phasing entropy of at least 0.8 (amber) triggers a window of $\pm 20$ variants.
\textbf{b}, The window is represented as a graph of allele nodes with 31 node features and six edge features (Supplementary Tables~13 and~14); squares represent features, not their values.
\textbf{c}, Each of four GPS layers combines a local GATv2 branch with a global self-attention branch; a classifier head assigns each allele node a score for a label-defined phase-disagreement onset.
\textbf{d}, Variants with an onset score of at least 0.35 are unphased without changing their genotypes, and the phase set is split where the remaining graph is disconnected; the largest component keeps the original PS and every other component receives the position of its first variant.
Exceptions and singleton handling are defined in Supplementary Note~4; window construction is shown in Supplementary Fig.~14.}
