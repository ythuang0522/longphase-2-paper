\subsection*{Supplementary Method 1. Allele-specific methylation calling (\code{modcall})}

\paragraph{Modification interpretation.} Only the 5mC probability (modification code \code{m}) was used. 5hmC calls (code \code{h}) were ignored; under a 5mCG\_5hmCG model, a cytosine called as 5hmC was counted as unmethylated whenever its 5mC probability was $\le 0.2$. Reverse-strand calls were mapped to the forward-strand CpG coordinate, and the two strands were genotyped jointly. Non-CpG modification calls were discarded.

\paragraph{Linking modes and the depth bound on the neighbour scan.} In \emph{SNV-anchored} mode, used whenever an SNV VCF was supplied, a candidate CpG was accepted as a strong anchor if it satisfied the co-segregation criterion of Methods against an SNV among the next 20 variants (\code{--connectAdjacent}). In \emph{methylation-only} mode, used where no informative SNV was in range, the same criterion was applied between two candidate CpGs. In both modes a pair was considered only while the number of reads spanning it exceeded $\max(6,\,(n_1+n_2)/4)$, where $n_1$ and $n_2$ are the numbers of reads recorded at the two sites. The neighbour scan for a site stopped at the first pair at or below this bound. Candidates that never satisfied the co-segregation criterion were retained as weak candidates. They were admitted if they satisfied the criterion on at least six reads against an already accepted CpG, in two expansion rounds (\code{--iterCount}, default 2).

\paragraph{Merging runs of CpGs.} Runs of consecutive CpG coordinates were merged into a single locus represented by the first position, and the strand-specific observations of the run were consolidated into one read list, so that methylation loci were phased jointly with sequence variants. The resulting VCF records the methylated and unmethylated read names (\code{MR}, \code{NR}), the read strand and the per-site depths, and is passed to \code{phase} through \code{--mod-file}.

\subsection*{Supplementary Method 2. Read-level allele observations and copy-number interval detection}

\paragraph{Variant eligibility and repeat annotation.} Homozygous and filtered small-variant records were carried through to the output but did not enter the phasing graph. An optional indel-quality filter excluded indels below a user-specified QUAL threshold and marked their FILTER field; the default threshold of 0 disabled this filter. An indel was flagged as a tandem-repeat indel when the dinucleotide immediately downstream of its position occurred in five consecutive copies. Flagged indels remained in the graph and cast votes at one-tenth weight. Homozygous SVs, SVs whose breakpoint coincided with an SNV and SVs with duplicated positions were excluded. The supporting read names and length of each retained SV were recorded.

\paragraph{Per-class allele extraction.} SNV alleles were read from the aligned base and inherited its Phred base quality. Insertion and deletion alleles were called from the CIGAR string: a read supports the ALT allele if a CIGAR insertion or deletion begins immediately after the variant position, and the REF allele otherwise; when a read deletion spans an SNV in a homopolymer of length $\ge 3$, the SNV allele was recovered from the base following the deletion. SV alleles were assigned by scanning a window of 20 CIGAR operations (\code{--svWindow}) around the SV start for an insertion or deletion whose length is within 10\% of the SV length (\code{--svThreshold}); reads listed by the SV caller as supporting the SV were also assigned the ALT allele. 5mC alleles were taken from the \code{modcall} read lists (methylated~$=$~allele~0, unmethylated~$=$~allele~1) restricted to reads whose strand matched the recorded strand. Soft- and hard-clipping operations longer than 5~bp were counted per reference position, separately for clips at the front (left) and back (right) end of an alignment, as evidence of alignment breakpoints for copy-number detection (below).

The copy-number-aware filter (Methods; Supplementary Fig.~1e,f) requires the boundaries of segments that recruit reads from more than one locus. These boundaries were detected from clusters of soft- and hard-clips by a scan of the per-position clipping counts of each chromosome in coordinate order.

\paragraph{Input.} For every reference position at which at least one alignment carries a clipping operation longer than 5~bp, two counts are recorded: $u$, the number of alignments clipped at their front (left) end, and $d$, the number clipped at their back (right) end. An interval still open at the end of the chromosome was evaluated at a position 30~kb beyond the last observed clip.

\paragraph{State.} The scanner is in one of three states: \emph{idle}, \emph{step} (a copy-number transition, entered on a strong front-clip cluster) and \emph{ramp} (the progressively nested breakpoints left by breakage--fusion--bridge cycles, entered on a weak front-clip excess). In the two active states it carries a running excess $c$ of front- over back-clips, a candidate start, a candidate end and three adaptive thresholds derived from the front-clip count $n$ that opened or re-anchored the interval:
\[
r = n, \qquad
p = \begin{cases} \lfloor n/2 \rfloor, & n \ge 10\\ 5, & n < 10\end{cases},
\]
\[
f = \begin{cases} 5, & n \ge 20\\ \lfloor n/4 \rfloor, & 10 \le n < 20\\ 2, & n < 10, \end{cases}
\]
where $r$ is the re-anchor threshold, $p$ the pull-down threshold and $f$ the decay floor.

\paragraph{Transitions.} With constants $\Pi = 5$ (push threshold), $A = 30{,}000$~bp (look-ahead), $E = 30$ (extend threshold), $\Lambda = 200{,}000$~bp (maximum interval length) and $\Sigma = 20$ (ramp commit threshold), the scanner at position $x$ with counts $(u,d)$ behaves as follows:

{\small
\begin{verbatim}
idle:
  if u >= PI and c == 0:
      enter STEP;  c = u - d;  start = x;  end = x + A
      set r, p, f from u
  elif u > d and c == 0:
      enter RAMP;  c = u - d;  start = x;  end = x + A

step:
  if u > r:                      # stronger breakpoint: re-anchor
      start = x;  end = x + A;  set r, p, f from u
  c += u - d
  if c > E:  end = x + A
  if d >= p:                     # pull-down reached
      emit [start, x];  reset to idle
  elif c <= f and x <= end:      # decayed to the floor in range
      emit [start, x];  reset to idle
  if x > end or c <= 0 or x - start >= LAMBDA:
      reset to idle              # candidate discarded

ramp:
  if (c > SIGMA and d >= c/4) or (c <= SIGMA and d >= PI):
      emit [start, x];  reset to idle
  elif u >= PI:                  # promote to STEP
      c = u - d;  start = x;  end = x + A;  set r, p, f from u
  else:
      c += u - d
      if c > E:  end = x + A
      if x > end or c <= 0 or x - start >= LAMBDA:
          reset to idle          # candidate discarded
\end{verbatim}
}

A stronger front-clip cluster re-anchored a \emph{step} interval: the candidate start moved to the new position and the three thresholds were recomputed, so that the reported interval began at the strongest breakpoint. The decay-floor exit applied only within the look-ahead window ($x \le \text{end}$); candidates outside this window were discarded.

\paragraph{Site removal.} Within each emitted interval the mismatch-rate statistic $\mathrm{MR}(v)$ defined in Methods is computed and heterozygous variants with $\mathrm{MR}(v) \ge 0.7$ are removed from all reads. A variant is scored only when both allele populations have a non-zero mean load; a site at which one population carries no other alternate allele is not removed on this basis.

\paragraph{Rationale for the mismatch-load statistic.} Comparable alternate-allele loads in the two read groups give $\mathrm{MR}$ near 0.5. Larger loads in the reads that carry the alternate allele increase $\mathrm{MR}$ and may indicate reads misaligned from paralogous sequence.

\subsection*{Supplementary Method 3. Tuning of the voting and read-filtering parameters}

The constants in this section were set empirically. Writing the ambiguity of a pair as $s = \min(P,Q)/\max(P,Q)$, which ranges from 0 for unanimous support to 1 for an even split, the voting rule has three levels: a pair votes at weight $w = 20$ when it is near-certain, that is, when $s \le \alpha = 0.1$ with total support $\ge 1$ or, irrespective of $s$, when one pairing has accumulated less than 1 unit of support while the other has at least 1. The pair votes at unit weight otherwise, and does not vote at all when $s > \beta = 0.7$. Each of $\alpha$, $\beta$, $w$, the base-quality threshold $\theta = 12$ and the single-read guard parameters ($N = 3$ votes, ambiguity cut $\mu = 0.2$) was fixed by a sweep on HG002 at 10--60$\times$ before the experiments reported here, and none was re-tuned afterwards. The sweeps were run during the development of \longphase in 2023--2024 on whole-genome phasing of earlier HG002 nanopore alignments, before the alignments evaluated here were generated and before the chromosome split used for GNN training was defined. The parameters were therefore tuned on data from the same individual as the evaluation, and chr17, chr21 and chr22 are held out from GNN training but not from this tuning. \todo{Supplementary Fig.~9 must either be produced or removed before submission.}

\subsection*{Supplementary Method 4. Graph neural network: features, architecture, labels, training and export}

The GNN predicts label-defined phase-disagreement onsets, comprising isolated disagreements and the first positions of consecutive disagreement runs. Its error-class output is used as an onset score for selective unphasing.

\paragraph{Node features.} Each allele node carried 31 features in four groups, phasing evidence, genomic context, graph structure and variant class, defined in Supplementary Table~1 (window construction in Supplementary Figs.~4 and~5). SNVs and indels were distinguished by $|\,|\mathrm{ALT}|-|\mathrm{REF}|\,|>0$.

\paragraph{Edge features.} Edges were taken from the DOT graph, restricted to pairs both of whose endpoints lie inside the window, symmetrized by copying the features of whichever direction was present, and augmented with a self-loop per node whose features are the mean of its non-self incident edges. The vote weight $w$ of an edge is the weighted haplotype vote recorded for the source allele when the vote was cast. Each edge carried six features (Supplementary Table~2).

\paragraph{Architecture.} The network used the GPS graph-transformer architecture\cite{rampasek2022gps} (Supplementary Table~3 and Supplementary Fig.~6). Node features were standardized with a batch-normalization layer whose statistics are frozen at inference, linearly projected to a 128-dimensional embedding and passed through a GELU non-linearity. Four identical layers then each combine a local and a global branch. The local branch is a four-head GATv2 message-passing layer\cite{velickovic2018gat,brody2022gatv2} in which the attention logit of head $t$ for edge $j \rightarrow i$ is
\begin{equation}
\alpha^{(t)}_{ij} \;\propto\; \exp\!\Big(\mathbf{a}_t^{\top}\,\mathrm{LeakyReLU}_{0.2}\big(\big[\mathbf{W}_r \mathbf{x}_i + \mathbf{W}_l \mathbf{x}_j + \mathbf{W}_e \mathbf{e}_{ij}\big]_t\big)\Big),
\end{equation}
where $[\cdot]_t$ selects the 32 coordinates of head $t$, $\mathbf{W}_e$ is a per-layer edge encoder without bias and the normalization is a softmax over the incoming edges of $i$; the head outputs are concatenated and a bias is added. The global branch is four-head scaled dot-product self-attention over all nodes of the window\cite{vaswani2017attention}, with no masking. The two branch outputs are added to the residual stream, followed by layer normalization, a two-layer feed-forward block (128$\rightarrow$256$\rightarrow$128, GELU) with a second residual connection and layer normalization. The final node embedding is concatenated with two edge-context scalars, the sum and the non-negative maximum, over the node's incoming edges excluding its self-loop, of the first edge feature, i.e.\ of the logistic-transformed vote weight $1/(1+e^{-w})$ rather than of $w$ itself. The pooled vector is then passed through a two-layer classifier (130$\rightarrow$128$\rightarrow$2) with softmax output.

The model comprises 687,358 stored values, including the frozen batch-normalization statistics. Inference was implemented in C++ without external runtime dependencies and used the erf-based GELU. Predictions for the two allele nodes of a variant were averaged. Only variants in the central zone of each window were scored: those whose genomic distance from the trigger is at most half the largest such distance in the window, $|p - p_c| \le \tfrac{1}{2}\max_u |p_u - p_c|$. Only labelled variants in this zone contributed to the training loss, and the same zone was scored at inference; the other variants supplied graph context. A variant scored in several windows received the running mean of its predictions from those windows.

\paragraph{Phase-set update.} A variant with a mean onset score $\ge 0.30$ was unphased while preserving its alleles: both \code{0|1} and \code{1|0} became \code{0/1}, and the PS tag was removed. Bridge protection was disabled in the reported runs. Connectivity was then evaluated over the surviving graph edges within each original phase set. The largest connected component retained the original PS identifier; each other component received the 1-based position of its first variant as its identifier. Corrected records were written separately for small variants, SVs and methylation sites.

\paragraph{Training augmentations.} Two label-preserving augmentations were applied to training windows only, each independently with probability 0.5: a haplotype swap, which exchanges the two allele nodes of every variant and complements the HP feature, an operation the phasing problem is invariant under; and a coverage down-scaling, which multiplies $h_1$, $h_2$, their sum, the outgoing-weight standard deviation and the raw edge weights by a factor drawn uniformly from $[0.4, 0.7]$ and recomputes the derived logarithmic features, applied only to windows containing at least one error node.

\paragraph{Labels.} Labels were derived from the v5.0q benchmark restricted to chr1--chr22 small variants, the same truth set used for evaluation, and describe the phase of a variant relative to its neighbours rather than in absolute terms. Within each window the variants were grouped by phase set; in each group the first variant present in the benchmark fixed the orientation of that group and was labelled \emph{correct}, and every later variant was labelled \emph{correct} if its allele-to-haplotype orientation agreed with that anchor and \emph{disagreeing} otherwise. Maximal runs of consecutive disagreeing variants were then resolved so that a run is charged to the event that created the run, not to each member. A run of length one became \emph{unphase} if that variant's PE was at least 0.9 and \emph{flip} otherwise, whereas a run of length two or more had its first variant, the switch point, relabelled \emph{break}, and all remaining members reset to \emph{correct}. Variants absent from the benchmark, and all SV and 5mC records, for which the benchmark carries no phase truth, were left unlabelled: they remained in the graph as context but did not contribute to the loss. The released model was trained and applied in binary mode, in which \emph{flip}, \emph{unphase} and \emph{break} are merged into a single \emph{error} class.

\paragraph{Class balance and loss.} Among the labelled central nodes of the training split, 17,812,710 (97.04\%) were \emph{correct} and 544,170 (2.96\%) \emph{error}, and 21.5\% of windows contained at least one error node; the prevalence is far above the genome-wide switch-error rate because windows are opened only at high-entropy triggers and because the label is assigned relative to a window-local anchor rather than to a whole phase block. Each minibatch kept all of its error nodes and a uniform random sample of correct nodes of at most four times that number (\code{--ratio\_corr 4}), drawn again for every minibatch. The cross-entropy on that subset was focal-weighted\cite{lin2017focal} by $(1-p_t)^{\gamma}$ with $\gamma = 1.5$ and used label smoothing of 0.05.

\paragraph{Model selection.} Checkpoints were selected on the validation chromosomes by a normalized net-gain criterion. At each epoch the predicted onset score was thresholded at each of 81 values spanning $[0.01, 0.99]$. For $P>0.5$, the score was
\begin{equation}
S = R\,\Big(2 - \frac{1}{P}\Big) = \frac{\mathrm{TP}-\mathrm{FP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN}},
\end{equation}
where $P=\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP})$ and $R=\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN})$ are precision and recall for the label-defined error class. For a fixed evaluation set, $\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN}$ is constant. Thus, maximizing $S$ is equivalent to maximizing $\mathrm{TP}-\mathrm{FP}$, assigning unit benefit to each true-positive detection and unit cost to each false-positive prediction. This score serves as a proxy for correction utility, rather than an exact fraction of switch errors removed from the corrected VCF. The score was maximized over thresholds subject to recall of at least $0.005$; for $P\le0.5$, the score was instead $P-1$. The selected checkpoint was epoch 44 ($S = 0.0235$ at $P = 0.626$, $R = 0.058$ and a threshold of 0.868); its classification metrics on the validation and held-out chromosomes are given in Supplementary Table~4. The score $S$ governed checkpoint selection; the deployment threshold was chosen separately using the end-to-end phasing trade-off described below.

\paragraph{Deployment-threshold selection.} Onset score thresholds $B$ from 0.05 to 0.95 in steps of 0.05 were evaluated on 60 HG002 nanopore runs, comprising ten down-sampling replicates at each of 10, 12, 14, 16, 18 and 20$\times$. Each threshold was scored, relative to phasing without GNN correction, by the fraction of switch errors removed and by the cost of correction, the number of phased SNVs withheld divided by the number of switch errors removed. Because the two trade off against each other, the criterion was the largest fraction of switch errors removed at a cost of at most ten withheld SNVs per removed error. For the released model, $B=0.30$ removed 33.1\% of switch errors at a cost of 8.3 withheld SNVs per removed error (60 runs pooled, whole genome). On the held-out chromosomes chr17, chr21 and chr22, the same threshold removed 21.2\% of switch errors at a cost of 10.5, slightly above the limit; the threshold was not re-selected on these chromosomes (Supplementary Table~15). This criterion concerns the final phasing output and is distinct from the classifier-based criterion used to rank training checkpoints.

\paragraph{Export and numerical agreement.} The trained model was rewritten with dense $N \times N$ adjacency and edge tensors, exported to ONNX (opset 17, dynamic $N$) and compiled into the C++ module; the logistic transform of the vote weight and the self-loop features are applied to the inputs before inference. Agreement was checked at each step: the dense rewrite against the PyTorch Geometric model and the ONNX graph against the dense rewrite, on a real training window, with an acceptance bound of $10^{-3}$ on the absolute difference in onset score; and the compiled C++ module against ONNX Runtime on six synthetic graphs of 3--120 nodes at edge densities 0.1--0.4, where the largest absolute difference was $3\times 10^{-7}$ (acceptance bound $10^{-5}$).

\subsection*{Supplementary Method 5. Read haplotagging}

\code{longphase haplotag} assigns each alignment to a haplotype using the phased VCFs produced by phasing with or without GNN correction. 

\paragraph{Alignment selection.} Primary alignments with mapping quality $\ge 1$ (\code{-q, --qualityThreshold}) are considered. Supplementary alignments are tagged only under \code{--tagSupplementary}; secondary alignments never are. Any pre-existing \code{HP}, \code{PS} and \code{PQ} tags are removed from every record before tagging. Unmapped records are passed through unchanged.

\paragraph{Evidence collection.} Each alignment is traversed once along its CIGAR, and four classes of evidence are collected into two per-haplotype tallies $(n_1, n_2)$:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \emph{SNVs} are read from \code{M}, \code{=} and \code{X} operations; a read carrying the allele phased to haplotype $k$ adds 1 to $n_k$.
\item \emph{Small indels} are scored by allele length rather than by sequence comparison. Writing $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ for the lengths of the two phased alleles at the site, a read that carries an indel there supports the haplotype whose allele has $\ell \neq 1$, and a read that does not supports the haplotype whose allele has $\ell = 1$. When both or neither allele has length 1 the site casts no vote.
\item \emph{SVs} are re-derived from the alignment, not taken from the caller's read list: a window of $\pm$\code{--svWindow} CIGAR operations (default 20) around the current operation is scanned for an insertion or deletion whose length is within \code{--svThreshold} (default 0.10) of the SV length, and the first match adds 1 to the haplotype the SV is phased to.
\item \emph{Allele-specific 5mC} sites are matched by read name, not by position: the methylated-read list \code{INFO/MR} of the phased 5mC VCF adds 1 to the haplotype that record is phased to, for each read it names. The unmethylated list \code{NR} is not consulted; a 5mC site contributes evidence only to the reads that are methylated at it.
\end{itemize}

Each informative observation contributed unit weight during haplotagging, whatever its class. During read-based correction inside \code{phase}, by contrast, SNV and SV alleles contribute 1, indel and tandem-repeat-indel alleles 0.1, and 5mC alleles are excluded (Methods).

\paragraph{Decision and phasing quality.} Let $m = \max(n_1,n_2)$ and $\mu = \min(n_1,n_2)$. The read is assigned to the majority haplotype when $m/(m+\mu) \ge$ \code{-p, --percentageThreshold} (default 0.6) and is otherwise left untagged. A Phred-scaled phasing quality is computed for every alignment as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PQ} = \begin{cases}
0 & m = 0,\\
40 & \mu = 0,\\
\left\lfloor -10\log_{10}\dfrac{\mu}{m+\mu} \right\rfloor & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\code{PQ} is written as an integer auxiliary tag \code{PQ:i} alongside \code{HP:i} and \code{PS:i}, and only on tagged reads.

\paragraph{Phase-set veto.} The phase sets of all informative variants on the read are counted. Reads with informative variants in more than one phase set were left untagged, regardless of their haplotype tally. The \code{PS} value written on a tagged read is that of the first phased variant at or after the alignment start, whether or not that variant is informative on the read; because the veto has already required every informative variant to share one phase set, the two coincide except when that first variant is uninformative on the read and belongs to a different block, a boundary case.

\subsection*{Supplementary Method 6. Evaluation metrics}

Metrics were computed with \code{longphase compare} (Supplementary Fig.~8), a C++ re-implementation of the \code{whatshap compare} algorithm\cite{patterson2015whatshap}.

\paragraph{Block intersection.} For each chromosome, heterozygous records common to the truth and a query are intersected by position and by REF/ALT alleles. The intersection is partitioned into maximal runs that lie in one truth phase set and one query phase set simultaneously; runs of length one carry no phase information and are discarded. The switch errors and the Hamming distance of a query are computed inside its surviving blocks but with different denominators: the switch error rate divides SNV switch errors by the number of consecutive SNV pairs assessed, whereas the Hamming distance divides misassigned variants, of every class present in both files, by the number of compared variants; the phased fraction is counted before this partition, as the number of truth heterozygous variants whose position, REF and first ALT match a query record that is heterozygous and phased, over all truth heterozygous variants of the class.

\paragraph{Switch errors and flips.} Each block yields two binary strings, the truth haplotype assignment $p_0$ and the query assignment $q_0$ of its variants. Both are converted to \emph{switch encoding}, the string of relative changes between consecutive positions, which removes the arbitrary orientation of the block. Switch errors are the Hamming distance between the two switch-encoded strings. The decomposition into isolated switches and flips follows WhatsHap's \code{compute\_switch\_flips}: a maximal run of $k$ consecutive disagreements in switch space is charged as $\lfloor k/2 \rfloor$ flips and $k \bmod 2$ switches. A single variant assigned to the wrong haplotype thus counts as one flip, not two switches. The \emph{switch error rate} is the total number of switch errors divided by the number of assessed consecutive pairs.

\paragraph{Hamming distance.} For each block the query strings $(q_0,q_1)$ are compared with the truth strings $(t_0,t_1)$ in both orientations, and the smaller of $\mathrm{H}(t_0,q_0)+\mathrm{H}(t_1,q_1)$ and $\mathrm{H}(t_0,q_1)+\mathrm{H}(t_1,q_0)$ is halved to give the number of misassigned variants in that block. Block values are summed and expressed as a percentage of compared variants.

\paragraph{Block N50.} Block spans in bp are sorted in decreasing order and the N50 is the span at which the cumulative sum first reaches half the total, which is the span-weighted median. It is computed twice: once per chromosome, and once over the block spans of all chromosomes pooled. The values plotted in Figs.~2c,f,i,l are the pooled, genome-wide N50 of the query, which does not depend on the truth set.
