\begin{abstract}
Long reads link genetic variants and native methylation, but turning them into reliable haplotypes remains difficult in repetitive regions. Here we present \toolname, which co-phases SNVs, indels, structural variants and allele-specific methylation in one graph, uses quality- and context-aware weighting, and scores every variant's phasing uncertainty. A graph neural network then unphases, rather than flips, predicted misphased variants. On HG002 nanopore data (10--60$\times$), scored against a benchmark built from a telomere-to-telomere assembly, \toolname makes 2.8- to 4.8-fold fewer switch errors than WhatsHap and HapCUT2 and runs six- to eightfold faster at 60$\times$; co-phasing lengthens haplotype blocks by 40\%. Against the earlier benchmark, the same outputs differ only 1.1- to 1.7-fold; at high coverage, most switch errors coincide across tools. On PacBio HiFi data, without retraining, \toolname makes 2.5- to 3.5-fold fewer switch errors than WhatsHap. It is a drop-in replacement in Clair3, ClairS, Severus and EPI2ME.
\end{abstract}
