\begin{abstract}
Long-read phasers rarely combine sequence variants with the methylation signal on the same reads, and seldom report which phase assignments are unreliable. Here we present \toolname, which phases SNVs, indels, structural variants and 5-methylcytosine in one graph, weights observations by base quality and sequence context, and scores every variant's phasing uncertainty. A graph neural network identifies phase-disagreement onsets for selective unphasing. Scored against a benchmark from the telomere-to-telomere HG002 assembly, \toolname makes 2.8- to 4.8-fold fewer switch errors than WhatsHap and HapCUT2 on nanopore data while running five- to sixfold faster on one thread at 60$\times$, and 2.2- to 2.6-fold fewer than WhatsHap on PacBio HiFi data without retraining. Co-phasing all four classes increases block N50 at 60$\times$ by 43\% relative to SNV-only phasing. Against the earlier, mapping-based benchmark, the tools differ only 1.2- to 1.3-fold at high coverage, and most of their switch errors coincide. \toolname keeps the command-line interface and output formats of \longphase, which Clair3, ClairS and EPI2ME call.
\end{abstract}

  \textbf{a}, SNVs, small indels, SVs and 5mC sites carried by the same long reads enter one phasing problem; alleles are coloured by read haplotype.
  \textbf{b}, Weighted phasing graph. Read-supported edges link allele nodes, and each observation is weighted by base quality and sequence context; the inset shows an indel down-weighted inside a tandem repeat.
  \textbf{c}, Each variant receives a phasing-uncertainty score (Methods), and a graph neural network (GATv2 and Transformer layers; Supplementary Fig.~6) classifies the variants around the most uncertain ones.
