\begin{abstract}
Long-read sequencing captures SNVs, indels, structural variants (SVs) and 5-methylcytosine (5mC) on the same molecules, yet phasers use these classes separately. Here we present \toolname, which phases all four in one graph and uses a graph neural network to withhold likely misphased variants. Scored against the telomere-to-telomere (T2T) HG002 assembly on nanopore and PacBio HiFi data at 10--60$\times$ coverage, \toolname had 1.7- to 5.0-fold lower SNV switch error rates than widely used read-based phasers and 1.5- to 5.4-fold lower rates than methylation-aware phasers at every coverage, and phased SVs with 1.3- to 2.3-fold lower error rates than WhatsHap. Co-phasing all four classes lengthened phase blocks by 18--46\% while switch error rates stayed at least 2.5-fold below those of WhatsHap. Switch errors shared by all phasers under the earlier GIAB benchmark largely disappeared under the T2T-derived benchmark, which distinguished the phasers more clearly, particularly in segmental duplications and other difficult regions. \toolname runs up to 8.9-fold faster on one thread and, as a drop-in replacement for \longphase, brings joint genetic and epigenetic phasing to established long-read workflows.
\end{abstract}
