\begin{abstract}
Long-read phasers rarely combine sequence variants with the methylation signal on the same reads, and seldom report which phase assignments are unreliable. Here we present \toolname, which phases SNVs, indels, structural variants and 5-methylcytosine in one graph, weights read observations by base quality and sequence context and uses a graph neural network to unphase likely misphased variants. Scored against a benchmark from the telomere-to-telomere HG002 assembly, \toolname achieves 2.9- to 5.0-fold lower switch error rates than WhatsHap and HapCUT2 on nanopore data while running five- to sixfold faster on one thread at 60$\times$, and achieves 1.7- to 3.5-fold lower rates than HiPhase, WhatsHap and HapCUT2 on PacBio HiFi data without retraining. Co-phasing all four classes increases block N50 at 60$\times$ by 44\% relative to SNV-only phasing. Against the earlier, mapping-based benchmark, switch error rates differ by 1.2- to 1.3-fold among tools at high coverage, and most of their switch errors coincide. As a drop-in replacement for \longphase, \toolname brings joint genetic and epigenetic phasing to established long-read workflows.
\end{abstract}
