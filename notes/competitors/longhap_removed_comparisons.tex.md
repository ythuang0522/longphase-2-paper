## sections/results.tex

```tex
We then asked whether phasing 5mC together with sequence variants is more accurate than adding methylation after phasing. MethPhaser\cite{fu2024methphaser} adds methylation after phasing: given the SNV phasing from which the joint SNV and 5mC phasing of \toolname started (Methods), it joins the blocks of that phasing without changing phase within them. On nanopore data, \toolname had the lowest switch error rate of the three phasers that use 5mC at every coverage, 1.5--1.8-fold lower than that of MethPhaser and 2.2--5.4-fold lower than that of LongHap\cite{pfennig2026longhap} (Fig.~\ref{fig:methylation}d and Supplementary Table~4). MethPhaser and LongHap produced longer blocks than \toolname at most coverages (Fig.~\ref{fig:methylation}e and Supplementary Table~4). At 10--60$\times$, MethPhaser had N50 1.1--1.8-fold that of \toolname (0.96--5.49 versus 0.87--3.12~Mb), at phased fractions within 0.2 percentage points. LongHap had N50 0.47--2.1-fold that of \toolname (0.41--5.40~Mb), with shorter blocks only at 10$\times$. MethPhaser and LongHap lengthen blocks by joining them with methylation evidence. Because one wrong join places a whole block on the opposite haplotype, Hamming distance measures this error better than the switch error rate, which counts it as a single switch (Supplementary Fig.~1e). In MethPhaser blocks, 4.0--7.2\% of SNVs were on the wrong haplotype, compared with 1.7--5.3\% for \toolname and 1.8--6.9\% in the input of MethPhaser before its joins (Supplementary Fig.~3); in LongHap blocks, 4.4--20\% were. The longer blocks of MethPhaser and LongHap thus contained more SNVs on the wrong haplotype.

With SNVs, indels, SVs and 5mC, \toolname had 2.5--3.2-fold lower SNV switch error rates than \whatshap in SNV-only mode and 3.0--3.8-fold lower rates than \whatshap with indels (Fig.~\ref{fig:methylation}f). Its blocks were 6--52\% longer than those of \whatshap in SNV-only mode and 6--21\% shorter than those of \whatshap with indels at 10--40$\times$ (within 1.4\% at 50--60$\times$; Supplementary Table~6). LongHap with the same four variant classes had 2.4--4.5-fold higher switch error rates than \toolname at 10--60$\times$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:methylation}f and Supplementary Table~4). At 10--60$\times$, its block N50 was 0.46--1.8-fold that of \toolname (0.47--6.83 versus 1.02--4.14~Mb), shorter only at 10$\times$, with Hamming distances of 6.4--21.9\%, compared with 3.6--7.1\% for \toolname.
```

## sections/methods.tex

```tex
\whatshap~v2.8\cite{patterson2015whatshap} and \hapcut~v1.3.4\cite{edge2017hapcut2} were run on the same alignments and variant calls; cross-tool SNV comparisons used SNV-only phasing, with indel phasing disabled in \whatshap.  \longphase~1.0\cite{lin2022longphase} and \toolname were run with the nanopore setting, and GNN correction used PE trigger 0.8, score threshold 0.35, no bridge protection and block splitting.  Co-phasing experiments separately evaluated the seven variant-class combinations listed in Supplementary Table~6, with and without correction.  Indel co-phasing was also compared with \whatshap's default indel phasing.  Margin v2.3.1\cite{shafin2021margin} and Ralphi\cite{battistella2025ralphi} were evaluated at 10--20$\times$, with ten down-sampling replicates per coverage; these tools were not evaluated at 30--60$\times$. Their configurations and that of MethPhaser\cite{fu2024methphaser} are listed in Supplementary Table~19.  MethPhaser was given the SNV-only output of \toolname without GNN correction instead of the \whatshap or \hapcut phasing its authors recommend as input. MethPhaser and the joint SNV and 5mC phasing of \toolname started from the same SNV phasing. Without GNN correction, differences between them reflect how methylation is used, after phasing to bridge existing blocks (MethPhaser) or during phasing as additional graph evidence (\toolname); with GNN correction, they also include its effect.  GCphase\cite{luo2024gcphase} was excluded from the genome-wide comparison because it ignored the FILTER field and phased homozygous-reference calls as heterozygous variants when given the full input VCF (about 1.2 million such calls in the 10$\times$ replicate-1 output). GCphase was not evaluated with input restricted to heterozygous SNVs.  The HiFi call sets were phased SNV-only with \toolname (\texttt{--pb}), with and without correction, with \whatshap, with \hapcut and with HiPhase (see below); the GNN weights were trained on nanopore data.  Three further phasers were run on replicate~1, at 10--60$\times$ in steps of 10$\times$ on nanopore data and at 10--50$\times$ on HiFi data, with default parameters or the workflow their documentation describes (Supplementary Table~19).  On nanopore data, LongHap\cite{pfennig2026longhap} phased SNVs with and without 5mC and, from the same reads, SNVs, indels and SVs with 5mC; because LongHap requires sequence-resolved alleles, Sniffles2 deletions and insertions were given to it with their allele sequences, and inversions, duplications and breakends were omitted.  TinkerHap\cite{hartmann2025tinkerhap} was run on both platforms and HiPhase\cite{holt2024hiphase} on HiFi data.  Both phase indels by default and have no SNV-only mode, so each was run on the full call sets and on the call sets restricted to SNVs, the variants that \whatshap phases with \texttt{--only-snvs}.  For the comparison with indels, \toolname, \whatshap and \hapcut were also run with indel phasing on HiFi data, and \hapcut on nanopore data.  These runs were scored in the same way as all others (Supplementary Table~4).
```

## sections/discussion.tex

```tex
\toolname combines genetic variation and allele-specific methylation in a shared phasing graph with selective withholding of phase assignments predicted to be unreliable. This approach extends methods that use methylation or SVs to connect blocks in regions with few heterozygous SNVs\cite{akbari2021nanomethphase,fu2024methphaser,akbari2023parentoforigin,lin2022longphase,holt2024hiphase}. Whereas LongHap adds methylation after sequence phasing\cite{pfennig2026longhap}, \toolname integrates both during haplotype construction. On the same reads, \toolname had 1.5--1.8-fold lower switch error rates than MethPhaser, which post-processes an SNV phasing, and 2.2--5.4-fold lower rates than LongHap (Fig.~\ref{fig:methylation}d and Supplementary Table~4); for MethPhaser, most of this difference came from GNN correction. SVs placed in the same graph were phased with 1.3--2.3-fold lower error rates than by \whatshap, and SVs of at least 1~kb with lower error rates than by HiPhase on HiFi data. The variant classes contributed differently: methylation improved contiguity at similar SNV switch error rates, whereas indels produced most of the gains in contiguity and increases in error. The same trade-off in \whatshap suggests that difficulties in indel representation or modelling may affect both methods. In joint SNV and indel phasing, \toolname with GNN correction had 8--32-fold lower switch error rates than \whatshap at variant pairs containing an indel. GNN correction reduced the additional error associated with indels (Supplementary Note~1 and Supplementary Fig.~7). More accurate indel calls could reduce the increase in phasing error associated with indel co-phasing\cite{zheng2026accelerated,kolesnikov2024local}.
```
